## Key Highlights

- **PHP2Shell, day two: the exploit window is open and the embargo post-mortem has begun.** Cloudflare's Matthew Prince counted tens of thousands of exploitation attempts in the first 45 minutes and "millions now"; Amazon says the pair of 9.2-severity bugs is "now being broadly exploited in the wild"; VulnCheck built the first exploit that defeats ASLR on a production-configured x86-64 server, warning, "Adversaries are clearly spending time (and tokens) on this bug." The UK took 32 government services offline, former NCSC chief Ciaran Martin called it "the most serious vulnerability discovered for five years," and the Hacker News poster who leaked the release ahead of schedule [apologised publicly](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50193416). Theo Browne's 29-minute breakdown argues the scarier story is the parallel Railway and OpenCode breaches, which he reads as a black market for stolen Anthropic and OpenAI API keys fed by distillation demand.
- **Anthropic's answer to the AI-finds-every-bug problem is to prove there are none.** With Theorem, Claude turned OSS-Fuzz harnesses into formal specs and proved crash-freedom for **239 entry points across 123 open-source libraries** at the RISC-V assembly level, generating about **180 million lines of Rocq** and finding bugs that "had eluded detection for years, despite trillions of test runs." It landed the same day Sam Altman told Stanford MBAs, "I no longer think we're going to be able to just patch all the bugs," and that the defense model has to switch to AI-powered shields.
- **Independent oversight got its first real stress test.** METR and Redwood Research spent three weeks inside OpenAI's codebase and found "novel vulnerabilities" in its misalignment monitoring, including an exploit that let agents hide "hundreds" of recent actions from the monitor. OpenAI says it patched them; the evaluators published only a high-level summary of a 61-page report. OpenAI also signed a safety research agreement with Poland's NASK, and the Guardian's editorial board argued the PHP episode makes global AI regulation unavoidable despite the White House.
- **Agent plumbing became a foundation.** Anthropic donated Agent Skills (1 million published, nearly 280 million installs) to the Agentic AI Foundation alongside OpenAI's Open Responses, Hugging Face's OpenEnv and Alibaba's OpenSandbox; the AAIF passed 300 members. Google launched Agent Compute Engine, the hardware-isolated microVM service that already runs Gemini's personal agents, and 1Password shipped a way for agents to log in without ever seeing a password. The same morning, Axios reported that JPMorgan, Bank of America and Morgan Stanley are restricting third-party agents after catching them acting inside customer accounts.
- **Money, chips and the supply ceiling.** OpenAI is closing a **$30 billion** round at a **$1.25 trillion** valuation led by UAE investors and Coatue; DeepSeek reportedly raised **$15 billion** two days after a US federal ban; Meta's negative free cash flow and a raised capex floor knocked the Nasdaq down 2.6%. Against that demand, Intel's CTO told Dwarkesh Patel that even with unlimited capex the installed tool base could maybe double leading-edge wafer output by 2030, not 10x it. Jensen Huang, meanwhile, spent Friday in Astana telling Kazakhstan to "transform the energy into intelligence and export intelligence."

## Analysis & Opinion

### [Websites across world hit by millions of attempts to exploit PHP code flaw](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/oct/22/websites-across-world-hit-by-millions-of-attempts-to-exploit-php-code-flaw) — The Guardian

Aisha Down and Robert Booth's status report on the second day of PHP2Shell. The UK's cybersecurity minister Liz Lloyd told parliament 32 government services were offline, "largely resulted from precautionary measures". The NCSC sent 50,000 alerts and reported no successful hacks, while former NCSC chief Ciaran Martin called it "the most serious vulnerability discovered for five years." Cloudflare's Matthew Prince described tens of thousands of attempts in the 45 minutes after disclosure, "hundreds of thousands within two hours, and millions now," plus thousands of probes in the ten hours *before* the public announcement. Calif's Ryan Austin, who found one of the bugs, struck the calmer note: "At the moment it doesn't look like exploitation has taken off or been widespread." Companion reporting from the Register's Carly Page lists the [32 unavailable and four partially unavailable UK services](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/22/php2shell-puts-dozens-of-uk-government-services-out-of-service/5304840), the Office for Budget Responsibility among them a week before the Budget, and notes that Germany, France, Belgium, Finland, the Netherlands and Australia's cyber agency pulled sites too. Lloyd confirmed, "The NCSC was briefed by OpenAI last week." Ars Technica carries the Financial Times' line that [Amazon saw exploitation four days before disclosure](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/hackers-got-a-four-day-head-start-on-epic-php2shell-vulnerability/) and the NSA's instruction to patch or disconnect.

### [PHP2Shell exploit bypasses protections designed to stop remote code execution](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/22/php2shell-exploit-bypasses-protections-designed-to-stop-remote-code-execution/5304883) — The Register

Jessica Lyons reports that VulnCheck built overnight what it believes is the first exploit for CVE-2026-109618 that achieves RCE on ASLR-enabled x86-64 systems running PHP's recommended production configuration (display_errors=off), chaining a confirmed memory leak into code execution on PHP 8.4.26. Director of exploit development Spencer McIntyre said ASLR had been "a major barrier to exploiting PHP2Shell on real-world servers," that VulnCheck had seen "hundreds" of proof-of-concept repos on GitHub of which "very few contain legitimate, working exploits," and, in his words, "Adversaries are clearly spending time (and tokens) on this bug." The exploit is restricted to the firm's paying intelligence customers. Lyons also covered a [maximum-severity GitLab bug](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/22/max-severity-gitlab-bug-allows-remote-code-execution-over-12-years-of-releases/5304854), CVE-2026-110264, an integer overflow in regex compilation that allows unauthenticated RCE on self-managed instances back to GitLab 7.0 from 2014. No exploitation seen yet, but, Lyons writes, "The ongoing PHP chaos may have given defenders a short head-start on this one."

### [Tell HN: I unknowingly broke the PHP RCE embargo. I am sorry.](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50193416) — Hacker News (601 points)

Joe Tidy's follow-up has Satya Nadella saying he hasn't seen anything like it "in a long, long time" and a UK sysadmin reporting: ["Sometimes it takes me longer to take a website down than to fix the PHP problem."](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cxnv025d8qpmo) For readers who have not followed the story, the BBC's Liv McMahon and Osmond Chia have the cleanest [what-is-PHP explainer](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c3qjnwye0ep5o), with Austin calling it "the most serious vulnerability I've ever found." ### [The Guardian view on AI safety: the push for global regulation must advance](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/oct/22/the-guardian-view-on-ai-safety-the-push-for-global-regulation-must-advance) — The Guardian (Editorial)

The paper's leader treats PHP2Shell as the case for international AI rules. ### [Third-party evaluators found vulnerabilities in OpenAI monitoring system during test pilot](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/22/metr-redwood-research-open-ai-test.html) — CNBC

Ashley Capoot reports the first published outcome of OpenAI's "employee-like access" pledge. From July 21 to August 11, one employee each from METR and Redwood Research worked on-site, chaperoned, on OpenAI-issued devices that "never left the premises," with access to the internal codebase, documentation and some staff. They stress-tested the internal misalignment monitoring system and found several "novel vulnerabilities," including, in CNBC's words, an exploit that would allow agents to prevent "hundreds" of recent actions from being monitored. OpenAI says it has patched those affecting production safeguards. The evaluators delivered a 61-page internal report but agreed to publish only a high-level post, because the pilot was not designed, as they put it, to "provide robust public transparency about OpenAI's monitoring system." Capoot situates this against Dario Amodei's September proposal, Anthropic's agreements with Accenture, METR and SecureBio, and the voluntary White House accord that encouraged companies to "partner with an independent external auditor or evaluator," while experts told CNBC the open questions are who funds these groups and to whom they report. OpenAI separately announced a [safety and security research partnership with Poland's NASK](https://openai.com/index/openai-nask-ai-safety-security-partnership), whose AI Safety Research Center serves as the Polish AI Safety Institute. The post notes CERT Polska used OpenAI's cyber models through Daybreak in July to find six MikroTik RouterOS vulnerabilities, "including flaws that could be combined to take control of affected devices without authentication."

## New Products & Tools

### [Donating Agent Skills to the Agentic AI Foundation](https://www.anthropic.com/news/donating-agent-skills-to-the-agentic-ai-foundation) — Anthropic

Anthropic is handing the Agent Skills standard (a folder with a SKILL.md plus scripts and references) to the Linux Foundation's AAIF, which it co-founded with Block and OpenAI and which already holds MCP. The numbers: more than 70 agent platforms including ChatGPT, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and VS Code have integrated it since the December 2025 standard. "1 million skills have been published and nearly 280 million installed." Anthropic donates the name, logo, GitHub org and agentskills.io domain. Engineers from Anthropic, Google and OpenAI will maintain it as individuals with David Soria Parra and Mahesh Murag as lead maintainers, and the proposed roadmap includes versioned releases, a formal proposal process and "security guidance for people who write and install skills." The Linux Foundation's release lists the [four new AAIF projects](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/agentic-ai-foundation-announces-four-new-projects-agent-skills-open-responses-openenv-and-opensandbox): Agent Skills (Anthropic), Open Responses (OpenAI, a provider-neutral request/response and streaming spec), OpenEnv (Hugging Face, containerised RL environments) and OpenSandbox (Alibaba, agent sandboxing), bringing the foundation to ten projects, and a second release says [membership passed 300](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/agentic-ai-foundation-surpasses-300-members-in-less-than-one-year) with Ant Group, the Gates Foundation, MongoDB, Nutanix, RBC, Vercel and Vultr as new Gold members. The Register's Thomas Claburn describes the beneficiaries as ["AI companies more interested in riding a tide that lifts all boats than making waves,"](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/23/vendor-affiliated-ai-agent-tech-gets-adopted-by-friendly-funded-foundation/5304912) with the caveat that blueprints help "at least until your AI agent unexpectedly ransacks some third-party organization."

### [Introducing Agent Compute Engine and expanded agent governance](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/ai-infrastructure/introducing-agent-compute-engine-and-expanded-agent-governance/) — Google Cloud

Announced at AGNTCon + MCPCon: a purpose-built compute service, in preview "in the coming months," that gives every agent "its own hardware-isolated microVM with container-speed and zero credentials held inside the sandbox." It already runs Gemini's agent for work tasks. Gautham Thambidorai says the microVMs deliver "the responsiveness, hardware isolation, and workload density needed to deliver interactive agent sandboxes to our users around the world." Google is also extending Gemini for business' governance controls to agents "wherever they operate." The accompanying [engineering deep dive](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/ai-infrastructure/agent-compute-engine-deep-dive/) by Ramanathan Venkatapathy and Rohit Jnagal frames it as the fourth first-principles rethink after Borg, TPUs and Titanium, with the design goal that every Gemini user gets "a personal agent that runs in the cloud, remembers their work, and acts for them while they are away." A third post launches [self-learning context enrichment in Knowledge Catalog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/data-analytics/knowledge-catalog-self-learning-context-enrichment-for-ai-agents/), which mines agent and user interaction traces for missing definitions and claims agents "nearly 4X more accurate, with up to 33% lower latency, and using up to 75% fewer tokens" on internal benchmarks. Note the convergence with NVIDIA's OpenShell, which [hit 424 points on Hacker News](https://github.com/NVIDIA/OpenShell) a day after Huang pitched it in Berlin: kernel-level policy enforcement on every file access, syscall and network connection, plus formal verification of policy changes before they apply.

### [LLMs are making formal software verification feasible at a much larger scale](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formal-verification) — Anthropic

Keane Lucas, Aengus Lynch and colleagues, with Theorem: Claude took the developer-written fuzz harnesses in Google's OSS-Fuzz, converted each into a specification (this entry point never crashes for any input under 64 MB) over the compiled RISC-V binary, patched the bugs it found, and then proved the spec for **239 entry points across 123 libraries**. The proofs total about 180 million lines of Rocq, roughly 600,000 lines per binary of median 18,000 instructions, and "virtually none of it needs to be manually reviewed" because the proof checker does that.
