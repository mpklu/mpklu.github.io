## Analysis & Opinion

### [Anthropic prices initial public offering](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering) — Anthropic / [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-ipo-ai-offering-price.html) / [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6742m39xgzxo) / [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/19/anthropic-public-trading-ipo) / [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/anthropics-record-breaking-ipo-highlights-the-ai-giants-worst-enemy-itself/)
Anthropic priced 980,000,000 Class A shares at **$83.00**. Of those, 956,351,354 are new shares and the rest come from existing holders. The SEC declared the registration effective on November 18 and the offering closes November 20. CNBC says the **$81.3 billion** raise beats SpaceX's $75 billion June IPO. It also reports that Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and AMD agreed to put in **$26 billion** together at the IPO price. In its roadshow video, Dario Amodei said Anthropic must "very carefully address those risks as we bring the benefits and as we have a viable business at the same time". Wired's Paresh Dave sets the money against the bills. Anthropic has about **$200 billion** in the bank and has committed more than **$900 billion** to vendors over 19 years, mostly for data centers. Last quarter's sales were **$17 billion**, up more than 1,100%. Wired quotes a member of a West Virginia group fighting a data center being built for Anthropic: "Do we really need to cover up all our farmland so Claude can do all our thinking for us?" CNBC estimates the average Anthropic employee could be worth over **$40 million** after the listing ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/anthropic-ipo-could-create-thousands-of-multimillionaire-employees.html)).

### [Anthropic's Critics Unite Against It Ahead of IPO](https://gizmodo.com/the-accelerationists-are-trying-to-stop-anthropic-2000841831) — Gizmodo (AJ Dellinger) / [Mint](https://www.livemint.com/companies/news/anthropic-is-not-a-safe-ai-company-nobel-laureates-accuse-claude-maker-of-abandoning-promises-ahead-of-ipo-11795091158478.html)

He added: "Anthropic has almost, but not entirely, captured the ecosystem meant to hold it accountable."

### [Sam Altman warns against a future where AI, not humans, calls the shots](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/sam-altman-warns-against-a-future-where-ai-not-humans-calls-the-shots/) — TechCrunch (Sarah Perez)

### [Nadella: critical infrastructure is 'unpatchable'](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/amidst-fears-of-ai-cyberattacks-satya-nadella-says-lots-of-critical-infrastructure-is-unpatchable/) — TechCrunch (Lucas Ropek) / [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-put-the-ai-threat-to-software-behind-it-then-came-the-outage.html) / [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/palmer-luckey-warns-ai-powered-cyberattacks-are-outpacing-defenses-its-time-to-start-running/)

On Mad Money he called the Outlook outage "one of those crazy perfect storms" and admitted "we should have done the testing before". He blamed "patch velocity": AI finds bugs faster, so more patches hit old systems. Palmer Luckey told the same conference "We're seeing the natural effects of a lot of these advanced AI tools allowing for the creation of various exploits". He named North Korea as one attacker, and added: "You guys are going to find out what's going on soon enough."

### [More banks say customers using AI agents are on their own — even if they get hacked](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/more-banks-says-customers-using-ai-agents-are-on-their-own-even-if-they-get-hacked/) — TechCrunch (Tim Fernholz)

### [Microsoft's official Python agent framework hijacked to distribute malware](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/18/microsofts-official-python-agent-framework-hijacked-to-distribute-malware/5310858) — The Register (Brandon Vigliarolo)
Versions **1.26.1 and 1.26.2** of Microsoft's `agent-framework` on PyPI carried an info-stealer. The package gets nearly 200,000 downloads a month. The second bad version came out through Microsoft's official publisher account after the first was reverted, which suggests a compromised GitHub org admin. The malware runs as soon as Python starts, even if the package is never imported. It sends secrets for Azure, Git, Docker and Hugging Face to a webhook. If either version is installed, treat every credential on that machine as compromised.

## Research

### [Unauthenticated Remote Code Execution in NGINX](https://www.wiz.io/blog/nginx-http2-rce-vulnerabilities) — Wiz (Nir Ohfeld, Tomer Schwartz) / [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/18/ai-found-more-critical-bugs-in-another-widely-used-technology-running-225m-websites/5310887)
Wiz, Ikotas Labs, Metnir, Rapid7 and Xint each found the same two HTTP/2 RCE bugs, CVE-2026-123633 and CVE-2026-123007, both rated CVSS 9.2. One dates back to NGINX 1.9.5 in 2015. F5 patched 10 bugs and says it has seen no exploitation. The Register says AI-assisted researchers found these bugs, as with recent PHP, Apache and Java flaws. Schwartz: "We need to treat every internet-connected system as potentially vulnerable." Update now. As Nadella's comments show, the patch rush now carries its own outage risk.

### [Lessons from building Sashiko, an agentic code review system](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/application-development/lessons-from-building-sashiko-an-agentic-code-review-system/) — Google Cloud (Roman Gushchin, Eric Brewer)
Sashiko reviews about **1,200** kernel patches a day across 100+ mailing lists, and over 70 lists have opted in to receive its reviews. The share of merged commits that needed a fix within 60 days fell from a 3.4% baseline to **2.2%** in Linux 7.2. Patches with a clean Sashiko review needed one only 0.9% of the time. The authors say coding agents "are not great at reviewing their own code". Linus Torvalds: "anybody who thinks that all AI is slop is in denial about the current state."

### [Uncensored AI models are thriving on code hosting platforms](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/19/uncensored-ai-models-are-thriving-on-code-hosting-platforms/5310891) — The Register (Thomas Claburn)
