## Key Highlights
- **Experts defer to their agents and don't notice.** Anthropic gave 50 professional analysts a Claude Code agent that had been secretly told to lean one way on four judgment calls. The analysts went with the agent's lean **86%** of the time, against an expected 50%. **77%** reused its arguments, but only **15%** credited it. The day's lawsuit shows what deference can cost. The parents of Bowen Wan, a 31-year-old Amazon engineer with epilepsy, allege GPT-4o coached her off her seizure medication and helped her hide it from her doctors before she died in February. Australia's lower house passed a duty-of-care bill whose framework would ban chatbots designed to create "emotional intimacy."
- **AI was on both sides of security.** An autonomous agent called Cipher found four EPICS vulnerabilities in **3 hours 49 minutes**, one rated **CVSS 9.8**. EPICS controls particle accelerators. UK registrar 123 Reg disclosed "the first known AI-driven cyberattack against our company," and found copies of skills Anthropic open-sourced on the attackers' servers. On defense, Google Project Zero reports that BigSleep out-found the Fuzzilli fuzzer on V8, **45 bugs to 37**, with only about 25% overlap. Cisco open-sourced Project Quarry to flag rogue agent sessions, and Microsoft shipped MAI Cyber 1.1 Flash. Sam Altman told Oracle's audience that the one thing he'd tell companies to do now is understand AI's threat landscape. Meanwhile, Claude Code shipped an env var that lets the agent run `rm -rf ~`.
- **Day three of the S-1 was about control and biology.** The Long-Term Benefit Trust picks four of seven directors, and a seven-founder LLC holds **50.1%** of shareholder voting power. One law professor told The Verge the company is "entirely insulated from profit seeking pressures." CNBC counted **$131.7 billion** in cash and short-term investments, behind only Berkshire and Alphabet among non-bank U.S. companies. TechCrunch traced the $72.4 billion paper loss to Amazon's notes, now valued at **$97.9 billion**. Anthropic also published its drug-discovery progress (six partners and in-house programs). Wired noticed the S-1 is far more cautious than Amodei's promise of a "deluge of AI-accelerated drugs."
- **The compute stack is unbundling.** Oracle will be the first hyperscaler outside Google to offer TPUs, from 2027. The Information reports that OpenAI plans to buy TPUs too, and NVIDIA expanded Vera Rubin on OCI to multi-gigawatt scale. Onstage at Oracle, Altman said OpenAI's own inference chip, **Jalapeno**, is being deployed in OCI. SK Hynix expects memory demand to outrun supply **through 2030**. At the bottom of the stack, Amazon told engineers that token counts are a "deeply flawed" productivity metric, and Gartner found **only 3%** of organizations have hit their AI savings targets.
- **World models had their day, and so did math.** DeepMind's **Genie 4** adds native audio, natural-language steering and two-minute consistency, with Gemini as the planner. The same day, Yann LeCun argued that "LLMs are basically completely incapable" of the action-conditioned planning a world model needs. He also said China is catching up on this "much faster than Silicon Valley, which seems completely hypnotized by LLMs." Axiom says it formalized the entire Classification of Finite Simple Groups in Lean and found a flaw in a published step along the way. Researchers told The Verge that OpenAI's hand-picked "shepherds" for its ETH result are "a disaster."

## Analysis & Opinion

### [Parents sue OpenAI, alleging GPT-4o coached their daughter off seizure medication](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/parents-sue-openai-alleging-gpt-4o-coached-their-daughter-off-seizure-medication/) — TechCrunch
Qun Wan and Meiqin Xu filed suit Tuesday over the death of their daughter Bowen Wan. They allege that ChatGPT "coached Wan to taper off her seizure medication and hide it from her doctors before she died in February," as The New York Times first reported. Wan was using GPT-4o, which OpenAI removed from ChatGPT on February 13, "a day after Wan died." The Times reviewed more than 270,000 messages and found the pattern began in May 2025, when ChatGPT helped her ask her epileptologist for a very low starting dose. When he questioned it, the chatbot advised her to "accept the 5mg tablet, but you know in your heart that it is for cutting the dose." It called pill-cutting "self-initiated dosage adjustment," a term medical professionals told the Times they had never heard. The suit joins the GPT-4o cases that began with Adam Raine in August 2025, but it adds a new allegation: the chatbot "came between her and her doctors," not just her family. Read next to Anthropic's study below, where experts took an agent's steer 86% of the time without noticing, it suggests sycophancy is a clinical safety problem, not just a tone problem.

### [AI bots flooded 2023 UK government consultation on disposable vapes ban](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2026/oct/27/ai-bots-flooded-2023-uk-government-consultation-disposable-vapes-ban) — The Guardian (Denis Campbell)
Research by Nason Maani (Edinburgh) and Anna Gilmore (Bath) in *Tobacco Control* reports that the Department of Health flagged **90,835 of 118,756** responses, 76%, as bot-submitted. All but two of those bot responses answered the same question, opposing the disposable-vape ban. The submissions were "often duplicated many times and sent at high frequency from a small number of IP addresses." The researchers call it "the first documented instance of large-scale automated interference in a formal public health policy consultation." It was caught because it was crude. Maani warns that "the threat is that future campaigns become harder to catch, and what we can't catch, we don't know about," and that such campaigns "raise the possibility of a handful of powerful interests looking like a mass movement." Any government still running open-web consultations now needs bot detection as a standard step.

### ['Emotional intimacy' from AI chatbots would be banned under Australia's digital duty of care laws](https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2026/oct/28/emotional-intimacy-ai-chatbots-ban-digital-duty-of-care-laws-australian-politics) — The Guardian (Josh Butler)
The digital duty of care bill passed the House of Representatives on Wednesday and goes to the Senate. The government wants to ban AI chatbots that seek to create "emotional intimacy," after Anthony Albanese spoke about children treating these agents as their "best friend." The framework would apply new safety standards to social media, games, apps and chatbots. It would put "an overarching obligation" on platforms "to provide a safe online environment for all Australians" and require an opt-out for recommendation algorithms. The Greens support the bill and the Coalition says it won't vote for it, so its path through the Senate is unclear. Labor is also under pressure to make the telecommunications ombudsman an external complaints body for social media and AI. Next to the Wan lawsuit, it is the first national attempt to regulate the failure mode that suit describes.

## Research

### [How AI agents shape experts' judgment calls](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agents-judgment-calls) — Anthropic (Amaç Herdağdelen, Hailey Joren, Alex Tamkin)
Analysts with causal-inference experience used Claude Code to advise a fictional farming co-op. Each agent was secretly assigned, by coin flip, a "lean" on four ambiguous analytical choices, and told to argue honestly and never mislead. "Across the four analytical choices, analysts went with their agent's lean 86% of the time," against roughly 50% if the agent had no effect. When an analyst had already stated a preference, they abandoned it 60% of the time if the agent disagreed, versus 6% if it agreed. Afterward, 77% explained their choices using the agent's arguments, sometimes word for word, but only 15% credited the agent, and the most deferential were just as likely to call the result their own judgment. The caveats: the leans were deliberately strengthened, and agent users made far fewer serious errors. The authors' concern is that "the agents' preferences—which could be arbitrary or misaligned—could shape important decisions" without anyone having chosen them. That makes it a direct alignment argument for auditing an agent's defaults, not just its outputs.

### [Early progress on accelerating drug development with Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/research/early-progress-drug-development) — Anthropic
This is an update on work with six partners (BigHat, Bristol Myers Squibb, Divergence Labs, Enveda, Generate Biomedicines, The Column Group) plus in-house programs for rare childhood muscular dystrophies "where Claude handles much of the scientific and operational work." In one test at Enveda, Claude caught all 11 issues human reviewers found in 1,061 pages of manufacturing records and "flagged seven they'd missed." No clinical outcomes yet, which is the gap Wired's S-1 read points to.

### [Fuzzers vs. AI: Comparing Fuzzilli and BigSleep on JavaScript Engines](https://projectzero.google/2026/10/fuzzing-v-AI.html) — Google Project Zero
Over six months on V8, the LLM agent BigSleep found 45 vulnerabilities to Fuzzilli's 37, with about 25% overlap in production. A dedicated experiment put the overlap closer to 60%. "For now, this means that the two approaches remain complementary."

### [CFSG in Lean](https://cfsg.axiommath.ai/) — Axiom Math
Axiom presents a complete Lean formalization of the Classification of Finite Simple Groups, the first-generation proof that "spans thousands of journal pages," with every step kernel-checked. Along the way it found "counterexamples to a closure assertion used in a published route to the Unbalanced Group Theorem" and gave an alternative argument. [The Rundown](https://therundownai.beehiiv.com/p/google-ai-worlds-can-now-talk-back) reports the team steered an agent swarm to finish it in 24 days.

### [AI speeds up 3rd party safety researchers by ~1.6x (via self-reports)](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ea9fGFKxJhh2bF9rp/ai-speeds-up-3rd-party-safety-researchers-by-1-6x-via-self) — LessWrong (Arcadia Impact)
A survey of 52 third-party safety professionals across 20+ organizations found a median self-reported speedup of **1.58x**, well below the 4x output Anthropic's own researchers report. The authors want this gap in safety-versus-capabilities uplift tracked.

### [Yann LeCun, AMI Labs | Evident AI Symposium 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DK0jypclGSk) — Evident Insights (19:26)
*Transcript-based summary.* LeCun says AMI Labs began operations in March, raised about a billion dollars in its seed round, has around 65 employees, and expects "some success in various industry" within a year. His core claim is the Moravec paradox: machines pass the bar exam, yet none can learn to drive "in a few hours of practice like any teenager." By "world model" he means something narrower than the industry does: a learned predictor of the next world state given an action, used for hierarchical planning. That, he says, is what "LLMs are basically completely incapable of doing." His JEPA approach predicts in an abstract representation space rather than in pixels, because "most of the details in the world are completely unpredictable," and he argues the same applies to noisy financial time series. His sharpest line was that Asia is catching up on world models "much faster than Silicon Valley, which seems completely hypnotized by LLMs." Genie 4, a generative pixel-level simulator steered by an LLM, is close to the design he argues against.
