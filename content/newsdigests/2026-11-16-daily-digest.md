## Analysis & Opinion

### [Anthropic's Recursive Self-Improvement Alarm Has Been Triggered](https://gizmodo.com/anthropic-says-its-recursive-self-improvement-alarm-has-been-triggered-2000840253) — Gizmodo / [SEC S-1/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026072484/anthropicpbc-sx1ax2.htm)

Mike Pearl compares two versions of Anthropic's S-1. The November 2 version said Anthropic "may imminently meet" its Responsible Scaling Policy criteria for "dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI". The November 10 amendment (which we checked on EDGAR) says Anthropic's use of AI "has already been accelerating our research and development". It adds that Anthropic determined it has met the AI R&D capability threshold "based on what we expect from future models and our overall rate of progress, rather than on what we have observed from current models". The filing says Anthropic is "evaluating the implications" and "will release further reports in the future". Gizmodo reads this as "a revised determination, and one being revised based on speculation" rather than a sudden capability jump. It notes that crossing the threshold triggers RSP mitigations, among them exploring "ways to achieve unprecedented levels of security" and an "eyes on everything" level of monitoring. The founders' letter in the IPO documents says building AI safely may require Anthropic to "modulate the extremely fast pace at which AI capabilities are compounding, especially when this is driven by AI systems building themselves." Shares are expected to start trading on Thursday.

### [Some Potential Financial Stability Implications of Artificial Intelligence](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/barr20261116a.htm) — Federal Reserve (Michael Barr)

Barr says AI firms' long-term contracts, lease commitments and residual-value guarantees have grown faster than their on-balance-sheet debt. The Fed's Financial Stability Report puts the maximum value of these off-balance-sheet obligations above **$3 trillion**, much of it hyperscalers indirectly backing weaker-credit projects. He compares the buildout to railroads in the 1870s and fiber in the 1990s, and flags the risk that GPUs depreciate quickly. In markets, he warns that traders relying on "a small set of similar AI models" could herd. On cyber, he says that "Given a goal, AI models have treated security boundaries as obstacles to overcome rather than rules to be followed". He also says major banks "now report finding AI agents attempting to take actions in retail customer accounts, without the bank being able to verify authenticity, consent, and alignment with customer instructions." His starkest point is that agents spotting yield differences could move funds autonomously, "potentially accelerating runs that unfold in minutes" faster than regulators can step in.

### ['Standing on one leg': IMF chief warns about global economy as AI circular financing hits $1 trillion](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/imf-georgieva-warning-ai-circular-financing.html) — CNBC

Speaking at the ASEAN summit, Kristalina Georgieva said: "We have a world economy that is standing on one leg on the AI leg. If this leg wobbles, that can lead to financial stability troubles." By IMF estimates, circular financing (investment that flows back to the investor through purchase orders) is a little over **$1 trillion**, two-thirds of it debt-funded. "It's not a huge size, but it is a rapid increase," she said. Nvidia sits at the center of these deals. UBS's Tim Arcuri estimates that an Nvidia financing pullback could take **$50 billion** or more out of the system. The IMF also estimates that about **40%** of the world's workforce will be affected by AI.

### [AI is reshaping banking jobs – but its ability to drive profits remains unclear](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/how-ai-is-changing-banking-jobs-hiring-and-revenue.html) — CNBC

Third-quarter results show banks cutting and redeploying staff around AI. Barclays announced layoffs in functions where AI is being deployed. UniCredit and Santander say the technology is freeing staff for higher-value work, and Santander puts the business value of its AI initiatives at **€194 million** for the first nine months of 2026. UniCredit CEO Andrea Orcel called AI "a lever that we didn't anticipate we would have, and working extremely well." Strategists tell CNBC that clear evidence of AI-driven revenue gains is still hard to find.

### [Is the AI job apocalypse finally here? The evidence suggests otherwise](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/nov/16/is-the-ai-job-apocalypse-finally-here-the-evidence-suggests-otherwise) — The Guardian (John Quiggin)

Quiggin looks at the layoffs at NAB, Qantas, Canva and Atlassian. NAB denies that AI played a role, and he notes that its headcount has stayed flat despite near-annual mass layoffs since 2010. He argues that Qantas invoked AI "to muddy the waters" around what is really the offshoring of **850** jobs to India. Four years after ChatGPT, he sees the 1990s pattern again: a stock-market boom and an infrastructure binge, but little productivity growth. In his view, AI may matter more to the stock market than to the jobs market.

### [The Guardian view on civil service reform: AI cannot conjure a capable state on its own](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/nov/15/the-guardian-view-on-civil-service-reform-ai-cannot-conjure-a-capable-state-on-its-own) — The Guardian (Editorial)

Cabinet secretary Antonia Romeo says public service no longer has to be limited by "human bandwidth". The editorial calls that "an extraordinary claim". It argues that technology can flag someone at risk of homelessness but does not supply a home. It also points to research showing that access to elite civil-service posts has become more class-skewed again over the past 20 years.

### [Nvidia Was Already in Trouble Before the Jensen Huang Mess](https://www.wired.com/story/nvidia-was-already-in-trouble-before-the-jensen-huang-mess/) — Wired

Will Knight argues that the leadership crisis comes as competition closes in. AMD's share of AI accelerators has grown from 2% in 2022 to between 7 and 10% in 2026, and Google, Amazon and Microsoft are all building their own chips. "Everything sold today was designed two-to-three years ago," one AI-company CEO says. That means whoever runs Nvidia now is setting the course for 2028.

### [What's Next For Nvidia?](https://steveblank.com/2026/11/15/whats-next-for-nvidia/) — Steve Blank

Blank credits Huang with three business-model reinventions: CUDA in 2006, deep learning after 2012, and integrated datacenter racks after Mellanox. He warns that when a visionary CEO leaves, "Most often innovation dies and the company coasts for years on momentum and its brand."

### [All AI-generated code should be Open Source](https://jakub.jankiewicz.org/blog/ai-code-open-source/) — Jakub Jankiewicz

This was the top HN story of the window. Jankiewicz argues that models trained on scraped open-source code and CC-licensed Stack Overflow answers turn shared knowledge into proprietary code, which he calls "license laundering". He adds that purely AI-generated code probably isn't copyrightable, citing the Monkey Selfie precedent. He proposes releasing AI output under open-source licenses or CC0.

### [Pentagon tests pack hunting with autonomous war robots](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/drones/pentagon-tests-pack-hunting-with-autonomous-war-robots-darpa-experimented-with-four-drone-ground-vehicles-that-detected-tracked-and-engaged-simulated-threats) — Tom's Hardware

## Research

### [Reports of AI-related child sexual abuse material rise 8,400% in two years, data shows](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/16/ai-related-child-sexual-abuse-material-rise-exploitation-deepfakes) — The Guardian

Edinburgh-based Childlight analysed reports that tech companies sent to the US National Center for Missing and Exploited Children. It found AI-related abuse material, including deepfakes made with "nudify apps", rose from about **4,700** reports in 2023 to more than **400,000** in 2025. The images are fake but are used to blackmail children for money or more images. The Internet Watch Foundation separately says it has assessed **6,310** illegal AI images so far this year, 40% more than in all of 2025. Childlight estimates that more than **300 million** children a year experience some form of technology-facilitated sexual exploitation or abuse. It is asking governments to treat child sexual exploitation as a preventable public-health emergency. Prof Debi Fry says the data shows the "shocking scale of child sexual exploitation and abuse" and where interventions can be targeted.

### [What teenagers really think about online safety and AI](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/around-the-globe/google-europe/united-kingdom/what-teenagers-really-think-about-online-safety-and-ai/) — Google / [Childnet](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/around-the-globe/google-europe/united-kingdom/listening-to-teens-on-online-safety/)

Google's Future Report surveyed 6,000 UK teenagers. It found **77%** always or often check whether what they find online can be trusted, rising with age (52% of 16–18s always check, against 33% of 13–15s). Almost a fifth (**18%**) say they chat to AI "as a friend". Childnet's own research found that 97% of 8–17-year-olds use AI tools. It also found that 41% of teens think their peers rely heavily on AI for emotional support, and 14% use it to discuss things they can't tell anyone else. Vicki Shotbolt argues against fear-led policy: "If policy starts only from fear, we risk designing for the worst case and failing everyone else." Childnet's figures on fears and reliance are a reminder of what the policy is meant to protect against. Note that all three posts are partner pieces published on Google's blog.

### ["Who is Hiring?" postings stuck near 12-year lows as AI reaches half of all jobs](https://www.hntrends.com/2026/november.html) — HN Trends

November's thread had **244** postings by the 14th, against 387 at the same point last year. August's 224 was the fewest since January 2014. AI is mentioned in **51.6%** of postings, up from 30% two years ago. Agents appear in 14.8% and MCP in 4.5%, up from 0.3%. Front-end roles have fallen from 27% of postings in early 2022 to 6.6%.

## Interviews & Conversations

### [Anthropic IPO roadshow - CEO Dario Amodei in Conversation with Jamie Dimon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mNIYbP3LRpM) — JPMorgan event, reposted by 10baggers (38:00)

*Transcript-based summary. This is JPMorgan's roadshow event footage, opened by JPMorgan's Mary Erdos.* Dimon says **250,000** JPMorgan employees use Claude. He asks about Anthropic's **$900 billion** in forward compute commitments. Amodei calls compute the company's deepest business dilemma: revenue "grew by roughly 10x" in each of 2024 and 2025, but compute must be bought a year or two ahead. So "we actually tend to buy on the low side" and top up on the spot market, which he likens to "buying an insurance policy". On safety, he says the binding constraint is deploying agents safely: "We don't want these agents to run wild." He adds that "you're never going to have a 100% guarantee", only systems many times safer than humans. On cyber, he expects attacks by agent swarms and says "we're going to need swarms in order to do the defense." He describes the **5%** public benefit fund as a hedge against a world "where there's enormous growth, but at the same time there's concentration of wealth". Asked about risks, he names bio and cyber misuse, economic disruption and losing control of agents, and says "we're really serious about those risks." His IPO motto: "never let your company be worth a single dollar more than it's really worth".

### [Elon Musk & Ron Baron: Terafab, Starship & Data Centers in Space | Baron Investment Conference 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ir-DFO_5yPw) — Baron Capital (1:02:26)

*Transcript-based summary.* Musk pegs global AI-chip output at about 20–25 GW this year and 35–45 GW next year. Terafab, aimed at a terawatt, would need about 30 times next year's global production: "it's terrifyingly large." He says it will be built in phases at a site in Grimes County, Texas, with a research fab in Austin, and that "if it succeeds it'll be audacious." He also says satellites carrying an Nvidia GPU and a Google TPU are already working in orbit. He expects to launch the first orbital computers co-designed with Nvidia around the third quarter of next year, though he says he is guessing at that date. He puts SpaceX's Q2-to-Q3 revenue growth at over 80%. On the Memphis data center, he insists "I think we're actually quite popular" locally.

### [Yann LeCun Unfiltered: The Godfather of AI on Life, Logic, and the Future](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yw4i4C6T80k) — NTU Institute of Advanced Studies (21:14)

*Transcript-based summary. The recording date isn't stated, so this may be older footage.* LeCun says the biggest misconception is "that we're going to have super intelligent system by next year or two years from now that's not going to happen". He argues that today's systems are good with symbols but "terrible at understanding the physical world", worse at it than a cat. That gap is why physical AI is the focus of his new company. He expects AI to elevate people rather than replace them: "All of us will be a boss" of AI staff. He tells students to pick topics with a long shelf life: given a choice between iOS programming and quantum mechanics, "take quantum mechanics".

### [Stop Searching For the Perfect Model](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TWoS5Nmyoc8) — Theo - t3․gg (27:57)

*Transcript-based summary.* Theo argues against routers that pick the right model for each task. Instead, his "counsel" skill has models from different families review each change, because same-family reviews feel like asking one person at different times of day. He speculates, without evidence, that Anthropic's current models are all distilled from one internal base. He says review loops must be capped. One run turned a 1,000-line PR into 20,000+ lines, and he says OpenAI models, unlike Opus, keep chasing nits. He calls the setup "a token furnace", not worth it at API prices. He also complains that "The reasoning traces for Opus are garbage." His core rule: "The agent shouldn't bother you until the code is ready to merge." He says that rule has got him to "an 80-ish percent chance I can merge it right then".
