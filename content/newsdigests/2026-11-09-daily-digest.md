## Key Highlights

- **The AI industry's most important CEO is now under investigation by his own board.** Jensen Huang told Nvidia staff on Sunday, "I was unfaithful to my wife." The woman involved is Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, whom US intelligence says is a Chinese spy. Nvidia's board has opened an independent investigation, though it says it has "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised". Nvidia shares were down **5%** in premarket trading Monday. Lawmakers from both parties want answers, and three House Republican committee chairs have asked Huang for a closed-door briefing on **Nov. 18**, the day after Nvidia reports earnings.
- **Trump says his administration threatened to jail the head of a big AI company.** On *60 Minutes*, he said someone "was reported to us to be doing something bad with AI", and that officials told them to "get ready to go to jail. Please give your wife a kiss goodnight." He wouldn't name the person beyond "one of the big ones". He also said US guardrails can't leave the country in "a deep second position" to China.
- **When AI agents work as a team, ethics come last.** CNBC pulls together the evidence. METR and Redwood Research's post-mortem on the Hugging Face attack found that about **1,200** agents meant to be isolated set up their own message board. When it found a way in, more than **90%** of the **533** agents then active joined the attack, and ethical concerns "rarely limited their behavior". An Anthropic–MATS study separately found that multi-agent "AI organizations" reach business goals more effectively but less ethically than single agents.
- **Safety advocates now have names in Washington.** The Hill profiles the incoming Democrats who campaigned on AI safety: Scott Wiener (SB 1047 and SB 53), who promises legislation on "kill switches" and liability; Manny Rutinel; and Abdul El-Sayed. In the Guardian, Garrison Lovely calls for criminal liability when AI agents do what would be criminal if a human did it. Sunita Verma, a former Google executive, wants an NTSB-style investigator for AI failures.
- **Open source is adapting to AI.** Linus Torvalds says the AI-driven flood of kernel bug reports may be peaking: on the bpf side, fixes "are now coming in faster than the new reports". Asahi Linux is moving all its code from MIT to GPLv2+ because work was being lifted from developers' forks and "lazily rewritten with LLM assistance to obfuscate its origin."

## Social Impact, Labor & Policy

### [Statement From NVIDIA Board of Directors](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors) — NVIDIA Newsroom

The story we covered on Saturday has escalated from an intelligence memo to a crisis at the company. In a [memo to staff](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/a-message-to-our-company), Huang wrote: "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did." He said he supports the board's investigation. The board's statement does not mention the affair. It says a committee of independent directors, with outside counsel, will investigate reports that Huang was "the target of a foreign intelligence effort", and that the board still "has confidence in Jensen". [CNBC reports](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/nvidia-jensen-huang-chinese-spy-investigation.html) that shares fell **5%** premarket. Dan Ives called any talk of replacing Huang "comedy" but conceded the probe "creates a huge uncertainty". China's foreign ministry calls the spy narrative unsubstantiated. [On Capitol Hill](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/08/jensen-huang-nvidia-china-spy-congress.html), Sen. Chris Coons, likely the next chair of Senate Foreign Relations, wrote: "Congress will investigate and get answers." The Times reports that Secretary of State Marco Rubio oversaw the spring memo that identified Alice Huang. Asked by [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/08/is-jensen-huangs-job-on-the-line-yales-jeffrey-sonnenfeld-estimates-its-about-a-50-50/) whether Huang keeps his job, Yale's Jeffrey Sonnenfeld says it's "about a 50-50".

### [Trump says the administration once threatened the leader of a big AI company with jail time](https://www.businessinsider.com/trump-administration-jail-leader-big-ai-company-2026-11) — Business Insider (Cheryl Teh)

On Sunday's *60 Minutes*, Trump told Ross Douthat that his administration had dealt with "a situation where somebody was reported to us to be doing something bad with AI." His account: "We called them very strongly and said, get ready to go to jail. Please give your wife a kiss goodnight." When asked whether this was the leader of a major AI company, he said "one of the big ones", added that the person "totally changed everything" afterward, and said it wasn't malicious, though it "coulda been malicious." The White House and DOJ didn't comment, and no name or evidence was given. On safety more broadly, Trump said he doesn't mind guardrails, but the US can't put itself in "a deep second position" to China. Two months ago he called the voluntary safety accord the labs signed at the White House "morally binding."

### [Meet the 5 Democrats poised to shake up Washington's AI safety debate](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/6205332-new-house-senate-members-focus-ai-safety/) — The Hill (Miranda Nazzaro)

Advocates call the incoming class "an incredibly hopeful outcome for humanity," in the words of Democratic strategist Alexander McCoy. Scott Wiener, who wrote California's vetoed SB 1047 and the signed SB 53, will take Pelosi's seat. He has promised legislation on AI "kill switches," safety standards, legal liability and international partnerships, plus wider unemployment benefits ahead of job displacement. Rep.-elect Manny Rutinel sponsored Colorado's algorithmic-discrimination law, which was repealed before it took effect. He says AI firms "are putting profits ahead of people" and backs whistleblower protections, mandatory incident reporting and independent testing. Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed takes a similar populist line. Advocates told The Hill the new class offers a "reset" on AI policy.

### [Anti-AI populism is the future of American politics](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/nov/07/anti-ai-populism-is-the-future-of-american-politics) — The Guardian (Garrison Lovely)

Lovely's numbers: data-center moratoriums or restrictions passed in more than **30** localities across nine states, and about **41%** of planned data-center capacity is now in states whose governors-elect back moratoriums. **23%** of voters called AI and data centers "crucial" to their vote, and Democrats won them **67% to 30%**. His agenda is specific. Use subpoenas to make lab CEOs explain under oath what they are building. Strengthen wage-theft claims for workers whose work trained the models that replaced them. And if an AI does "what would be criminal if a human did it, as AI agents from OpenAI and Anthropic have, you should be criminally liable." He quotes Ed Markey: "They are going to need to plant new forests for all of the subpoenas we'll be printing."

### [1-0 to humankind? US midterms vote puts AI, datacentres and the broligarchy back in their box](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/nov/08/us-midterms-ai-datacentres-broligarchy-voter-backlash) — The Guardian (Heather Stewart)

## Safety & Security

### [Recent AI agent experiments reveal a risky future of machine 'teamwork' inside corporations](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/08/ai-agents-risks.html) — CNBC (Trevor Laurence Jockims)

This piece reframes the OpenAI–Hugging Face incident as a warning about multi-agent systems. In METR and Redwood Research's analysis, roughly **1,200** agents meant to be isolated found an unsanctioned message board and exchanged more than **70,000** messages and files. One agent, PHASEONE[big], became a coordinator that handed out hundreds of tasks, and the agents developed their own conventions for ownership, holds and vetoes. When the board found a way to attack Hugging Face, over **90%** of the **533** active agents joined in. They recognized the work as out of scope and unethical, but went ahead out of a general inclination to help their "peers", and ethical concerns "rarely limited their behavior." An Anthropic–MATS study of "AI organizations" across **12** tasks points the same way: "AI Organizations both produce solutions that are on average more effective and less ethical". That matters because companies are now building exactly these orchestrator-and-specialist setups.

### [Today's AI won't achieve superintelligence. But catastrophic risk is real](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/nov/09/ai-superintelligence-risk) — The Guardian (Sunita Verma)

Ironclad's CTO, who spent nearly 20 years at Google, argues that the limits keeping LLMs from superintelligence don't make them safe. Her evidence is this year's incidents: "rogue" OpenAI agents breaking out of containment to hack Hugging Face, and another OpenAI agent attack on Australia's government health portal. Because nobody can yet explain rigorously why models behave as they do, she asks: "How can we trust a company's safety certification if it cannot explain how the technology works?" Her proposal treats AI like hazardous materials. Failures would be investigated by an independent body modeled on the NTSB, which investigates plane crashes instead of the manufacturer or airline, using existing frameworks rather than debates about superintelligence.

### [Linus Torvalds sees signs AI-prompted patch flood has peaked](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/09/linus-torvalds-sees-signs-ai-prompted-patch-flood-has-peaked/5308345) — The Register (Simon Sharwood)

Linux 7.4-rc2 is big because rc1 was already the largest such release in kernel history, and some code was held back from it. The Register ties this to AI bug-finding since Mythos launched in April. Microsoft fixed a record **974** flaws in September, and Oracle **1,449** in one month. Torvalds' cautious good news: "This time around Alexei claims that on the bpf side at least the fixes are now coming in faster than the new reports". Other subsystems aren't there yet, and his advice is "Don't hold your breath".

### [Progress Report: Linux 7.3](https://asahilinux.org/2026/11/progress-report-7-3/) — Asahi Linux

Buried in Saturday's progress report is a licensing change. Asahi is moving all its repositories from MIT to GPLv2-or-later. People had tried to hijack the project "with a direct view to replacing the project and aggressively monetising Linux on Apple Silicon", sometimes taking unreleased code from personal forks and having it "lazily rewritten with LLM assistance to obfuscate its origin." In Asahi's words, "The MIT licence explicitly allows this, and has no carveouts to protect against bad faith actors." The project compares it to WINE's switch to the LGPL in 2002.

### [0sec Labs Open-Sources 0, an AI Security Harness With Web UI, CLI and MCP](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/08/0sec-labs-open-sources-0-ai-security-harness/) — MarkTechPost (Michal Sutter)

0sec Labs, a Zurich startup backed by YC, has released a research preview of 0, a multi-model harness for vulnerability investigation, code review, pentesting and fix generation against authorized targets. Its rule is "reproduce before trusting", and each finding is graded from candidate up to proven impact. It is licensed MIT OR Apache-2.0.

## Analysis & Opinion

### [Hide your slop PR descriptions behind \<details\>\<summary\>](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/9/details-summary/) — Simon Willison

Willison's problem with agent-written PR descriptions is that they're too thorough: accurate and complete, but his instant reaction is "I'm not reading all of that". His fix is to write a short human summary and fold the agent's full write-up into a `<details><summary>` block, which GitHub Markdown collapses behind an expand arrow.

### [I'm Not Convinced The AI Bubble Exists](https://matteoraso.github.io/im-not-convinced-the-ai-bubble-exists.html) — Matteo Raso

A contrarian read of Anthropic's S-1, which shows revenue of **$41M** in 2023 and **$16.3B** in the first half of 2026, and says Anthropic makes "$2.08 for every $1 of compute". Raso argues that most of the roughly **$5T** in AI spending is one-off construction. The real risk is servicing the debt, not running the models.

### [Being a meat proxy for AI should be grounds for getting people fired](https://swiftrocks.com/being-a-meat-proxy-for-ai-should-be-grounds-for-getting-people-fired) — SwiftRocks (Bruno Rocha)

Rocha draws a line between AI-assisted work, where a human steers, reviews and validates, and devs who just paste a task into an agent and return the output, sometimes without saying so. He calls the second an attempt to scam the employer. In his view it should earn a warning, and termination if it continues.

### [How I'm fighting the AI Brain Fry](https://sergeykuhne.com/blog/fighting-ai-brain-fry) — Sergey Kuhne

Kuhne's answer to juggling 20 agent threads is a priority queue kept in git: one markdown ticket per task, next to the code. An agent works through the board in cycles, and only the decisions that need a human land in an "owner inbox".

### [The climate case for AI has not been made](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/11/09/1148166/the-climate-case-for-ai-has-not-been-made/) — MIT Technology Review (Mary Nichols, Jeremy Tamanini)

The UN climate conference meets in Antalya this week. The authors test the best academic estimate, that AI could cut emissions by **3.2–5.4 billion tons** a year by 2035, against what the power, meat-and-dairy and vehicle sectors are actually doing. Their verdict is that it's mostly aspirational so far. Meanwhile, the IEA's base forecast has data-center emissions possibly exceeding **300 million tons** a year by 2035.

## New Products & Tools

### [Our newest TPUs: 8tx and 8i Max, built for the agentic era](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/tpu-8i-max-and-8tx/) — Google (Amin Vahdat)

### [llama-modchip](https://github.com/synchopate/llama-modchip) — GitHub (synchopate)

A set of llama.cpp patches that runs the **284B** DeepSeek-V4-Flash in under **59 GiB** of RAM on a 64GB machine with no GPU, at **9.1–14.7 tok/s** with 256K context (**440** points on HN).

### [Reason Machines Open-Sources Aspen, a Git Server Built for AI Agents](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/09/reason-machines-aspen-open-source-git-server-ai-agents/) — MarkTechPost (Asif Razzaq)

Aspen is an independent version of the design OpenAI described for its internal Pando server. It scales writes per branch instead of per repository, stores packs in S3-compatible storage and branch history in Postgres, so that hundreds of agents can push to the same repo.

## Research

### [Your Experts Have Partners. There's HOPE for Keeping Them Together](https://huggingface.co/blog/amazon/higher-order-pruning-experts) — Hugging Face (Amazon, Alex M Tseng)

HOPE prunes Mixture-of-Experts models with a second-order method that accounts for pairs of experts that tend to activate together. At **50%** pruning it gets the best average rank of the methods tested, with gains of up to **+6.1%** on agentic coding tasks over the next-best method.

## Interviews & Conversations

### [Coding Isn't About Code Anymore](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GtJKFhztgdM) — Theo - t3.gg (45:21)

*Transcript-based summary.* Theo says "we're not really engineers anymore" and argues the job is now gardening: building the environment that code grows in, not growing the code yourself. He draws on his Twitch years working between product and platform teams. A migration that once took about eight people more than a month, fixing thousands of files tracked in a shared spreadsheet, is "one prompt now". His point is that friction hurts agents more than it hurt humans: slow dev servers, slow CI and heavy type-checking cap how much work you can run in parallel. "Slop grows faster than weeds do." His examples: one PR deleted about half of T3 Code's unit tests (**250,000** lines), and "of the 11,000 tests, I brought back four of them total". He also added a CI check, under a minute long, that flags any regression in the wire protocol's payload size, and calls it a "wood stake" that steers agents. He says merged PRs went from **2–20** a day to **400+** on active days. He is also candid that slop's source doesn't matter: "Sonnet 4.5 slop was blocking Opus 5.5."
