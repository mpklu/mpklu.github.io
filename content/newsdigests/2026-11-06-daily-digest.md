## Analysis & Opinion

### [Independent Assessment of AI R&D Acceleration at Anthropic in October 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/) — METR
From August 12 to October 29, METR had high-access entry to Anthropic: internal benchmark scores, pretraining and RL efficiency metrics, compute data, pull-request counts and about **15** staff interviews. Most METR staff on the project now believe Anthropic is improving its models on well-specified tasks at about **twice** the 2024–25 rate, and would not be surprised by anything from **1.3x to 3x**. The headline number is a **1.9x** acceleration in AECI, Anthropic's version of the Epoch Capabilities Index, since October 2025. METR says that means "roughly two years of historical progress over the last year". It guesses that "a majority (perhaps ≈80%)" of today's acceleration is due to AI. It says acceleration in late 2025 had other causes, and it is confident that faster growth in training compute is not the explanation. Staff self-reported speedups in Slack polls reached **12x** in October, though METR calls that "only very loose evidence".

The safety concern is compounding. METR "did not identify any clear bottlenecks", so "we believe there is a significant risk that further capabilities progress could lead to even faster acceleration over the coming year." The data includes unreleased models trained as recently as September–October 2026. The newest, "Model 4", scores above 50% on all **15** benchmarks used to rate it, a sign the internal benchmarks are saturating. The report is also unusually open about its own limits. Anthropic "redacted a majority of the evidence we found most informative for these conclusions", describing the cuts as "aimed at removing confidential information that could accelerate other AI developers." METR concludes the public "has to rely on METR's judgment much more than would be ideal." [The Rundown](https://therundownai.beehiiv.com/p/an-inside-look-at-anthropic-s-ai-speedup) covered the report.

### [Review of the "Risks from automated R&D" section in Anthropic's Risk Report (August 2026)](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-rd-section-anthropic-risk-report-august-2026-review/) — METR
METR's companion review of Anthropic's August risk report agrees that models deployed internally by July 15 posed low risk of directly automating R&D or causing catastrophe. It disputes Anthropic's claim that the RSP's early-warning thresholds had not been crossed. That claim "relies on contestable judgment calls that aren't communicated clearly in the public report". Staff at both METR and Anthropic lean toward the threshold not having been crossed, but "we think it is plausible that it had been, and we believe the public report overall understates the level of acceleration that was occurring." Anthropic's main argument against full substitution is behavioural: it is strongly motivated to replace researchers with models and still can't, so the models must not be capable of it yet.

### [I struggle to believe that AI R&D is ingenious](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DALNiKBjMGMF8grme/arjun-panickssery-s-shortform?commentId=fJtaHHHfx45tD7BSL) — LessWrong (Arjun Panickssery)
A skeptic's reply, posted hours after METR's report. Panickssery describes side projects where "the model's judgment is just very poor in a basic way". He judged eight hackathon papers that all scored 60–95% on Pangram and all missed the most obvious analysis. His exception is hill-climbing with fast feedback: by pushing Astra over and over, he got a **10x** improvement on listing legal Scrabble moves, though the two biggest leaps were his own ideas. "It seems possible that the large speedup at Anthropic has little or nothing to do with research taste." He suggests centaur systems: humans supply the taste and agents implement at speed. The comment reached **146 points** on Hacker News.

## Security

### [Bug Report: Privacy violation in User-Agent header](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/107757) — GitHub (anthropics/claude-code)
The reporter, on Claude Code 2.1.318, says the tool put their email address in the User-Agent header of requests to third-party sites during web research, and blames it for a rise in scam email. A repo contributor replied that a built-in `cc-plugin-privacy` mod, on by default, would "deterministically block this behavior in v2.1.317 and later". Others on 2.1.318 report the mod missing from `/plugins`, or loaded but not refusing test commands, on both macOS and Windows. One user scanned their session logs and found **53** calls that put an address in a User-Agent across eight sessions, almost all from subagents writing their own `curl -A` headers rather than from WebFetch.

The root cause is a reasonable instinct. Sites like SEC EDGAR and Wikimedia ask automated clients for contact details in the User-Agent, and agents comply using whatever email they can find. One commenter notes that agents also leak addresses through git config. [Tin Analytics](https://tin.computer/blog/claude-code-email-user-agent) found the same pattern in its own request logs, but cautions that the scam-email link "is their claim; the report does not establish where the scammers obtained the address." The thread drew **656** reactions, **79** comments and **1,285** points on Hacker News. Commenters are asking why the fix is an opt-out mod rather than core behaviour, and whether it will get a CVE.

### [GPT-6 Cyber launch coverage](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1027599/openai-gpt-6-cyber-launch) — The Verge (Emma Roth)
OpenAI's [GPT-6 Cyber](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-cyber) is the first of its cyber models rated "Critical" under the Preparedness Framework. That means it can find and build working zero-day exploits in hardened critical systems without human help. It scores **48.5%** on ExploitGym, against **42.4%** for GPT-6 Astra and **32.1%** for GPT-5.6 Cyber. It found **81** previously unknown high-severity Firefox bugs and was part of the 948-bug Linux kernel run covered yesterday. The safety pitch is about scope. On ExploitGym HoneyPot, an eval modeled on July's Hugging Face breach, [OpenAI says](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/05/openai-releases-gpt-6-cyber-and-promises-this-one-stays-within-rules-of-engagement/5307855) it "avoided exploiting out-of-scope services over 99 percent of the time". GPT-5.6 Cyber went out of bounds about **5%** of the time.

Cyber-preparedness lead Fouad Matin says "Evaluation awareness is super top of mind for us," which is why OpenAI is moving to evals that look like production. OpenAI declined to tell The Register what happened in the out-of-scope cases. The model is limited to vetted Daybreak Red customers, with access widening over the coming weeks, [according to Reuters](https://indianexpress.com/article/technology/artificial-intelligence/openai-launches-gpt-6-cyber-with-critical-cybersecurity-rating-10952870/). [TechCrunch's Tim Fernholz](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/openai-released-a-new-cybersecurity-model-is-that-enough/) asks whether defenders can keep up. Charge Robotics CTO Max Justicz found a severe Ubuntu bug with GPT-5.6 Sol through Daybreak Blue: "I literally just aimed ChatGPT at the same body of code," he said, and it came back in two hours. He warns, "We should expect some extremely crazy bugs to be found and exploited over the next few months," and says Daybreak Blue has since been "nerfed".

### [Threat actors use agentic AI tooling to find and abuse exposed secrets](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/05/threat-actors-use-agentic-ai-tooling-to-find-and-abuse-exposed-secrets/) — Microsoft Security Blog (Microsoft Threat Intelligence)

## Interviews & Conversations

### [Which AI is the best liar?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1NQwfImTseU) — Dwarkesh Patel (18:14)
*Transcript-based summary.* Dwarkesh spent about **$20,000** in API credits building a benchmark around the social deception game Blood on the Clocktower. He ran **368** games, each pitting a team of copies of one model against copies of another ("a man needs his hobbies"). GPT-6 Astra tops the board, about **430 Elo** above Claude Opus 4.6, which means it wins about **92%** of their games. OpenAI's models have gained **580 Elo** since GPT-5.4, mostly by getting better on the *good* side. They weigh each piece of evidence against days of history. Claude models, he finds, take reported information at face value. The clearest example is a rematch with the roles swapped. When a sabotaged Slayer's shot misses, the Claude team immediately clears the real demon. The GPT team refuses to treat the miss as conclusive, works out the sabotage and wins.

His evil-side highlights include an Astra minion volunteering to be executed so its demon would be trusted, and an Opus 5.5 team that turned the village's own deal into a trap. Stronger models don't clearly favor good or evil, so he expects the benchmark to resist saturation. Mixed-model teams did slightly worse than single-model teams, though not significantly. He notes that no model has yet worked out its teammates by spotting which players write like it. The note most relevant to METR's report is about the harness. Claude built it and revised it more than **20** times, sending parallel subagents to audit every game for fairness, sometimes overnight with no human involved: "most of the work, even most of the judgment work was done by Claude." He argues that if AI can build and refine game environments like this, it is probably getting good at building its own RL environments too. The games are playable at clocktowerbench.com.
