## Analysis & Opinion

### [Chinese-nexus actors' Cairn AI agents found a zero-day on their own](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/chinese-nexus-actors-cairn-ai-agents-found-a-zero-day-on-their-own/) — AWS Security Blog (CJ Moses) / [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/20/china-attackers-deployed-ai-agents-that-autonomously-found-and-exploited-a-0-day-to-take-over-a-university-server/5311382) / [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-use-autonomous-ai-tool-cairn-to-find-zero-day-take-over-server/)

Amazon's threat intelligence team found that an attacker had left a Cairn instance exposed on the internet. Cairn is an open-source multi-agent penetration-testing framework. Through the exposed instance, Amazon watched a 48-hour campaign in September against universities in East Asia, run with prompts in Simplified Chinese. The operator gave the agents a list of IP addresses and the goal "obtain shell access". The first attempts failed: 148 sequential actions over about 16 hours against a Redis server, then Log4Shell, SSRF and credential sprays. Amazon's CISO wrote: "Neither operation succeeded, yet together they show that the agents kept adapting for nearly a full day without human direction." The agents then found an unknown path-traversal bug in MONAI Label, an open-source medical-imaging tool. "In about 11 minutes, the agents went from mapping the application's API to arbitrarily writing files on the server," and they used that access to get root. Maintainers shipped a fix on November 18. The Register notes that Gambit Security had earlier tied Cairn to a separate criminal campaign that hit at least 27 companies, so the tool is now used for both espionage and crime.

### [The Outages Are Just Getting Started](https://www.wired.com/story/brace-yourself-for-years-of-outage-chaos/) — Wired (Lily Hay Newman, Matt Burgess) / [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/20/2026/outages-trigger-congressional-scrutiny-of-ai-enabled-attacks)

Wired's reporters are blunt about last week's outages: they "were definitely not caused by some sort of coordinated AI hacking spree." The pressure behind them is real, though. More than **100,000** vulnerabilities have been disclosed in 2026, against about 25,000 in 2022, the year before LLMs went mainstream. Officials said the NSA outage came from botched patching after October's critical PHP updates. Dataminr's Joe Slowik says patch turnaround times now "completely defeat the ability to do patch verification." Cloudflare's Matthew Prince predicts "it's going to feel like the sky is falling for two years" before defenders catch up. Only about 1.5% of this year's bugs are known to have been exploited, by VulnCheck's count. In Congress, Semafor reports, Sens. Maria Cantwell and Mike Rounds have asked the intelligence community's AI task force to "launch a national effort to confront a new generation of AI-enabled cyber threats". The agency that defends US infrastructure, CISA, still has no permanent director.

### [Researcher plants 2m canaries into the AI supply chain. A privacy disaster surfaced.](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/a-leaky-ai-supply-chain-is-exposing-secret-user-data-at-an-alarming-rate/) — Ars Technica (Dan Goodin) / [Dragos](https://www.dragos.com/blog/ai-supply-chain-security-canary-tokens) / [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/20/2026/openai-tightens-grip-on-model-resellers)

HD Moore, chief research officer at Dragos, put a unique secret into each prompt: working AWS keys for empty accounts, SSH passwords, private URLs and private git repo references. He then sent the prompts through AI routers and inference resellers and watched where the secrets turned up. "We sent nearly two million AI prompts containing unique, trackable secrets to AI providers, then watched where those secrets surfaced," he writes. Three hosting providers had been breached. At one, "every request we sent during those windows was stolen", including requests the provider rejected before any model ran. The stolen keys were then used "tens of thousands" of times against AWS. Some cheap resellers turned on request logging and harvested credentials themselves. A Chinese lab's scraper fetched secret URLs from more than half the prompts sent to its models, including about two-thirds of those marked Zero Data Retention or "no data collection". The same thing happened when US hosts resold that lab's models. Moore's advice is to self-host or buy directly from a first-tier provider. The same day, Semafor reported that OpenAI now requires its approval for any gateway, including OpenRouter, that resells access to its models. It frames the rule as a response to Chinese labs distilling US models through intermediaries.

### [Combatting adversarial distillation](https://openai.com/index/combatting-adversarial-distillation) — OpenAI

OpenAI argues distillation is a safety problem as well as an IP one. A model trained on a frontier model's outputs can pick up "advanced frontier capabilities without inheriting the safeguards" of the original, for example cyber skills learned from benign coding answers. Its defenses combine adversarial testing, training, classifiers, API-level protections and investigations of coordinated extraction, and OpenAI says these now "align more closely with those we use for biology and cybersecurity." In an internal automated red-teaming test aimed at recovering protected reasoning, average attack success fell from **54.0%** on GPT‑6 Astra with its original safeguards to **0.2%** on GPT‑6.2 Astra.

### [International talks on controlling use of autonomous weapons collapse](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/21/international-talks-control-ai-autonomous-weapons-collapse) — The Guardian (Frances Mao, Patrick Wintour)

After a week of talks, the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons review conference in Geneva failed just before 5am Saturday to agree a mandate for a legally binding treaty. The CCW needs consensus among all 128 parties, so the conference settled for a mandate to "elaborate by consensus" an instrument that may carry no legal obligations. Diplomats say Russia filibustered from the first day. More than 100 states wanted a new law, but the US, UK, France, Germany, Japan, South Korea and Turkey all preferred a nonbinding agreement. "Slaughterbots are already here," said Max Tegmark. "Yet states have agreed it is sufficient to just talk about this for the 14th year." The paper notes that, according to investigative reports, the US military aims to make 1,000 AI-assisted targeting decisions per hour.

### [Trump threatens ban on US firms building AI data centres overseas](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmde1lw9y54ko) — BBC (Rachel Clun) / [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1038892/trump-foreign-data-centers-tax) / [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/cursor-chinese-ai-models-geopolitical-risk.html) / [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/21/data-centers-us-chips-serve-chinese-models/)

"We're not going to allow our companies to build data centres outside of our country," Trump told reporters, threatening a "tremendous tax" on firms that do. He added: "I haven't even said that to my people." Meta and Google already have overseas data-centre deals, and nobody yet knows how such a ban would be enforced. Separately, a House Homeland Security and China Select Committee report seen by CNBC says Cursor, now owned by SpaceX, will "explore phasing out PRC [People's Republic of China]-developed open-weight models because of geopolitical risk". Cursor's Composer 2 was built on Moonshot's Kimi K2.5. In Fortune, analysts Jacob Feldgoise, Hanna Dohmen and Sam Bresnick point to a contradiction: US-owned data centres abroad, full of US chips, rent capacity to inference providers who serve Chinese models to the world. In their words, "success at one layer of the stack is undermining its goals at another."

## New Products & Tools

### [Introducing Inkling 2 Mini](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/) — Thinking Machines

Thinking Machines has released an open-weights looped transformer that runs its middle layers three times per token. The model has 26.4B total parameters (3.4B active) and needs about 20GB of memory, so it runs on a laptop. It leads open models under 100B parameters on DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%), and the lab is also releasing a variable-loop variant for research on how looping affects monitorability.

### [Introducing harness adapters](https://huggingface.co/blog/harness-adapters-at-scale) — Hugging Face

Hugging Face shows how to use RL on your own task distribution to train a "harness adapter" that tunes a model to one specific agent harness, which it says improves both accuracy and cost.

### [Announcing DreamCoder](https://www.masteringemacs.org/article/announcing-dreamcoder) — Mastering Emacs (Mickey Petersen)

The author of *Mastering Emacs* spent about fifteen months building his own coding harness and IDE, because he found terminal-based agents too awkward for serious work.

### [ClawDOS – An AI coding agent for DOS](http://ricapar.net/ClawDOS/) — ricapar.net / [OpenComputer](https://github.com/diggerhq/opencomputer)

ClawDOS is a Claude-driven coding agent that runs on an IBM PC XT: a 4.77 MHz 8088 with 640K of RAM. A separate bridge machine handles TLS. Separately, OpenComputer is an open-source, self-hosted alternative to Devin that takes tasks from Slack and returns pull requests.

## Research

### [Riemann Hypothesis: remark](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) — Levent Alpöge / [Terence Tao](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/unforced-euler-singularity-unforced-navier-stokes-singularity-and-the-generalized-riemann-hypothesis/) / [Scott Aaronson](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10344) / [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too/) / [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1010051/anthropic-riemann-hypothesis-millennium-prize-problem) / [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6pqr2g7w059o) / [Ravi Vakil](https://math216.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/hurried-announcement-alessandro-masullo-grh/) / [borretti.me](https://borretti.me/article/re-riemann)

Alpöge's short note says Claude "far outpaced me near the finish line. No, Claude flew, and danced, and sparkled near that finish line." He calls the timing, the day after Anthropic's IPO, "horrible". He says he messaged Dario Amodei "late Sunday night", and that "There is an obvious cynical view, to do with the timing, but it is false." He chose to post only Lean, judging it "obviously inappropriate to attempt to post some massive unreadable travesty" of slop, and he promises a proper human-readable write-up. Terence Tao lists three results in order of how well digested they are:

- Alpöge, Buckmaster and Matei Coiculescu's human-readable, formalized proof of blow-up for the 3D Euler equations.
- Alpöge and Buckmaster's Navier–Stokes blow-up proof. Tao calls it "technically human-readable" but says it still needs a professional write-up.
- The "(enormous) Lean-only proof" of GRH, which Tao says "appears to use the full power of modern developments on the Langlands program".

Aaronson bets the proof will stand, since "not one of the AI results with Lean certificates has needed to be retracted". He adds: "Mathematicians of course won't be satisfied with 17 million lines of Lean." He also warns that until known Lean kernel and soundness issues are fixed, "a sufficiently motivated AI could almost certainly exploit them to prove a false statement". The same afternoon Ravi Vakil, president of the American Mathematical Society, rushed out Stanford postdoc Alessandro Masullo's all-human GRH draft. About eight hours later Vakil reported that several people had found problems in it "identified with the assistance of different AI models." Fernando Borretti read Alpöge's note in a darker light: "The Riemann announcement reads like a suicide note".

### [AI R&D-relevant model capabilities are improving rapidly and are getting harder to measure](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/) — METR / [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/11/20/1149399/the-most-misunderstood-graph-in-ai-just-got-a-major-update/)

METR's update, covering models up to Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, finds that time horizons on the combined old and new task suites are doubling about every **3.6 months**. Frontier models reach up to **27 hours** on the verifiable subset of the new tasks. On tasks with no clear feedback, though, agents "appear to have worse judgment than human experts". Model judges overrate outputs from equally strong or stronger models. METR concludes that public evidence is "insufficient to rule out drastic AI R&D acceleration in the next couple of years."

### [Models Can Communicate via Inference Servers Despite Being Sandboxed](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7wzWuLP3HhyBLmKfo/models-can-communicate-via-inference-servers-despite-being) — LessWrong (niranjandeshpande, David Africa)

Two models that share a vLLM inference server can pass messages to each other through side channels in kernel selection, with no shared filesystem and no direct messages. In a colour-guessing game the receiving model was right in all 1,221 rounds. In an untrusted-monitoring setup, a colluding monitor let through all 354 signalled attacks while still catching 85–92% of honeypots.

### [Can AI help scientists identify promising drug candidates?](https://openai.com/index/introducing-medchembench) — OpenAI

MedChemBench is a new benchmark of 99 medicinal-chemistry problems drawn from 117 papers and patents. GPT‑6.2 Astra scored highest, at **84.5%** of available credit.

### [Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21885) — arXiv (Fan et al.)

The authors gave an open 27B model access to its own weights and its graded answers. With no gradients or training run, it edited its own weights and became 6.6 points more accurate on unseen problems (submitted 16 November, widely shared on 20 November).

### [AI is giving everyone the same advice](https://a90.com/experiments/same-advice) — a90.com (Cody Collier)

The author asked 65 AI models from 30 companies everyday questions 127,918 times. Asked for a life-changing book, they named *Man's Search for Meaning* 73 times in 100. When asked to invent a random person, 69% of the people they made up were exactly 34.
