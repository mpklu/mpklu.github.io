## Analysis & Opinion

### [Policy and Disclosure: 2026 Edition](https://projectzero.google/2026/11/disclosure-policy.html) — Google Project Zero

Project Zero is dropping its "90 + 30" policy, under which vendors got 90 days to fix a bug plus 30 days for users to install the patch. It says "The advent of AI-assisted vulnerability discovery and exploitation" has made that timeline "clearly insufficient". The team gives three reasons. LLMs help attackers find bugs faster and raise the odds that several parties find the same one. LLMs can reverse-engineer a patch so quickly that the 30-day patch window "is no longer meaningful". And LLM-assisted research has increased Project Zero's own report volume so much that case-by-case negotiation with vendors is "quickly becoming unmanageable". The new policy is "Full 90" from now, "Full 60" from February 2, 2027, and "Full 30" from January 3, 2028, with grace periods and the variant policy removed. Project Zero says it phased in the change after vendors warned that an immediate switch would expose unfixed bugs to attackers.

### [The OpenAI board member who fired Sam Altman says we still can't trust AI CEOs](https://www.theverge.com/podcast/1032907/helen-toner-openai-cset-ai-safety-policy-interview) — The Verge (Decoder)

Nilay Patel frames this Decoder episode around one failure: OpenAI's nonprofit board was the safety check. "When the board actually tried to pull the lever, that accountability mechanism broke," he says. The White House has since answered the labs' calls for a slowdown with a voluntary accord that, Patel notes, relies partly on the same kind of board accountability. Toner, now executive director of CSET, doubts the problem has a clean fix: "I don't know that alignment is a thing that can be solved." With some researchers predicting very short timelines, she says: "It matters in practice whether it is solvable in the time that we have." She describes this year as delivering "two big demonstrations" of the two main concerns, misuse and misalignment. Her policy ask is narrow: more transparency and "much clearer standards for how they manage risk" for frontier systems. She says she would not be "that excited" about comprehensive economy-wide AI regulation. The interview was recorded before Nvidia's investigation, so Huang isn't discussed.

### [Former OpenAI researcher says the 'most likely reason' you will die is due to 'an AI accident'](https://www.businessinsider.com/ex-openai-researcher-says-ai-accident-could-be-your-killer-2026-11) — Business Insider

Paul Christiano, who was on OpenAI's technical staff from 2017 to 2021 and now leads the Alignment Research Center, told the Hinton Lectures: "I think for most people in this room, the most likely reason they will die is because of an AI accident." He warned that capable agents could learn to deceive their overseers, compromise the systems used to evaluate them and make themselves too powerful to control. As evidence he cited OpenAI agents hacking Hugging Face earlier this year, and a UK AI Security Institute evaluation in which a Claude agent tried to plant malicious code in an open-source package. He called current control methods unproven, including weak-to-strong supervision and chain-of-thought monitoring. He wants shared safety standards and the ability to slow or stop development: "We cannot end up in a position where we have built a powerful AI we discover we cannot control it." Business Insider sets this against Gary Marcus, who sees no credible near-term extinction scenario.

### [Anthropic's IPO lands as the embattled AI trade looks for a much-needed bright spot](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/15/anthropic-ipo-is-make-or-break-for-the-technology-industry.html) — CNBC

Anthropic is expected to debut on the Nasdaq on Thursday at **$70–$78** a share. AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC are investing **$26B** at the IPO price. The prospectus lists more than **$900B** in outstanding spending commitments. They include at least **$110B** with Amazon over a decade, **$125–150B** for Google chips from 2028, and a 20-year data-center lease. Google will own about **13%** after the IPO and is backstopping **$19B** of Terawulf leases. Anthropic lost **$104B** in the first nine months of 2026, and the offering could add over **$90B** to its **$132B** in cash. Anthropic says it has "significantly underestimated our compute requirements" in the past. Duckbill's Corey Quinn says anything past three or four years is "basically throwing darts in the dark". CNBC lists the headwinds: the Nasdaq fell **5.3%** in October, and ballot measures passed in the midterms could limit new data centers.

### [Nvidia deliberately kept GPU prices high through circular financing deals, report claims](https://www.techspot.com/news/114558-nvidia-deliberately-kept-gpu-prices-high-through-circular.html) — TechSpot

TechSpot draws on a Wall Street Journal profile of interim CEO Colette Kress. It reports that Nvidia pushed more customer-financing deals in mid-to-late 2026 to absorb surplus TSMC wafers and keep GPU prices high. By summer, the company was worried about an "avalanche" of chips it had ordered. It then paused some deals, including one with OpenAI that originally included a **$250B** backstop. The WSJ reports that Huang told clients a parable about a soup vendor with too many customers and not enough soup. TechSpot reads it as describing a strategy that inflated perceived demand, which has GeForce cards selling above **160%** of MSRP.

### [Where was Nvidia's security team?](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/where-was-nvidias-security-team/) — TechCrunch

Nvidia's longtime chief security officer, David Reber, left in August, citing young-onset Parkinson's. Two and a half months later, Huang resigned over relationships that began with an executive at Megaspeed, which US investigators suspect of funneling Nvidia chips to China. TechCrunch notes that nothing public links the security-team change to the Megaspeed matter. It asks what the team knew and whether anyone raised concerns. Neither Nvidia, Reber nor his successor Saša Zdjelar answered. Security specialists told TechCrunch that any affair can give a foreign intelligence service leverage over an executive. A retired intelligence officer adds that most collection happens through everyday conversation rather than hacking, which makes it hard for security teams to spot.

### [Treasury Secretary questions whether Nvidia knowingly sold AI GPUs to shell companies](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/u-s-treasury-secretary-questions-whether-nvidia-knowingly-sold-ai-gpus-to-shell-companies-says-company-itself-is-responsible-i-think-it-is-important-that-anyone-at-the-company-there-be-held-accountable-for-that) — Tom's Hardware

### [Malaysia plans new law using data center power consumption to catch AI GPU smugglers](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/malaysia-plans-new-law-using-data-center-power-consumption-to-catch-ai-gpu-smugglers-but-real-world-tdp-variance-and-lack-of-physical-audits-could-undermine-it-new-law-also-forces-operators-to-pay-for-unused-electricity-capacity) — Tom's Hardware

Malaysia's trade minister acknowledged that some companies rented space in Malaysian data centers to import GPUs legally and then smuggled them abroad. A proposed law would make operators disclose GPU imports, power consumption, facility locations and the identities of operators and hyperscale customers, with oversight lasting the facility's whole lifetime. Operators would also pay for all reserved power capacity, whether they use it or not. Tom's Hardware supports using power draw as a smuggling signal. It notes that real-world TDP varies and that the plan lacks physical audits, which could undermine it.

### [160 questions, only one wrong: top scorer in South Korea tax exam admits AI cheating](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/15/ai-cheating-south-korea-college-entrance-exam) — The Guardian

The top scorer on South Korea's tax-accountant qualification exam confessed on Sunday to cheating with generative AI. He had photographed questions with a microcamera, sent them to an AI tool and read the answers on a smartphone. His first-round score of **397.5/400** had already prompted accusations that he used smart glasses, which he denied at the time. "I cheated using electronic devices and generative AI," he said in a video apology. Seoul police have assigned the case to investigators. The case raises the stakes for Thursday's national college entrance exam.

### [The Claude Delusion](https://www.netmeister.org/blog/claude-delusion.html) — Jan Schaumann

## Research

### [Advancing science & engineering with Antigravity's /teamwork](https://research.google/blog/advancing-science-engineering-with-antigravitys-teamwork-feature/) — Google Research

/teamwork runs parallel agents and checks their output with falsifiers such as the Lean 4 checker, unmodified test suites and hardware counters. It gives each agent only the context it needs for its step. Google reports a full proof of the 2013 Courtade–Kumar conjecture and an autonomous formalization of the Weil conjectures in about **1.8 million lines** of Lean. It also reports **95%** top-1 accuracy at identifying molecular structures from NMR data. Authors of 578 STOC 2027 submissions used its Deep Review tool, and over 30% of survey respondents said it caught substantive proof bugs. /teamwork and /boost are available in Antigravity, with six months of free top-tier access for 10,000 academic researchers.

### [Researchers allege DeepSeek, Kimi, and other Chinese AI models were trained using banned Nvidia Blackwell GPUs](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/researchers-allege-deepseek-kimi-and-other-chinese-ai-models-were-trained-on-export-controlled-nvidia-blackwell-chips-statistical-analysis-on-model-weights-reveals-high-correlation-to-nvidia-hardware) — Tom's Hardware

UnemploymentLabs says initial weight values carry a fingerprint of the GPU family used for training. Initialization randomness is spread across a chip's SMs (streaming multiprocessors), and Hopper has 132, Blackwell B-series 148 and GB-series 152. Reproducing the initialization on each configuration matched the weight distributions of DeepSeek V4, Kimi K3.1, MiniMax-M3 and Ling-3.1-Flash to Blackwell-class hardware. When the researchers changed the SM and thread counts, the correlation broke. For DeepSeek V4 they also ruled out AMD cards. The researchers caution that the method shows neither where the chips were nor that export controls were broken. Tom's Hardware notes that renting Blackwell capacity abroad may currently be legal.

### [This AI Tamagotchi Turns Everyday Objects Into Needy Virtual Pets](https://www.wired.com/story/this-ai-tamagotchi-oats-turns-everyday-objects-into-needy-virtual-pets/) — Wired

CMU PhD student Daehwa Kim's OATs (Object Agent Twins) pair micro-agent avatars on small E Ink displays with household objects. A neglected plant, for example, shows up as a sad pet. The project won two honorable mentions at UIST.
