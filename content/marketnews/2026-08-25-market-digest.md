## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Oil hits one-week low as investors shrug off latest U.S. sanctions on Iran](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/25/oil-hits-one-week-low-as-investors-shrug-off-iran-sanctions.html) — CNBC

The day's dominant macro story, and it cuts against the obvious reading. WTI settled at **$80.32 (-5.52%)** and Brent at **$85.05 (-7.72%)** — a sharp break lower *despite* Washington rolling out expanded sanctions targeting Iran's economy and shipping networks, plus pressure on countries that continue trading with Tehran.

Crude fell *because* of the sanctions, not in spite of them. ING commodity strategists noted the market "seems largely unfazed by Washington's push for tighter economic pressure on Iran, with traders treating the US effort to nudge partners away from Iranian trade as marginal rather than market moving." The [Oil & Gas Journal](https://www.ogj.com/general-interest/economics-markets/news/55400452/oil-prices-fall-as-us-sanctions-on-iran-posing-limited-near-term-supply-risk) framed the same point: the announcement posed limited near-term supply risk. The deeper shift is that the US-Israel confrontation with Iran has moved from military escalation to economic pressure, which *deflates* the war-risk premium that had been priced into crude. Traders are also positioning ahead of an OPEC production-policy meeting next week.

This is risk-premium unwinding, not demand destruction — an important distinction, because the two have opposite implications for equities.

### [Warsh to Make First Jackson Hole Speech as Fed Chair](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/warsh-first-jackson-hole-speech-200000444.html) — Yahoo Finance

Fed Chair **Kevin Warsh**, sworn in May 22, delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote **Friday, August 28 at 10:00 a.m. ET**. The symposium runs August 27–29 under the theme "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy."

The stakes are unusual. The July FOMC held the target range at **3.50%–3.75%** with *three dissents in favor of a hike* — an unusually hawkish split. Markets price roughly **34% odds of a September rate increase**, down from above 75% in mid-July after CPI cooled to 3.4%. Warsh gets twenty minutes at the podium three weeks before the September 15–16 meeting, and long-end Treasury yields have risen materially since he took the chair.

Today's crude collapse quietly undercuts part of the hawkish case. A meaningful strand of the September-hike argument rested on energy-driven inflation pressure; a 5–8% break in crude erodes that input directly.

### [US stocks advance as investors shrug off trade and geopolitical worries](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-aug-25-2026) — TheStreet

Broad gains with Treasury yields falling roughly 7bp to 4.62%. The timing is not coincidental. These are high-beta, pre-revenue proxies for the AI power-buildout thesis, bouncing on the eve of the print that will either validate or puncture that thesis. Treat the magnitude as a positioning signal rather than a fundamental re-rating.

## Market Outlook

Equities closed broadly higher in a session whose real action was in commodities: crude broke 5–8% lower as traders judged new Iran sanctions to be economically symbolic rather than supply-disruptive, while gold pushed to a three-month high and the 10-year yield fell 7bp. The divergence matters — falling energy prices with a firm gold bid points to a market repricing *monetary* policy rather than geopolitical risk, and a crude decline of this size materially weakens the energy-inflation strand of the September rate-hike case that markets still handicap near one-in-three. Beneath the index level, positioning grew visibly more aggressive: the semiconductor complex recovered half of Monday's drop and beaten-down nuclear names bounced 5–12% on no company-specific news, both of which are leveraged expressions of the same AI-capex bet rather than independent signals of conviction. That concentration warrants caution, because two catalysts now sit back-to-back and either could resolve it violently. Watch NVIDIA's guidance tomorrow after the close — where a beat is essentially fully priced and the stock has fallen after six of its last eight reports — and then Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote Friday morning, which will set the tone for September far more than any single data release this week.
