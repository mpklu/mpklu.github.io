## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [NVIDIA's Q2 blowout resets the AI growth debate](https://fortune.com/2026/08/27/nvidia-stock-q2-earnings-call-i-think-the-whole-market-was-like-whoa-analyst-says/) — Fortune

NVIDIA posted fiscal Q2 revenue of **$96.2 billion, up 106% year over year** and 18% sequentially, against analyst estimates near $92.2 billion. The more consequential item was guidance: the company issued a rare full-year fiscal 2028 growth forecast of **70%**, versus roughly 44% consensus, with CEO Jensen Huang noting that demand exceeds even that figure and supply is the binding constraint. The print pulled the entire semiconductor and AI-infrastructure complex higher on Thursday and accounts for most of the NASDAQ's 1.57% gain.

### [Warsh takes the Jackson Hole podium for the first time as Fed Chair](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-22/kevin-warsh-to-make-first-jackson-hole-speech-as-fed-chair) — Bloomberg

The Kansas City Fed's annual Economic Policy Symposium runs **August 27–29** in Jackson Hole, themed "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy." Kevin Warsh, who succeeded Jerome Powell as Chair in May, delivers his first symposium keynote **Friday, August 28**. With inflation still above target and the 10-year yield at 4.67%, the speech is the market's clearest read yet on how this Fed weighs sticky inflation against a visibly cooling labor market ahead of the September 15–16 FOMC meeting.

### [Meta settles state child-safety claims for up to $17.1 billion](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/26/nx-s1-5944781/meta-settlement-child-safety-lawsuit) — NPR

Meta agreed to pay **47 states, the District of Columbia and several territories up to $17.1 billion** to resolve claims that its platforms were designed to be addictive and harmful to minors — the largest settlement of its kind. Beyond the payment, Meta accepts a two-hour default daily cap for under-18 users, stronger age verification, restricted access during school hours and overnight, an independent auditor with broad access, and an injunction against further misleading safety claims. The agreement still requires judicial approval, and several provisions tighten automatically if YouTube, TikTok and Snap adopt similar measures.

### [Druckenmiller attacks Treasury's long-end bond buybacks](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/25/stanley-druckenmiller-leads-doubters-who-think-bessents-bond-ploys-will-fail.html) — CNBC

Treasury announced on August 19 that it would **double long-dated buybacks from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation**, targeting 10-to-30-year maturities, after the 30-year yield hit a **19-year high**. The intervention drew immediate criticism from Stanley Druckenmiller — Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's early-career mentor — who called it a mistake rooted in price management. The episode matters beyond the personalities: it signals that the long end, not the policy rate, is now the binding constraint on fiscal and monetary flexibility.

## Notable Voices

### [Stanley Druckenmiller leads doubters who think Bessent's bond ploys will fail](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/25/stanley-druckenmiller-leads-doubters-who-think-bessents-bond-ploys-will-fail.html) — Stanley Druckenmiller, CNBC (2026-08-25)

In an essay titled "Let the Bond Market Speak," Druckenmiller urged Treasury Secretary Bessent — his own former protégé — to abandon the expanded long-end buyback program and let the market price government debt freely. His line, "every basis point of artificial yield suppression is a subsidy to procrastination," frames the buybacks as fiscal avoidance rather than market plumbing. A wrinkle worth noting: Druckenmiller acknowledged the letter was drafted with AI assistance, which became its own news cycle.

### [Dan Ives Says the AI Trade Is Only in the "Third Inning"](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/27/dan-ives-says-were-in-the-3rd-inning-of-the-ai-rev/) — Dan Ives, The Motley Fool (2026-08-27)

Ives continues to argue the AI cycle is roughly a third of the way through a nine-inning game, with the story rotating from chip capex toward monetization across infrastructure, cloud and software. He sees further upside in tech for the balance of 2026 and names Palantir as his preferred non-Magnificent-Seven exposure. Today's NVIDIA guidance is the strongest evidence yet for the capex leg of his thesis; the monetization leg remains unproven.

## Market Outlook

The NASDAQ's 1.57% advance was effectively one stock's story: NVIDIA's 8.74% surge on 106% revenue growth and a 70% forward growth guide dragged the semiconductor complex up with it, while the VIX slid to 14.51. What distinguishes this move from earlier AI-momentum episodes is that it is anchored in reported results and issued guidance rather than multiple expansion — but breadth stayed thin, with the Dow up only 0.20% and the Russell 2000 up 0.28%, and gold's simultaneous 1.33% gain to $4,659 suggests the hedging bid never left. The macro backdrop warrants more caution than the tape implies: Michigan sentiment near 51, one-year inflation expectations at 4.3%, an August payroll consensus of just 12,000 jobs, and a 30-year yield at a 19-year high that has already pushed Treasury into doubling long-end buybacks. That combination — decelerating labor, sticky expectations, and a long end that resists suppression — is a harder problem than any single earnings report can resolve. Attention now turns to Chair Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote on Friday morning and the September 4 payroll report, which together will set the terms for the September 15–16 FOMC meeting.
