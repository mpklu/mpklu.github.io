## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Brent crude oil tops $116, a six-month high, as tensions swell in the Middle East](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/oil-price-brent-wti-iran-war-saudi-arabia.html) — CNBC

Brent settled up 3.1% at **$116.72**, its highest close since April 29, and WTI up 3.8% at **$103.27**, as investors increasingly price in renewed U.S. strikes on Iran after the Nov. 3 midterms. Brent is up 6.2% on the week, its third straight weekly gain. Iran's army chief said the military is prepared for "asymmetric warfare," Houthi drone strikes on the Shaybah oil field and Dammam's airport killed four people this week, the State Department has ordered non-essential staff out of Saudi Arabia, and Reuters reported Saudi Aramco told Dhahran headquarters staff to work remotely. Crude above $100 is also the one thing the bond market agreed on today (see below).

### [10-year Treasury yield drops back to 5% this week as investors seek cover from AI worries](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/bonds-yields-us-treasurys.html) — CNBC

The 10-year yield rose 5 basis points to **4.999%** on Friday after breaking below 5% on Thursday for the first time in a month; the 2-year rose 5 bp to 4.686% and the 30-year 4 bp to 5.312%. The AI credit stress has not eased: CoreWeave's 9.75% 2031 bond now yields **15.637%** and its five-year CDS spread sits at **1,129 bp**, a level generally considered distressed, and Morgan Stanley cut its CoreWeave target to $70 from $99 on Friday while saying a hyperscaler-backed financing is "the most reasonable path forward." Fwdbonds' Chris Rupkey said the only consistent story on Friday was oil, which "means more or less that more inflation is on the way." Markets expect no change at next week's FOMC from the current **3.75% to 4.00%** range but price a roughly **71%** chance of a quarter-point hike in December, per CME FedWatch.

### [Flash PMI: U.S. economy "continues to boom," prices charged jump](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-10-23/gold-struggles-sp-pmi-shows-health-activity-manufacturing-services-sectors) — Kitco News

S&P Global's October flash composite PMI eased to **57.6** from 58.4, but manufacturing rose to **56.0** (consensus 55.5, prior 55.9) and services held at **57.9** (prior 58.8, in line). Chris Williamson of S&P Global: "October's flash PMI data indicate that the US economy continues to boom at the start of the fourth quarter. The output readings from the survey are consistent with GDP growing at a 5% annualized rate after a 4% expansion was signalled for the third quarter." The University of Michigan's final October [consumer sentiment](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/22/stock-market-today-live-update.html) came in at **48**, essentially flat on the month and above the 46.3 consensus, with expectations up 5.4% and current conditions down 8.3%. Neither print gives the Fed a reason to soften next week.

### [France is flashing euro crisis signals. This time, the fault lines are different](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/france-debt-euro-zone-contagion.html) — CNBC

The French 10-year yield broke above 5% this week for the first time since 2002, and the spread over Bunds passed **170 bp** on Thursday for the first time since the early-2010s debt crisis. Moody's rating review was due late Friday, France will deploy 70,000 police for Saturday's protests, and per [Reuters via CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/yen-shines-as-ai-jitters-spur-safe-haven-rush.html) the euro fell to its lowest against the yen since October 2025 while the yen headed for its best week since early September on safe-haven flows out of the AI trade.

### [American Express is cutting 5% of its workforce to 'prepare for AI'](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/american-express-job-cuts.html) — CNBC

Amex is taking a **$336 million** restructuring charge to cut roughly 5% of its nearly 77,000 staff; CFO Christophe Le Caillec said the goal is to "reduce layers and increase spans of controls, so that we can automate and take advantage of AI." Barclays confirmed a similar 5% cut on Thursday. The stock's 3% drop was the biggest single weight on the Dow, and the pattern, strong profits plus AI-justified layoffs, is becoming the financial sector's template. Separately, Sen. Susan Collins said the U.S. will [exempt nine categories of Canadian imports](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/trump-tariffs-canada-susan-collins.html) from tariffs, and Trump signed an [executive order on mortgage insurance and refinancing](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/trump-mortgage-executive-order-costs.html) whose consumer impact is unclear without details.

## Market Outlook

Friday was the day the safety trade ran out of buyers: the Dow fell 653 points on staples, utilities, health care and an AI-justified layoff at American Express, while the Nasdaq barely moved and the chip stocks that were crushed on Thursday bounced. That is not a resolution of the AI financing question, which CoreWeave's 1,129 bp CDS and Morgan Stanley's target cut say is still open; it is a market with nowhere left to hide, and the one asset that did catch a bid, crude at $103 WTI and $116.72 Brent on Iran strike expectations, is itself an inflation problem. The macro tape leans hawkish: flash PMIs that S&P Global says are consistent with 5% annualized growth, the fastest price-charged readings since 2022, and a 71% December-hike probability mean the 10-year's trip below 5% was a flight to safety, not a growth scare, and it reversed within a day. Our watchlist's nuclear names printed new 52-week lows while Apple printed a new high and is about to pass Nvidia in market value, which is the whole rotation in one line. Next week is the test: UPS and SoFi on Monday, Alphabet and the FOMC decision on Wednesday, then Amazon, Intel and Strategy into Thursday's first-look Q3 GDP and September PCE.
