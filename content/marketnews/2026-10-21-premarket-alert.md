*Super Micro just told the market its quarter came in roughly $4 billion above its own guidance, and the stock is up 12% before the bell. That alone trips the alert. But the bigger story is one level up: SpaceX printed a quarter that beat on every line, Elon Musk said its boards have formed committees to discuss merging with Tesla, and the whole AI-infrastructure complex is bid into Tesla's own report tonight.*

*Index futures are not doing much on net. Underneath, it is a tech-led tape with a French bond market quietly crossing a 24-year line and oil still grinding higher on Hormuz mines.*

## Overnight Developments

### [Supermicro Provides First Quarter of Fiscal Year 2027 Preliminary Business Update](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001375365/000137536526000025/smcifirstquarterfy2027busi.htm) — Super Micro Computer (SEC 8-K)

After Tuesday's close, Supermicro pre-announced its September quarter: revenue is estimated at **$18.5 billion to $19.5 billion versus guidance of $14.5 billion to $15.5 billion**, "primarily due to increased demand for AI platforms." Gross margin is estimated at **12.0% to 12.5% versus guidance of 10.4% to 10.8%**, on customer and product mix. The midpoint is about **27% above** the guided midpoint, and the margin guide moving *up* alongside volume is the part that separates this from a pure pass-through quarter.

Two caveats the release itself makes. The numbers are **unaudited and preliminary**, and the company says actual results "may differ materially." And this is a business update, not earnings; the full report and call are **Tuesday, November 3 at 5:00 p.m. ET**. SMCI is trading around **$36.41 pre-market, +12.1%** from a $32.47 close, which puts it back above where it was before last Friday's 10% drop.

Affected sectors: AI servers, Semiconductors, Data-center hardware

### [SpaceX up over 8% in premarket after earnings beat and Musk hints at Tesla tie-up](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/21/spacex-spcx-stock-musk-merger-tesla.html) — CNBC

SpaceX reported third-quarter revenue of **$14.6 billion, up 161%** year over year, against an LSEG consensus of $12.72 billion, with net income of **$4.4 billion** versus a $1.05 billion loss a year earlier. Its AI unit, now called "Super Intelligence," grew ninefold to **$7.56 billion** and is the company's largest business. First-quarter 2027 revenue guidance is **$33 billion to $37 billion against a $22.2 billion** average estimate.

Then the headline: Musk said on the call that he is considering a combination with Tesla and that both boards have created committees to discuss it. "I do think there's a lot of merit to combining the companies as we are pretty much in the superintelligence singularity," he said. SpaceX shares were **up 8.5%** pre-market per CNBC; Tesla was up 1.4%, and Rocket Lab, CoreWeave, Nebius and other space and neocloud names rose with them. Truist called the quarter "the first data point during the earnings season to support the bull case for AI cloud providers." **Tesla reports after the close today.**

One watchlist spillover worth naming. CNBC's [live blog of the call](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/20/spacex-spcx-earnings-live-updates-q3-2026html.html) notes Musk said SpaceX aims for "volume production" of AI chips at the planned Terafab factory in Texas during the **third quarter of 2028**, with Tesla and **Intel** as partners. That is the most likely reason INTC is up about 2.3% this morning; Barron's headline attributes the move to the Terafab comments, though that piece is paywalled and we could not read it.

Affected sectors: Tech, Semiconductors, AI infrastructure, Space, Autos

### [French 10-year bond yield hits 5% for first time since 2002 amid unrest](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/21/france-protests-bonds-debt.html) — CNBC

France's 10-year OAT yield **briefly crossed 5%** shortly before 5:30 a.m. ET, the first time since 2002, and was last around 4.98%, up about 6 basis points. The backdrop is a weekend of rioting and looting, with damages officials put in the hundreds of millions of euros, layered on top of a 2027 budget fight in which Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu is targeting **€54 billion in cuts** and Finance Minister Roland Lescure told RTL the government is willing to bypass parliament again via Article 49.3: "If we veer off course, we'll activate the guardrails."

It is not a US-market driver on its own, but the corporate read-through is already visible: [Carrefour shares fell 14%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/21/carrefour-french-protests-guidance-cut.html) after warning the unrest will hit its business. Separately, [UK inflation jumped to 3.5%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/21/inflation-uk-cpi-interest-rates.html), cementing rate-hike expectations there.

Affected sectors: European financials, Consumer, Sovereign debt

### [Oil prices climb as traders assess Middle East developments](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/21/oil-price-today-wti-brent-rise-on-middle-east-worries.html) — CNBC

Crude is higher for another session. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said Tuesday that naval mines had stopped three tankers using routes Tehran deemed unauthorized in the Strait of Hormuz, with "two violating oil tankers... set ablaze." Yemen's Houthis claimed Monday strikes on Saudi Aramco facilities at Yanbu, the Shaybah oilfield, and the Riyadh and Abha airports. Goldman Sachs puts the global average crude freight rate **above $20 a barrel versus about $3 a year ago**, with roughly $7 of that attributable to Hormuz war-risk premiums.

As of about 7:00 a.m. ET, **December WTI is $99.86, +2.1%** and **December Brent $110.74, +1.8%** against Tuesday's settle. Note the contract: November WTI expired yesterday, so today's front month is December and there is no roll distortion in that number. A social-media headline asking whether the US had struck Iran circulated overnight; no dated wire report confirms any such strike, and CNBC's oil coverage does not mention one.

Affected sectors: Energy, Airlines, Transports, Refiners
