## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Crude Spikes on Hormuz Impasse, Then Gives Most of It Back](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/09/28/oil-price-today-wti-brent-trump-iran.html) — CNBC

President Trump rejected Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend, and crude gapped higher overnight — November WTI was up **4.1%** and November Brent **4.0%** before the US open. The move faded through the session: November WTI settled at **$92.87 (+0.50%)** and November Brent at **$105.87 (+1.49%)**.

That round trip is the day's most important tell. The spike rested on a policy headline rather than a supply disruption, and it unwound almost as fast as it arrived — but not before it did real damage to the rates complex.

### Yields Hit a 19-Year High, and Gold Pays for It

The 10-year Treasury yield rose **6 basis points to 5.24%** — a level last seen in **June 2007**. The mechanism is straightforward: higher crude feeds inflation expectations, which feed Fed hike odds, which lift the long end.

Precious metals took the hit. Gold fell **-3.83% to $4,155.90**, its lowest close since **August 4**, and silver dropped **-4.76% to $61.19**. This was a rates-driven selloff, not a risk-appetite story — which is why it happened on a day when equities were also lower.

### Fed Hike Odds Firm for October

The Fed raised its target range to **3.75%–4.00%** on September 16, the first increase since 2023. Futures now price roughly **65–70%** odds of another 25bp hike at the October 27–28 meeting. With oil injecting fresh inflation risk, that probability is the single most important number in the market right now.

## Market Outlook

Equities fell across the board — S&P 500 **-0.77%**, NASDAQ **-0.92%** — but the index moves understate what actually happened underneath, where a 6bp rise in the 10-year to **5.24%**, its highest since June 2007, did the real work. The chain ran from a geopolitical headline to the rates complex: crude spiked 4% overnight on the Hormuz impasse, faded to roughly flat by the close, and left behind firmer inflation expectations and **65–70%** odds of an October Fed hike — enough to knock gold down **3.83%** and to punish anything long-duration, with NuScale **-6.1%**, SoFi **-3.92%**, and the broader nuclear and fintech complexes leading the decline. The one genuine bid, NVIDIA's **+1.68%** on a record **$150 billion** buyback increase, is itself a balance-sheet story rather than a growth story, which tells you what the market is willing to pay for right now. That a 4% oil spike could round-trip in a single session and still leave this much damage in rates suggests the market's inflation sensitivity warrants attention well beyond the Iran headline. Friday's **September payrolls** print is the next real test, with consensus at **90K** against a prior **84K** — a hot number would make the October hike close to a settled question.
