## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [July jobs report surprises to the downside — payrolls fall 23,000](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-august-7-nasdaq-dow-sp-500-july-jobs-report-surprises-100009572.html) — Yahoo Finance

The BLS reported the US economy **lost 23,000 jobs in July**, against consensus expectations of roughly +80,000. The unemployment rate nonetheless fell to **4.1% from 4.2%**, as labor force participation dropped to 61.4%, the lowest in over five years. The decline was concentrated in government payrolls (-53,000, partly seasonal) while private payrolls added about 30,000; June was revised down to +20,000 from +57,000. Equities read the miss as removing the risk of further tightening and rallied broadly.

### [Gold extends surge as rate-hike odds recede](https://finance.yahoo.com/personal-finance/investing/article/gold-prices-today-friday-august-7-2026-gold-prices-continue-to-rise-even-after-july-jobs-report-misses-122102132.html) — Yahoo Finance

Gold rose roughly **2.3% to about $4,400**, up around 4.8% on the week and back to levels last seen in mid-June. Coverage attributes the move to two forces: the weak jobs print reducing expectations of near-term Fed tightening, and continued Middle East instability keeping energy costs and inflation concerns elevated ahead of the September FOMC. Which of the two is dominant is genuinely unclear.

### [Canada discussing trade concessions with US for tariff relief](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/08/07/339377.htm) — Claims Journal (AP)

Ottawa and Washington have exchanged written bargaining positions on a deal trading Canadian concessions for relief from sectoral tariffs, with no agreement reached. The talks come ahead of previously announced 50% tariffs on a range of Canadian imports and broader Section 338 tariffs scheduled to take effect **August 19**.

### [Jamie Dimon warns market leverage is "pretty high"](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/06/jpmorgan-jamie-dimon-leverage-market-disruption.html) — Jamie Dimon, CNBC (2026-08-06)

Dimon flagged record margin debt and warned that a substantial amount of additional leverage is hidden, flowing through SPVs and securitizations rather than being classified as margin debt. He cautioned that this obscured leverage lets structural risk build unnoticed and that "somebody will disrupt the market," also citing deficits, rearmament and geopolitical tension as inflationary risks.

### [Chamath Palihapitiya says AI agents will undermine bottoms-up software strategy](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/08/60940290/chamath-palihapitiya-says-ai-agents-will-undermine-bottoms-up-software-strategy-amid-ip-alpha-leakage-concerns) — Chamath Palihapitiya, Benzinga (2026-08-05)

Palihapitiya argued that AI agents make the traditional bottoms-up enterprise software go-to-market motion obsolete, because agents can rapidly clone popular tools and erode their differentiation. He expects enterprises to lock down unauthorized AI tools over IP and "alpha leakage" concerns, calling bottoms-up GTM potentially "the worst strategic GTM decision of the past decade."

## Market Outlook

Equities took a weak July payrolls print — a 23,000 job loss against roughly 80,000 expected — as a reason to rally, with the NASDAQ up 1.30% and the Russell 2000 up 1.10% as traders priced out the risk of further tightening. The more telling move was in gold, which rose about 2.3% to roughly $4,400 while the dollar slipped to 99.61 and the VIX drifted down to 14.90 — a combination that suggests investors are hedging currency debasement and inflation rather than an equity drawdown. Beneath the index level the day belonged to nuclear and AI-adjacent small caps, where Oklo's first criticality at Groves lifted the entire SMR complex and Tempus, Rocket Lab and Super Micro all posted mid-to-high single-digit gains. That concentration of risk appetite in speculative pre-earnings names warrants attention, particularly with Hims & Hers, Rocket Lab and Super Micro all reporting within four sessions. Tuesday's July CPI release is the next real test — if the labor market is cooling while breakevens grind up toward 2.26%, the September FOMC becomes considerably harder to handicap.
