## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Oil falls after Iran's supreme leader discusses conditions to end the war](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/27/oil-today-iran-us-crude-wti-brent-trump.html) — CNBC

Brent fell **1.7%** to settle at **$113.88** and WTI **1.8%** to **$99.90**, back below $100. Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei met top security official Mohsen Rezaei to discuss the Islamic Republic's conditions for ending the war with the U.S. His post said the current state of "neither war nor peace" needs to end, while stressing "control" over the Strait of Hormuz. Saudi Arabia's defense minister held direct talks with Iran's president in Tehran on Monday. UBS's Giovanni Staunovo still sees Brent near **$110** through year-end, because global inventories have fallen about **500 million barrels** since February and strategic releases are largely over. A deal headline would hit oil first. The inventory hole would keep it from falling far.

### [Fed starts its two-day meeting; CNBC survey says hikes come in December, not this week](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/27/federal-reserve-rate-hike-timing-expectations.html) — CNBC

Of the 30 respondents to CNBC's Fed Survey, **93%** expect a pause on Wednesday and **76%** a hike in December. Thirty percent said the Fed should hike now, and respondents expect at least two dissents in favor of a hike. Janus Henderson's Richard Bernstein called the Fed's hesitancy "unfathomable" with nominal GDP running 6–8%. Moody's Mark Zandi argued the opposite: "This isn't the time for more Fed rate hikes." Respondents put recession odds at **26%**, down from 29%, and see CPI easing to 3.6% from 3.7%. CME FedWatch priced a [nearly **93%** chance of a hold](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/27/treasury-yields-federal-reserve-monetary-policy-meeting.html). The decision is at 2 p.m. ET Wednesday, and the midterms are a week later.

### [Strong data: confidence jumps to 91.7, durable goods +1.8%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/27/treasury-yields-federal-reserve-monetary-policy-meeting.html) — CNBC

The Conference Board's consumer confidence index rose **7.5 points to 91.7**, against an 81.0 consensus, as gasoline prices eased. September durable goods orders rose **1.8%**, slightly above the 1.7% forecast. New home sales came in at **676,000**, down 0.6% from August but above the 653,000 estimate. All three beat, which does nothing to soften Wednesday's message. The 10-year ended near **4.99%**, and the 2-year rose about 2 bp to 4.737% in the morning.

### [Nvidia and AMD commit $4 billion to Meta's equity raise](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/27/nvidia-invest-meta.html) — CNBC

Nvidia will invest **$3 billion** and AMD **$1 billion** in Meta's $95 billion offering. Meta will expand its Vera Rubin and Vera CPU deployments, and AMD said its Helios racks are running at Meta. Per [CNBC's Michelle Fox](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/27/ai-debt-hyperscaler-earnings.html), the absence of a Meta bond deal pulled its credit-default swap spreads back in, though they are still up **177%** this year (Amazon's are up 129%). FactSet consensus has Alphabet at **-$2.3 billion** of Q3 free cash flow on $55.2 billion of capex, and Amazon at **-$9.9 billion** on $58.7 billion. The circularity, chip vendors funding the customer that buys their chips, is the part credit markets will keep watching.

## Market Outlook

Tech extended Monday's Anthropic rebound: the Nasdaq rose 1.18%, the Russell 2000 1.16% and the VIX fell to 17.52, while Boeing's 9% drop held the Dow flat. The rally now rests on financing news, not earnings: $63 billion of bank and Blackstone money for Anthropic's Broadcom purchase, and $4 billion from Nvidia and AMD for Meta's raise. That eased credit fears, but it also binds chip vendors more tightly to their customers' balance sheets. BTIG's Krinsky notes the SOX's recent 5%-up, 5%-down swing has only four precedents in 30 years, clustered in 1995 and March 2000, which preceded drawdowns of 53% and 83%. Strong confidence and durables data, plus oil still above $110 Brent, give a hawkish Fed little reason to soften Wednesday's message even as WTI slipped under $100. The next 48 hours will show whether this is a turn or a squeeze: the Fed at 2 p.m. Wednesday, Alphabet and Microsoft after the close, then Amazon, Intel, GDP and PCE on Thursday, each a test of whether AI spending is earning its keep.
