## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Stock Market Today (Sept. 2, 2026): Dow edges higher as oil prices rise and bond yields climb](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/stock-market-today-sept-2-134032621.html) — Yahoo Finance

Equities recovered as the Treasury complex took a breather from the run to multiyear highs. The 10-year closed flat at **4.80%** after touching an intraday 4.81% — still at the top of its 52-week range, but the *rate of change* stopped, and that distinction did all the work today. Note what did not happen: the underlying macro stress didn't resolve, it just paused.

### [Markets see Warsh endorsing a rate hike in September — not everyone is convinced](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/31/markets-see-warsh-endorsing-a-rate-hike-in-september-not-everyone-is-convinced.html) — CNBC

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole framing — that a September hike is "finely balanced" — leaves the September 15–16 FOMC as close to a genuine coin flip as these things get, with markets pricing roughly 50% odds of a 25bp **increase**. Two data points stand between here and there: Friday's August employment report and the September 11 CPI print, which lands four days before the meeting opens.

### [Canada's counter-tariffs on $27.6B of US goods take effect September 8](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/08/list-of-products-from-the-united-states-subject-to-counter-tariffs-effective-september-8-2026.html) — Department of Finance Canada

Tariffs of 15–50% on more than 700 US goods — steel, dairy, appliances, farm equipment, electronics — begin next Tuesday in retaliation for the US 50% levies on Canadian imports. The timing matters more than the magnitude: a fresh supply-side price impulse arrives one week before a Fed meeting where the hawks already have the microphone.

## Market Outlook

Equities snapped a three-day losing streak with the Russell 2000 leading at +1.13% and VIX shedding 7% to 15.20 — but the catalyst was an absence rather than an event, namely a 10-year Treasury that finished flat at 4.80% instead of climbing again. That distinction matters, because none of the three pressures driving last week's selloff actually eased: crude is still above $90 on Hormuz supply risk, Canada's counter-tariffs on $27.6 billion of US goods land September 8, and Chair Warsh has left a September rate **hike** as a live coin flip. Gold reclaiming its entire prior-day loss while the dollar sat still suggests the haven bid never left — it was simply outmuscled by rates yesterday. The rotation into rate-sensitive names, with SoFi up 4.63% and the Russell outpacing the megacaps, warrants attention as a positioning tell rather than a conviction trade. Friday's August employment report is the first of two prints that will settle the September FOMC question, and a soft number is the shortest path to validating today's bounce.
