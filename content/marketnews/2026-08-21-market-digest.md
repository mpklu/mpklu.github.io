## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [US-Canada Trade Teams Meet for Third Straight Day as Tariff Deadline Looms](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-08-21/us-canadian-trade-teams-to-meet-again-as-tariffs-deadline-looms) — US News & World Report
Canadian and US negotiators met Friday for a third consecutive day in Washington, racing a Saturday deadline before new American tariffs take effect. Canadian Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc met with USTR Jamieson Greer. A deal would close roughly 18 months of trade friction; failure puts fresh tariffs on Canadian imports live over the weekend, making this a genuine gap-risk event for Monday's open.

## Market Outlook

Equities closed a losing week on a firmly risk-on note, with the Dow up 0.98%, the Russell 2000 outpacing the S&P, and the VIX surrendering 5.5% to 15.13 — but leadership came from crypto-linked and beaten-down speculative names rather than the megacaps, as Apple, Amazon, and NVIDIA all finished red. That rotation, combined with gold gaining nearly 2% against a flat dollar and the 10-year backing up 4bp to 4.74%, suggests the rally was more about covering and hedging than conviction about the growth outlook. Core PCE at 3.3% against a 3.63% funds rate — with three July FOMC dissents arguing for a *hike* — leaves little room for the dovish repricing that Tom Lee's 7,900–8,000 August target would require, and warrants caution toward positioning for a melt-up here. The immediate catalysts are stacked and unusually well-defined: the US-Canada tariff deadline hits Saturday with negotiators still at the table, NVIDIA reports Wednesday into visible pre-print de-risking, and Q2 GDP plus July Core PCE land the same Thursday morning. Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote as Fed Chair on August 28 closes the week, and with the market's implied path for September still unsettled, that speech is the most consequential scheduled event on the near-term horizon.
