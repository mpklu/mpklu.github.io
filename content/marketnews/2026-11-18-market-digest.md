## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Fed officials expect more rate hikes, worry about AI impact on inflation, minutes show](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/fed-minutes-october-2026.html) — CNBC
The October minutes said "almost all participants" judged further hikes would likely be appropriate. "Some" wanted a quarter-point hike at that meeting, and a couple raised the possibility of a larger move in December. Officials called AI-related demand "a material source of inflation risk." Staff also flagged "off-balance-sheet leasing commitments and circular financing arrangements among AI-related firms." The vote to hold at 3.75%–4% was 10–2, with Cook and Hammack dissenting in favor of a hike. Earlier in the day, Governor Christopher Waller [told CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/federal-reserve-governor-christopher-waller.html) a **half-point hike** is probably needed at the Dec. 8–9 meeting. "You might need to, like, shock people," he said. Futures already fully price a quarter-point hike in December.

### [Nvidia sinks after earnings, dragging down neoclouds CoreWeave, Iren](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/nvidia-stock-earnings.html) — CNBC
Nvidia was up almost 4% pre-market on its beat-and-raise, then closed **-2.9%**. It is now down more than 13% over the past week and finished 1.5% above its 52-week low. CoreWeave fell 4.1% and Iren 3.6%. Traders had also looked past a Wall Street Journal report that the [SEC is investigating Nvidia](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/stock-market-today-live-updates.html). Senate Banking Chair Tim Scott said Jensen Huang and Commerce Secretary Lutnick [have agreed to testify](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/jensen-huang-nvidia-tim-scott-congress.html) in December. The House Energy and Commerce Committee asked interim CEO Colette Kress for a briefing by Dec. 2.

### [Anthropic prices IPO at $83 per share, above expected range](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-ipo-ai-offering-price.html) — CNBC
A person familiar told CNBC the deal priced at **$83**, above the $70–$78 range. Trading starts Thursday on Nasdaq under the ticker **ANT**. Amazon and Google each hold stakes valued at more than $100 billion. Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and AMD agreed to invest a combined $26 billion in the offering. Anthropic reported $33.24 billion of revenue for the nine months to Sept. 30 and a run rate of about $81 billion at the end of October. Amazon (+3.8%) and Alphabet (+0.6%) rose as direct proxies. Cramer suggested some of Nvidia's selling may be [investors raising cash](https://www.cnbc.com/investingclub/2026/11/18/cramer-looks-at-what-turned-the-market-lower-where-stocks-could-go-next.html) for ANT allocations.

### [Why UnitedHealth and Other Medicare Advantage Insurers Are Falling](https://www.barrons.com/articles/unitedhealth-humana-cvs-stock-medicare-advantage-audits-1a7e683d) — Barron's
Managed care extended Tuesday's rout. By our bars, **UNH fell 10.2%**, **Humana 13.0%**, **Elevance 7.6%** and **CVS 5.2%**. Barron's points to two causes. The first is CMS's proposal to claw back Medicare Advantage overpayments as far back as payment year 2018. The second is Sen. Bernie Sanders' renewed push for Medicare for All. 24/7 Wall St. [said it found no single catalyst](https://247wallst.com/cards/unitedhealth-group-unh-enriched-price-move-01m7r3zqd506n3a21436zbyw8x) for the scale of UNH's drop.

### [Metals and oil slide; energy is the worst sector](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/stocks-moving-the-most-today-live-updates.html) — CNBC
Gold and silver fell to their lowest levels since September 2025 as December hike bets pulled money away from non-yielding assets. Newmont and Freeport-McMoRan fell more than 6%. Crude dropped on last week's rise in U.S. crude and fuel inventories, even as Iran said it had targeted U.S. sites in Kuwait. S&P 500 energy was the [worst sector](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/stock-market-today-live-updates.html), down nearly 3%. Housing kept sliding too. October pending home sales [hit a record low](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/two-new-housing-reports-point-to-bleak-winter-ahead.html) in the index's 25-year history, and building permits fell to their lowest since May 2020.

## Market Outlook

The S&P 500 slipped 0.2% for a fifth straight loss and stayed below its 200-day average. The Dow lost 1.1%, with UnitedHealth's 10% collapse doing much of the damage, while Anthropic proxies lifted the Nasdaq 0.4%. The more important signal came from the Fed. The minutes and Waller's call for a 50 bp hike to "shock people" pushed gold down 3.5% to a 52-week low and kept the dollar at its 52-week high. In that rate backdrop, even a $130 billion Nvidia guide couldn't hold a 4% pre-market gain. Sentiment looks fragile, not washed out. The market is selling good news in AI and policy risk in healthcare, and BTIG sees the 7,000–7,100 zone as the next stop if 7,250–7,350 support gives way. Thursday's ANT debut is the near-term test. A strong open could pull capital back into AI, while a weak one would show this tape has no appetite left even for the year's marquee listing.
