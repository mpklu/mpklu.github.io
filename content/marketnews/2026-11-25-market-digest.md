## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Core PCE runs hot, GDP revised up to 4.3%, and a December hike stays fully priced](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/pce-inflation-report-october-2026.html) — CNBC (Jeff Cox)
October headline PCE rose 0.3% m/m and **3.8% y/y**, in line with forecasts but up from 3.7% in September. Core PCE came in at **3.3%**, 0.1 point above the forecast. The Q3 GDP revision jumped to a **4.3%** annualized rate, against a Dow Jones consensus of 3.6%, and real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 5.8%. Personal income rose 0.7% (0.4% expected). Initial jobless claims fell to **189,000** (200,000 expected), near the lowest since the late 1960s. A Dec. 8–9 hike remains fully priced. Odds of a third hike in 2027 actually fell to about 1-in-3 from 50-50 on Tuesday, per FedWatch, because bonds [rallied on the risk-off move](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/treasury-yields-bonds-inflation.html). By the close, the 10-year (^TNX) was down about 6 bp.

### [Tech plunges, then recovers most of the drop](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/nvidia-ai-stock-tech-selloff.html) — CNBC (Samantha Subin)
In late morning, information technology was down about 5%, and consumer discretionary and communication services were each down more than 3%. Only staples and real estate were green. "We just feel that this is panic right now," Barclays' Venu Krishna said on CNBC. He added that "the market is expecting some sort of a macro shock." By the close, Nvidia was down about 4%, Micron 4.5% and Oracle more than 5%, and XLK finished down only about 1%. Ambarella fell more than 19% on a weak outlook. Among the [day's movers](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/stocks-moving-the-most-today-live-updates.html), HP Inc. rose about 11% midday after a beat and a dividend increase, Deere fell more than 4% on a soft fiscal 2027 outlook, and Dell slipped despite a large beat and a $132.3 billion AI backlog. Anthropic bounced more than 4% but stayed below its $83 IPO price.

### [U.S. proposes Section 301 tariffs on 14 trading partners](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/us-tariffs-trump-section-301.html) — CNBC (Lim Hui Jie, Kevin Breuninger)
The proposed "overcapacity" levies cover China, the EU, Japan, India and others. Most rates range from **15% to 20%**, and USTR Jamieson Greer said they are "not really additive" and act as "a top up" of a few percentage points. China is the exception: a new 20% rate stacks on the roughly 25% first-term duties, for **about 45%** in total. Greer said none of the tariffs are likely to take effect for "a couple months." FX markets [barely reacted](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/forex-dollar-currency-federal-reserve-euro.html). "This isn't a Liberation Day 2.0," CIBC's Sarah Ying said.

### [S&P puts Nvidia on CreditWatch Negative; Vance says Huang was never under investigation](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/vance-jensen-huang-nvidia-doj-investigation.html) — CNBC (Kif Leswing)
S&P Global [cited](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/stock-market-today-live-updates-.html) questions about "board independence and 'key man' risk." Vice President Vance said the DOJ told him Huang was "under no investigation." Trump had said Monday that an investigation had been done. Reuters reported that no formal probe took place, and that Attorney General Blanche relied on the investigations into Super Micro and Megaspeed. Separately, [three sources told CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/nvidia-china-ai-chip-remote-access.html) that an unusual volume of Nvidia GPUs, including thousands of B300s, has come up for resale since the Nov. 16 BIS rule restricting Chinese remote access. Huang's closed-door House interview is Tuesday, Dec. 1, and his Senate Banking appearance is Dec. 3.

### [Oil jumps after Houthis claim an attack on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/oil-wti-brent-hormuz-middle-east.html) — CNBC, via Reuters
Brent closed up **3.5%** at $94.61 and WTI settled up 2% at $83.68, after both had been down about 2% before the U.S. open. PVM's Tamas Varga said the Houthi statement triggered the rally, and that short-covering ahead of the holiday added to it. The EIA reported a 1.1 million-barrel crude build, about in line with the 1.5 million expected. OPEC+ meets **Nov. 29** to set 2027 output policy.

### [Asian chip stocks fall sharply overnight](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/stock-market-today-live-updates-.html) — CNBC
South Korea's Kospi fell **6.16%**. Samsung lost 7.31% and SK Hynix 6.99%. In Tokyo, Advantest fell 12.54%, Tokyo Electron more than 10% and SoftBank Group 9.55%. The Stoxx 600 tech index was down for a ninth session in ten. India's Nifty rose 1.57%, as it has gained from money leaving AI plays.

## Market Outlook

Wednesday packed a correction's worth of movement into one session. The S&P 500 fell 3.2% by 9:45 a.m. and the VIX hit 34.95, then the index recovered nearly all of it to close down just 0.25%, while the Dow and Russell finished higher. The morning drop had three causes: a firm core PCE (3.3%), a GDP revision that leaves the Fed little reason to stop hiking, and another tariff headline. The recovery suggests sellers are running out of stock to sell, at least in a thin holiday market. Eight watchlist names (GOOG, CRCL, MSTR, NNE, OKLO, SMR, SOFI, HIMS) set new 52-week lows intraday, and all but GOOG closed at least 8% off those lows. Treat that as a sign of a possible capitulation low, not a confirmed one. The split within AI is now wide: Meta rose 3.4% while Nvidia fell 3.9% under S&P's CreditWatch, the GPU-resale report and the unresolved questions about the DOJ. Bonds rallied on the risk-off move even as inflation runs hot, a combination that will be hard to sustain once a December hike is delivered. With Thursday closed and Friday a half-session, the next real test is Dec. 1: Huang's House interview and Nvidia's GTC, followed by the jobs report on Dec. 4 and the Fed on Dec. 9.
