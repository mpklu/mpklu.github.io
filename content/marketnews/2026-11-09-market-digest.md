## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Dow surges 1,100 points, Nasdaq climbs more than 2% as tech rebounds](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/08/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) — CNBC
Lower oil and a chip rebound did the work. AMD gained about 13% after Oppenheimer upgraded it to outperform with a $600 target, Broadcom rose nearly 9%, and Amazon (about 3%), Microsoft (almost 3%) and Tesla (5.5%) all gained. Not everyone is buying the bounce. Citi tactically downgraded "the US, Global Tech, and European Tech to Neutral (from Overweight)," wanting "cleaner positioning and more stable AI sentiment" first. Infrastructure Capital's Jay Hatfield called the chip sell-off "overdone." He still expects the S&P 500 to stay rangebound through year-end: "it's not like the coast is clear." AI "picks and shovels" led the rebound: Coherent rose 18% and Bloom Energy, Monolithic Power and Astera Labs each gained more than 13% midday, per CNBC's [movers list](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/stock-market-movers-live-updates.html).

### [Nvidia stock falls 4% as scrutiny turns to Jensen Huang's affair with alleged Chinese spy](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/nvidia-jensen-huang-chinese-spy-investigation.html) — CNBC
Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation after reports that U.S. intelligence agencies believe Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive, has been passing information to Beijing. In a staff email, Huang wrote: "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry." The board says it has "no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised." China's Foreign Ministry called the "so-called Chinese spy narrative" unsubstantiated, and Trump called Huang "an honorable guy." The contagion risk is what matters for the rest of the chip group. Panmure Liberum's Joachim Klement says the damage is contained for now, but if Huang's position is "endangered," Nvidia "might cut back on supporting other companies." Huang has [no clear second-in-command](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/jensen-huang-affair-nvidia-succession.html). Sen. Elizabeth Warren is pushing for legislation "to stop advanced AI chips from fueling China's military."

### [Rubio sees little hope of diplomatic breakthrough amid 'outrageous' Iranian demands](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/us-iran-war-trump-hormuz.html) — CNBC
Oil fell anyway. On Sunday Trump said further strikes were "highly unlikely" if talks to constrain Iran's nuclear program and reopen the Strait of Hormuz progress. Rubio later said "I wouldn't say we're on the verge of a breakthrough," and Tehran says it wants U.S. sanctions lifted and the war ended on all fronts before negotiations begin. **WTI settled down 4.38% at $86.85** and **Brent down 5.85% at $92.57**, per the [live blog](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/08/stock-market-today-live-updates.html). That extends last week's roughly 13% WTI drop. Airlines were the cleanest beneficiaries: American and United rose more than 6% and Delta 4.6%, per the [movers list](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/stock-market-movers-live-updates.html).

### [October CPI preview: Fed's Goolsbee sees "old-fashioned demand overheating"](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/october-cpi-report-2026-inflation.html) — CNBC
Tuesday's CPI is expected to show headline prices up **0.3%** and core up **0.2%** on the month (Dow Jones consensus), slower than September's 0.6% and 0.3%. There will be no official annual rate, because last year's government shutdown left a gap in the index. Bank of America's interpolated estimate is 3.8% headline and 2.7% core. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said Monday that the AI data-center build-out "is, to my eyes, starting to look more just like old-fashioned demand overheating." Markets price better than **80% odds of a December hike**, per CME FedWatch. BofA's Stephen Juneau: "Absent an indisputably soft 0.1% [month-over-month] print, we doubt the inflation data will dissuade the Fed from hiking in December."

### [CoreWeave confronts 'doom cycle' narrative as neocloud credit risk overshadows growth](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/coreweave-debt-credit-risk-neoclouds.html) — CNBC
CoreWeave reports Tuesday and Nebius Thursday, both into a credit market that has turned on them. Some CoreWeave 2031 bonds yield above 16%, and its five-year CDS spread has risen 50% in a month to above 1,240 bp. Voters rejected data centers in all but one of 41 related ballot measures last week. On the other side, Goldman Sachs CEO David Solomon [told CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/goldman-ceo-ai-financing-market.html) that "There's still very strong demand for investments in the AI infrastructure build." He called the market "still pretty constructive" for Anthropic's potential IPO, which Goldman is helping lead.

### [Elizabeth Warren set to take over the Senate Banking Committee](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/elizabeth-warren-senate-banking-agenda.html) — CNBC
According to an aide, Warren's agenda for January includes capping credit card interest rates, tightening export controls on AI chips to China, and regulating prediction markets. She has also told tech CEOs, Wall Street executives and "Trump family cryptocurrency businesses" to preserve their documents. That puts consumer lenders, chipmakers and crypto names all in scope.

## Market Outlook

The S&P 500's 2.35% rally, its best day since April, and a VIX back under 20 recovered most of last week's AI-driven losses, helped by a 4% slide in crude. Nvidia's 4.3% drop showed the rebound is selective. Leadership has shifted from Nvidia to AMD, Broadcom and the AI power names, and Amazon and Alphabet faded from their highs. After the bell, Hims & Hers swung as much as 10% lower on a margin-driven miss, and Rocket Lab slipped despite record revenue, so there is little tolerance for profit disappointments. The macro backdrop has not eased: Goolsbee is calling the data-center boom "old-fashioned demand overheating," and futures price better than 80% odds of a December hike. Tuesday's CPI decides the next leg. BofA thinks only a 0.1% print would take a December hike off the table, and CoreWeave's results the same evening will test whether the credit markets financing the AI build-out are steadying.
