## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Nvidia board is not considering bringing Jensen Huang back as CEO, source tells CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang.html) — CNBC
NVDA fell **4.1%** to its lowest close since July 1, 2025. It hit its session low after CNBC reported that the board is focused on an investigation into Huang, led by Wachtell Lipton, and is not in talks to bring him back. Earlier in the day, President Trump [said he was open to Huang returning](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/trump-lutnick-nvidia-national-security-jensen-huant.html) and that national security "was not put at risk." Over the weekend, Treasury Secretary Bessent had called for a "dramatic shakeup" and an outside CEO. Before Tuesday's report, Mizuho [cut its target to $220 from $250 and UBS to $240 from $300](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/15/stock-market-today-live-updates.html). Wolfe's Rob Ginsberg flagged **$150** as key support, with "the next major support level" around $100 if it breaks.

### [Nvidia fallout: Commerce targets loophole for China's data center use](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/nvidia-commerce-china-lutnick.html) — CNBC
Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule closing the loophole that let Chinese firms rent Nvidia computing power in data centers in third countries. It also now requires a license to ship advanced chips to **Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil**. BIS said the rule was "written by and at the direction of" Treasury and the National Security Council. It is the first policy response to the Huang scandal. Micron and Intel fell more than 7% and AMD 5%. This is the export-control risk HSBC flagged on Friday, and it hits TSM and SMCI as well as NVDA.

### [Dollar hits highest since April 2025 as Fed rate bets lend support](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/yen-steady-against-euro-and-dollar-after-soft-japanese-gdp-data.html) — CNBC
DXY closed at **103.61**, a fresh 52-week high, after last week's hot inflation data. CME FedWatch puts a **95.7%** probability on a quarter-point hike at the Dec. 8–9 FOMC and 4.3% on a half-point. Separately, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh [laid out an AI "disappoint" scenario](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/fed-ai-task-force-kevin-warsh.html). In it, "equity valuations could fall, leading to an economic slowdown and a more notable rise in unemployment." The [IMF's Kristalina Georgieva warned](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/imf-georgieva-warning-ai-circular-financing.html) that the world economy is "standing on one leg on the AI leg." The IMF estimates circular AI financing at a little over **$1 trillion**, two-thirds of it debt-funded. On the data side, the NY Fed's Empire State index fell 18 points to **7.8**, below the 16.7 consensus, though its employment gauge hit its highest since June 2022.

### [U.S. crude oil declines to $85 a barrel on recovering Middle East exports](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/us-crude-oil-declines-to-87-a-barrel-on-recovering-middle-east-exports.html) — CNBC
Oil rose early on reports of tankers striking mines in the Strait of Hormuz, then reversed. Both benchmarks finished down more than 3.5%. CNBC tied the drop to Hormuz shipments recovering and to the IEA's Friday forecast of a fourth-quarter supply surplus above **1.7 million barrels a day**. In a Sunday note, Goldman Sachs said it expects Brent to average $88 in 2027 and fall to $78 by December 2027. Energy was one of the day's worst S&P sectors, down about 2% in afternoon trading.

### [Bitcoin slides to $62,000 following liquidation cascade, then rebounds slightly](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/bitcoin-slides-to-62000-following-liquidation-cascade-then-rebounds-slightly.html) — CNBC
Bitcoin hit $62,000 late Saturday, its lowest since August, after breaking below its roughly $73,000 200-day average. By Monday evening it was back near **$65,000**. It is coming off a sixth straight weekly decline. Nansen's Nicolai Sondergaard said "a break below $60,000 is possible if Nvidia's results add to the tech selling." MSTR fell 7.7% and CRCL 7.3%.

### [China confirms Xi-Trump-Putin historic summit](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/trump-xi-putin-ukraine-nuclear-tariffs-russia-iran-.html) — CNBC
The three leaders meet in Shenzhen on **Thursday** on the sidelines of APEC, the first such three-way gathering. Ukraine is the stated focus. The same day, Anthropic is [scheduled to begin trading on Nasdaq as ANT](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/anthropic-ipo-date-price-range.html), after pricing 825 million shares at $70–$78 on Wednesday evening. That makes Thursday the week's second big test of risk appetite after Nvidia.

## Market Outlook

The AI unwind broadened on Monday. The Nasdaq fell 2.5% for a third straight loss, and the selling spread from chips into financials while the VIX jumped nearly 19% to 22.91. The day's policy news cut against chips: Commerce's third-country rule went after the China-access channel that underpins part of Nvidia's sales story, while the dollar at a 52-week high and a near-certain December hike gave investors no reason to stretch on valuation. The rotation is consistent across assets: staples at a record, Costco and Berkshire at highs, and bitcoin, gold and crude all lower. That reads as de-risking, not a hunt for new growth. Tuesday's Nvidia report is the hinge. A clean beat with the roughly 70% FY2028 growth framework intact could stabilize the group, while any China-related guidance cut would put the $150 support level, and the AI complex with it, under real pressure before Anthropic starts trading Thursday.
