## Overnight Developments

### [Dow futures extend the post-election rally while Nasdaq lags](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/04/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) — CNBC
Wednesday's Democratic sweep of both chambers sent the Dow up about 1,500 points, helped by an **~8% drop in crude** (WTI Dec settled at $88.12 vs. $96.09 Tuesday). This morning the rotation continues: banks lead Dow futures higher (Goldman, JPMorgan and American Express are up more than 1% each), while AI and neocloud names slip on fears that newly elected Democrats will tighten data-center rules. CoreWeave, Nebius and Iren are each down roughly 2.5–3.6% pre-market. Oil has stabilized, with WTI up about 1.8% to ~$89.70.
Affected sectors: Financials, AI infrastructure, Semiconductors, Energy

### [Qualcomm sinks on a weak forecast, AppLovin soars](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/04/qualcomm-qcom-earnings-report-q4-2026.html) — CNBC
Qualcomm beat estimates but guided low, saying the memory shortage is hurting smartphone sales. It is down about **10% pre-market** (~$156 vs. $173.19). AppLovin is up about **21%** (~$329 vs. $271.75) after beating estimates and raising guidance. Qualcomm is one of the weights on Nasdaq-100 futures this morning.
Affected sectors: Semiconductors, Ad tech

### Asian chipmakers hit hard on AI-curb worries
The data-center backlash fear hit Asia's chip names hardest. **SK Hynix fell 9.5%** and **Samsung Electronics fell 7.6%** in Seoul, and the **Kospi dropped about 5%**. [OpenAI pledged to pay the full cost of powering its data centers](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/openai-to-pay-for-data-center-power-after-election-shows-ai-concern.html) as part of a set of commitments published Thursday, in an explicit response to the election results.
Affected sectors: Semiconductors, AI infrastructure

### [Bank of England hikes to 4%, its first increase since 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/interest-rates-inflation-uk.html) — CNBC
The Bank of England raised rates 25bp to 4%. It is the last major central bank to start tightening since the Iran war began. Markets had priced the move at more than 99%, so this isn't a surprise. Traders see about a one-in-four chance of another hike in December.
Affected sectors: Rates, UK financials
