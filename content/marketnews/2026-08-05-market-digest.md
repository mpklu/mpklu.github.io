## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Gold surges to a record as Hormuz deal talks reprice the Fed](https://watcher.guru/news/gold-price-surges-4-50-today-adds-1-3t-to-market-cap) — Watcher.Guru

Gold jumped roughly **3.7%** to about **$4,307/oz**, its sharpest single-session move in months. The catalyst was diplomatic rather than defensive: the US, Iran, and Oman are reported to be nearing an interim deal to reopen the **Strait of Hormuz**, with President Trump suggesting an announcement could come as soon as today. Softer crude and cooler implied inflation shifted rate expectations — CME FedWatch pricing moved from **two further Fed hikes to one** by year-end. Lower expected policy rates, not fear, did the lifting here. Ten-year breakevens confirm the move, easing to **2.23%** from 2.27%.

### [Alphabet's AI leadership exodus](https://www.fxleaders.com/news/2026/08/05/alphabet-goog-stock-drops-to-350s-as-deepmind-leadership-exodus-spark-investor-concerns/) — FX Leaders

Alphabet fell **4.05%** after a sweeping reorganization of its AI division and the departure of Chief Scientist **Jeff Dean**, who leaves after ~15 years to found a startup, Discovery Loop, alongside Google veterans Oriol Vinyals, Quoc Le, and Sanjay Ghemawat. The exits land while the flagship Gemini release remains delayed past its expected June launch, sharpening concerns that Google is ceding ground to Anthropic and OpenAI. Alphabet alone accounted for the bulk of the Nasdaq's decline.

### [Fed held at 3.50%–3.75% with three hawkish dissents](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/29/business/live-news/federal-reserve-interest-rate-07-29-26) — CNN Business

At the July 29 meeting the FOMC voted 9–3 to hold. Dissents from Hammack, Kashkari, and Logan cited inflation running above the 2% target for five-plus years — a reminder that the near-term policy risk remains tilted toward tightening, not easing.

## Market Outlook

Today's tape split cleanly along a single fault line: the Dow set a record on defensive and industrial strength while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq snapped a four-day rally, with Alphabet's 4% slide on its AI leadership exodus doing most of the damage. The more consequential development was in metals — gold's 3.7% surge to roughly $4,307 was not a fear trade but a rates trade, as progress toward reopening the Strait of Hormuz softened crude and pushed FedWatch pricing from two further hikes to one, a shift corroborated by ten-year breakevens easing to 2.23%. That combination, easing inflation expectations alongside a VIX down 4% to 15.81, suggests the market is treating geopolitical de-escalation as disinflationary rather than risk-positive, which warrants attention because it makes equity leadership unusually dependent on individual earnings execution rather than a rising macro tide. The Alphabet reaction is instructive on that point: after a quarter of heavy AI capex commitments across the complex, investors are now pricing execution and talent risk, not just spend. Friday's nonfarm payrolls report — following a July private-sector print that slowed to 44,000 — is the week's decisive catalyst, and a soft number would test whether the one-hike repricing has further to run.
