## Overnight Developments

### [Nasdaq futures tumble as the AI sell-off returns, led by Nvidia and Anthropic](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/stock-market-today-live-updates-.html) — CNBC
The AI sell-off picked up again overnight. Nasdaq-100 futures are down nearly **3%**. CNBC reports Nvidia down about 5% and Anthropic down about 5% pre-market, which puts Anthropic further below its $83 IPO price. The tech ETF XLK is down more than 3.5%. The selling started in Asia: South Korea's Kospi **fell 6.2%**, giving back all of Monday's Huang-rally gain, and the Nikkei fell **2.8%**. Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid said a "risk-off tone" had returned to the market, though he added that the declines so far "don't have any of the characteristics that accompanied the outright bear markets of recent decades." Defensive sectors such as healthcare and consumer staples are holding up.
Affected sectors: Semiconductors, AI infrastructure, mega-cap tech, high-beta growth

### [AI debt worries grow as lenders get pickier](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/ai-debt-big-tech-spending-nvidia.html) — CNBC
Bond investors are questioning how the AI build-out will be paid for. According to Man Group, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Alphabet are on track to spend more on capital projects than their businesses generate in cash in both 2026 and 2027. L&G's Matthew Rees said BBB-rated names like Oracle are "trading more like single B." Man Group's Dan Taylor summed it up: "the era of spending first and asking questions later seems to be over."
Affected sectors: Hyperscalers, AI infrastructure, credit

### Tariff reports and today's inflation data
CNBC's live blog says traders are watching reports that the White House will announce new tariffs on more than a dozen major trading partners, including China and the EU. Nothing has been announced yet, and a [source told CNBC on Tuesday](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/11/24/new-u-s-tariffs-will-be-targeted-will-not-exceed-trade-deal-levels-source-says.html) the new tariffs will be targeted and will not exceed trade-deal levels. **October PCE** is due this morning, with consensus at +0.3% for the month and **3.8%** year over year. Weekly jobless claims come out a day early because markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving.
Affected sectors: Rate-sensitive growth, importers, retail
