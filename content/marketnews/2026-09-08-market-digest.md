## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Canada's retaliatory tariffs worth $27.6 billion take effect](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/08/canada-retaliatory-tariffs.html) — CNBC
Canada imposed counter-tariffs on **$27.6 billion** of American goods at 12:01 a.m. Tuesday, with duties ranging from **15% to 50%** across hundreds of categories. Steel, aluminum and iron products face the top 50% rate, alongside furniture, motorcycles, clothing and certain beauty products — more than 300 metals products and derivatives in all. Ottawa framed the action as a "dollar for dollar" response to US Section 338 tariffs, matching each US rate on the corresponding good. The move follows the collapse of negotiations last month, when Prime Minister Mark Carney suspended talks over terms he called "unfair, uneconomic." Industrials bore the brunt of the selling, and the Dow's 1.18% decline more than doubled the Nasdaq's.

### [Brent crude hits $98 after Iran's Houthi allies attack Saudi energy facilities](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/08/oil-prices-today-brent-wti-hormuz-iran-war.html) — CNBC
Iran-backed Houthi militants struck multiple Saudi energy facilities, wounding more than 70 people and prompting the Saudi energy ministry to halt operations at certain sites. Brent climbed to its **highest level in six weeks**, compounded by Iran's statement that an agreement with Oman to manage shipping through the **Strait of Hormuz** is nearing completion — raising concerns about Tehran's growing leverage over the waterway. The escalation follows US-Iran military exchanges over the weekend. Energy was the day's clear winner; the inflation implications are the harder problem.

### [Markets see Warsh endorsing a rate hike in September](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/31/markets-see-warsh-endorsing-a-rate-hike-in-september-not-everyone-is-convinced.html) — CNBC
Following Fed Chair **Kevin Warsh's** Jackson Hole keynote — in which he said underlying inflation trends have not "meaningfully improved" and the Fed has "work to do" — CME FedWatch odds of a **25bp hike at the September 15–16 FOMC meeting jumped to roughly 66%**, nearly double the 35% priced on August 27. A hiking Fed into a tariff-and-oil shock is a materially different regime than the one markets spent early 2026 positioning for.

### [Japanese yen strengthens on BoJ rate-hike outlook](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/japanese-yen-strengthens-against-us-dollar-on-boj-outlook-mufg-202609080623) — FXStreet/MUFG
USD/JPY fell to its **lowest level since February** as overnight index swaps moved to more than fully price a 25bp Bank of Japan hike at the September 17–18 meeting. Hawkish comments from BoJ board member Hajime Takata, who argued the bank should move "nimbly" rather than adhere to a fixed semiannual pace, accelerated the move. The yen has gained roughly 3% over the past month, and carry-trade unwind risk is back on the table.

### [Gold moves lower following fresh escalations with Iran](https://finance.yahoo.com/personal-finance/investing/article/gold-prices-today-tuesday-september-8-2026-gold-moves-lower-following-fresh-escalations-with-iran-111035525.html) — Yahoo Finance
Gold fell despite the geopolitical bid as **rising real yields overpowered a softer dollar**, extending last week's payroll-driven retreat. Bullion still holds a 4.1% month-over-month gain. A day where war headlines lift oil but sink gold is a day where rate risk, not haven demand, is setting the price.

## Market Outlook

US equities reopened from the long weekend with a broad risk-off session, the Dow's **-1.18%** slide leading the tape as Canada's **$27.6 billion** in retaliatory tariffs took effect and a Houthi strike on Saudi energy facilities pushed Brent to a six-week high near **$99**. The combination is awkwardly timed: an energy-driven inflation impulse arriving one week before an FOMC meeting where futures already assign roughly **66% odds to a rate hike**, and the market's response — gold down **1.61%** despite an active war premium, the 10-year up **3bp** to 4.81% — tells you real-rate risk, not haven demand, is setting prices right now. Beneath the index-level weakness, capital rotated into idiosyncratic stories rather than broad beta: Intel **+9.05%** on a reported 10% processor price hike, NuScale **+15.26%** and Nano Nuclear **+9.20%** on a sector bounce with no fresh catalyst, while crypto-levered names like Circle **-5.75%** and Strategy **-4.40%** tracked bitcoin below $79,000. A VIX that jumped **8.19%** to 15.72 while still sitting below 16 suggests hedging demand is rising from a genuinely complacent base, which warrants attention rather than alarm. Friday's August CPI print is now the single most consequential number of the week — it will either validate the hawkish repricing or hand Tom Lee's contrarian hold thesis a lifeline, and the tariff-plus-oil setup argues the risks to that print are skewed to the upside.
