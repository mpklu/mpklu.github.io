## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [U.S. stocks fall after Iran says Strait of Hormuz will remain shut](https://fortune.com/2026/08/11/u-s-stocks-fall-after-iran-says-strait-of-hormuz-will-remain-shut/) — Fortune

The second day of the same story, and the more consequential one. Iran reiterated
that the waterway stays closed until its conditions are met, removing the "deal is
imminent" premise that had capped crude on Friday. WTI added another 1.6% to $83.41
and Brent 1.6% to $89.15 — a two-session gain of roughly 6.7% in both. Energy,
utilities and industrials closed green; large-cap tech was the index's drag. A
chokepoint premium that persists into a CPI print is a materially different setup
than one the market expects to unwind within days.

### [Trump pauses the reciprocal tariff increase to 125% for another 90 days](https://taxfoundation.org/research/all/federal/trump-tariffs-trade-war/) — Tax Foundation Tariff Tracker

The scheduled escalation to 125% was deferred again, extending the arrangement
struck in June that holds China duties at a combined 30% — 20% "fentanyl" plus 10%
reciprocal. The 35% Canada tariff implemented August 1 is unaffected. Markets have
now watched this deadline slip repeatedly, which is precisely why the reaction was
muted: the pause is priced as the base case, and the tail risk is that one day it
isn't.

## Market Outlook

Equities slipped for a second session as Iran's confirmation that the Strait of
Hormuz stays shut lifted crude another 1.6% and pushed money out of megacap
technology into energy, utilities and small caps — the NASDAQ fell 0.60% while the
Russell 2000 gained 0.32% and the VIX actually declined. The composition matters more
than the direction: capital is rotating away from companies whose AI spending is
consuming free cash flow, as Alphabet's 3.6% drop on $205B capex guidance and
leadership turnover illustrates, and toward the ones already converting that spending
into revenue, as TSMC's 44.7% July growth and Super Micro's FY2027 guide of $65–72B
both demonstrate. With ten-year breakevens up four basis points to 2.29% and gold
adding 1.50%, the market is quietly rebuilding an inflation hedge rather than
dismissing the energy shock. That combination warrants attention into tomorrow's
8:30am July CPI release, which now lands with roughly a 6.7% two-session move in
crude sitting behind it and a Fed still 35 days from its next decision. Watch whether
the small-cap and energy leadership holds through the print — if it does, this is a
rotation with legs rather than a one-week reaction to a chokepoint.
