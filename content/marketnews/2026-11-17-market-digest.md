## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Nvidia beats on results and guidance in first earnings report since Huang's exit](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html) — CNBC
Nvidia reported adjusted EPS of **$2.68** against $2.48 expected and revenue of **$115.84 billion** against $109.10 billion (LSEG). Data center revenue was $107.6 billion, and non-GAAP gross margin was 74.9% against a 74% estimate. It guided this quarter to about **$130 billion**, above the $125.1 billion consensus, and said it spent $28.1 billion on buybacks. CNBC put the after-hours gain at about 3%, but it faded quickly: NVDA traded near $158.46 (+1.9%) at 16:40 ET. Earlier in the day, Jensen Huang said in his [first statement since resigning](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-jensen-huang-statement.html) that he "would be honored" to return as CEO if the board asked. Sources told CNBC on Monday that the board isn't considering that. The earnings call starts at 5 p.m. ET.

### [Widespread outages hit banks, air travel, the NSA and Apple](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/apple-microsoft-faa-jpmorgan-outages.html) — CNBC
The major averages fell to their lows after CNN reported that a classified NSA communications network was down. The NSA later blamed "scheduled routine maintenance." Apple confirmed a separate services outage and attributed it to "an internal update." It was the fourth major system failure in four business days, after Microsoft's Outlook outage, Friday's FAA ground stop and Monday's JPMorgan problems. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told CNBC: "This is not cyber attack, and this is not a coordinated effort by a nation state."

### [St. Louis Fed's Musalem says four more rate hikes may be needed](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/alberto-musalem-fed-interest-rates.html) — CNBC
In Musalem's words: "The economy will likely require several interest rate increases before the spring of 2027." His real-rate target implies hikes at each of the December, January, March and April meetings. He doesn't vote until 2028. Tuesday's data didn't give the doves much: October retail sales [rose 0.2%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/stock-market-today-live-updates.html), with the ex-autos figure above forecast, and import prices jumped 1.1% against a 0.7% estimate. Goldman raised its Q4 GDP tracking to 2.4%. Housing is where the rate pressure shows. The NAHB builder index [fell four points to 25](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/homebuilder-sentiment-plunges-to-14-year-low-leading-more-builders-to-drop-prices.html), its lowest since April 2012, with the 30-year mortgage at 7.51%.

### [Wall Street's fear gauge is surging again — and it's not just about AI jitters](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/wall-streets-fear-gauge-is-surging-again-and-this-time-its-not-just-about-ai-jitters.html) — CNBC
The SPY put/call volume ratio hit 1.5, its highest since the first week of April, and Cboe's three-month VIX briefly touched its highest since April. S&P 500 volatility rose faster than Nasdaq 100 volatility, which points to fear spreading beyond tech. Software led the losses: the IGV ETF fell more than 3%, and [Salesforce dropped nearly 10%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/stocks-moving-the-most-today-live-updates.html) as traders hedged ahead of Anthropic's listing. Anthropic is [likely to price above its $70–$78 range](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/anthropic-ipo-price.html), sources told CNBC. Books closed at 4 p.m. ET Tuesday, and trading starts Thursday as ANT.

## Market Outlook

The S&P 500 and Nasdaq fell for a fourth straight session. The S&P 500 lost 1.0% to its lowest close since July, the Dow dropped 1.6%, and the VIX climbed to 25.46, its fourth gain in a row. Unlike Monday, the selling didn't stay in chips. Software, managed care, crypto and turnaround names all fell, and an NSA network outage briefly pushed the Dow down more than 1,000 points. The macro backdrop gives investors little cushion. Musalem is arguing for several hikes by spring, import prices came in hot, and retail sales held up, so the rotation into defensives looks more like de-risking than a bet on a soft landing. Nvidia's beat and $130 billion guidance settled the near-term demand question. But the after-hours gain shrank from about 3% to 2% within minutes, which suggests the market still wants answers on leadership and China. Wednesday's FOMC minutes and Thursday's Anthropic debut are the next tests of whether the AI complex can find a floor above Nvidia's $150 support.
