## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Wholesale prices jumped 6.3% annually in September, biggest gain since 2022](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/15/september-producer-price-index-retail-sales-data.html) — CNBC

September's producer price index rose **1.3% for the month and 6.3% year over year**, the largest monthly gain since March 2022 and the biggest annual increase since December 2022. The monthly figure was more than double the 0.6% consensus. Energy costs drove the index, with the Bureau of Labor Statistics data showing signs of spillover into other categories — the central worry for a Fed that would prefer to look through a supply shock.

### [Consumer prices rose 3.7% annually in September as gas prices skyrocket](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/cpi-inflation-report-september-2026.html) — CNBC

Wednesday's CPI set up the PPI surprise. Headline CPI rose **0.6% on the month to 3.7% annually**, against Dow Jones estimates of 0.6% and 3.6%. Core CPI was tamer at **2.6%** annually after a 0.3% monthly rise. Gasoline jumped **9.1%** in the month and accounted for roughly half the headline gain; over the past year gas is up **34.2%** and fuel oil **74.2%**. Pressure was broad-based beyond energy, with shelter up 0.4%, used vehicles up 0.7% and tariff-sensitive apparel up 0.3%.

### [Economists: Hot PPI shows the Fed's work is not done](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/15/pce-ppi-september-fed-reserve-rate-hike.html) — CNBC

Fed officials responded to the energy-led inflation in September by approving **their first interest rate hike in more than three years**, a quarter point that took the funds rate to a **3.75%–4.00%** target range. Thursday's PPI feeds directly into the Fed's preferred gauge: Wall Street now estimates core PCE rose **0.32%** in September, up from 0.25% in August, which would lift the annual core rate to **3.1%** from 3.01%. Fed futures on Thursday priced only a **29% probability of a hike at the October meeting but a 91% probability of at least one hike by December**. "On the economic data alone, there is little reason for the Fed to hesitate," wrote Mizuho's Alex Pelle.

### [Why a $2 trillion deficit and 5% Treasury yields are becoming America's biggest economic challenge](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/15/treasury-yields-deficit-national-debt-ai.html) — CNBC

Long-term yields above 5% and a roughly **$2 trillion** federal deficit are pushing mortgage rates toward **7.5%**. CNBC traces the rise in the 10-year to the war with Iran, which began **February 28** and sent oil and diesel prices sharply higher, leaving bond traders demanding more compensation for the risk of further central bank tightening. Competition for capital is intensifying as companies issue hundreds of billions in new debt to build AI infrastructure, and with the November 3 midterms likely to lock in divided government, meaningful deficit reduction looks years away.

### [Big pharma stocks close near all-time highs as tech selloff pushes investors toward safer bets](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/big-pharma-stocks-near-all-time-highs-as-tech-selloff-pushes-investors-toward-safer-bets-0a5d5d07) — MarketWatch

The clearest expression of Thursday's rotation. Defensive healthcare caught the bid that AI names lost — visible in this watchlist, where Pfizer touched a fresh 52-week high on a day the chip complex sold off.

## Market Outlook

Thursday delivered a textbook rotation rather than a rally: the Dow rose 1.08% and the Russell 2000 1.38% while the Nasdaq managed just 0.08%, as a September PPI print of 1.3% monthly — more than double consensus — pushed capital out of long-duration AI stories and into pharma, consumer and small-cap names. The deeper signal is that the market no longer treats this inflation impulse as transitory. The Fed has already delivered its first hike in three years, futures price a 91% chance of another by December, and the 10-year holds above 5% against a $2 trillion deficit. The discount rate applied to speculative growth has structurally repriced. That is why TSMC could beat on every line, raise full-year growth to 44% and still close down 1.8%, and why NuScale, Oklo, Nano Nuclear and SoFi all printed fresh 52-week lows intraday before violent reversals — positioning, not fundamentals, is setting prices in the high-beta complex. The intraday recoveries across those names and Rocket Lab's halving of a 16% loss suggest buyers are engaging at washout levels, but one session of dip-buying does not undo an eight-session, 20% drawdown in a name like Intel. Attention now turns to the October 27–28 FOMC, where only a 29% hike probability is priced, and to September PCE later this month. Meta's October 21 report is the first real test of whether megacap AI spending still earns the market's benefit of the doubt.
