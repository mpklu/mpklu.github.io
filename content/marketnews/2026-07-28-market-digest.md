## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Oil Prices Plunge as US-Iran Tensions Ease](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/28/oil-price-today-wti-brent-us-iran-hormuz.html) — CNBC

Crude gave back its war premium in a single session, with WTI down 3.2% to $78.97 and Brent off 3.9% to $83.80. The catalyst: strikes on Iran were suspended to allow negotiations, and US and Omani intermediaries met to restore normal shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Roughly 20% of global oil flows through that chokepoint, so the de-escalation removes the single largest supply-risk bid in the energy complex. The relief is real but the agreement is fragile — this is a repricing of probability, not a resolution.

### [Fed Expected to Hold, But the Hike Tail Is Live](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/live/fed-meeting-live-federal-reserve-july-interest-rate-decision-141813444.html) — Yahoo Finance

The FOMC convened today for a two-day meeting with the decision landing Wednesday at 2:00 PM ET. Bond traders put roughly 68.5% odds on a hold at the current 3.50%–3.75% target and about 31.5% on a hike — an unusually fat tail for a meeting the consensus calls a non-event. The hawkish scenario runs through Iran-driven energy inflation, which today's oil collapse arguably just defused.

### [Section 122 Tariffs Expire, Replaced by Section 301 Levies](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/trump-administration/live-blog/trump-congress-2026-midterm-iran-ukraine-doj-dhs-ice-live-updates-rcna588796) — NBC News

The temporary 10% tariff lapsed on July 24 and has been re-enacted under Section 301 of the Trade Act. Mexico tariffs were pushed out 90 days effective July 31, and USMCA renewal talks with both Canada and Mexico remain stalled. The legal vehicle changed; the effective rate largely did not.

### [Rotation, Not Risk-Off](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/28/dow-jumps-520-points-while-nasdaq-struggles/) — Motley Fool

The Dow's 537-point advance came from economically sensitive names — industrials, banks, energy consumers — while the chip complex was liquidated. VIX fell 2.46% to 18.21, which tells you this was money moving between sectors rather than money leaving the market.

## Market Outlook

Today was a rotation, not a retreat: the Dow's 537-point gain against a red NASDAQ came as crude shed 3.2% on Iran de-escalation and money moved out of semis and pre-revenue energy into cyclicals, with VIX *falling* to 18.21 to confirm it. The dispersion beneath the flat surface is the real story — UPS beat, raised guidance, and fell 6.6%, while Oklo secured DOE approval and printed a new 52-week low, which tells you the market is currently unwilling to pay for either forward volume quality or long-dated build stories regardless of the news. Meanwhile Apple closed at a fresh 52-week high after touching a $5T cap, and Alphabet's 24% revenue growth still got sold on negative free cash flow — the tape is drawing a hard line between AI monetization and AI spending. With headline CPI ticking down and 10-year breakevens easing to 2.21%, the hawkish case for tomorrow's FOMC decision looks weaker than the 31.5% hike odds imply, which suggests caution about positioning for a surprise. The next 72 hours decide the second half: Meta and SoFi tomorrow, then Apple, Amazon, Strategy and Tempus on Thursday — four capex-heavy narratives that either validate Ives or vindicate Dimon.
