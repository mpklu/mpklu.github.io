## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Traders raise October hike odds as the 10-year touches 5.00%](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-september-18-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-080504071.html) — Yahoo Finance
The 10-year Treasury yield rose about 5bp to 5.00%, its highest of the week, as traders increased bets on a follow-up hike at the October meeting. This is the market walking back Thursday's interpretation of Wednesday's [25bp hike to 3.75%–4.00%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/fed-rate-decision-september-2026.html) as a one-and-done adjustment. Notably, the VIX fell another 4.1% to 14.81 on the same day — the rates market and the volatility market are telling different stories about the same Fed.

### Bank of Japan hikes to a 31-year high
The BoJ raised its policy rate to the highest level in 31 years, a move that supported global equities on the read that the tightening is a growth signal rather than a shock. The yen did not cooperate: USD/JPY still rose 0.55% to 156.80, a classic sell-the-news reaction that leaves the carry trade largely intact.

### [Crude eases as Middle East supply fears unwind](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/crude-oil) — Trading Economics
Brent has come off Tuesday's four-month high near $108.75 as the market prices in gradually increasing flows through the Strait of Hormuz and alternative export routes. Constraints are expected to persist into year-end, keeping regional production below pre-conflict averages until Q2 2027 — so this is fear draining out, not a supply glut arriving. Crude cooling is the single most helpful development for a Fed that just hiked on inflation.

## Notable Voices

### [It's not clear to me we've slayed inflation](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/jpmorgan-ceo-jamie-dimon-its-not-clear-to-me-weve-slayed-inflation-210823303.html) — Jamie Dimon, JPMorgan Chase, Yahoo Finance (2026-09-17)
Dimon responded to the Fed's first hike in three years by declining to call the inflation fight over, extending a warning he has repeated for years — he labelled inflation the "skunk at the party" in his April shareholder letter. Coming from the CEO of the largest US bank the day after the FOMC, it lands as a vote for the hawkish read of the dot plot. He separately argued AI oversight ["should be light touch"](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/jamie-dimon-on-ai-oversight-it-should-be-light-touch-154920383.html) at the federal level, to avoid a patchwork of state rules.

### [This market has eerie parallels with 2018](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/cramer-market-eerie-parallels-2018-what-it-means-for-investors.html) — Jim Cramer, CNBC (2026-09-11)
Cramer's checklist — second year of a Trump presidency, a strong preceding rally, oil above $100, the 10-year at 5%, inflation above target — is a week old and describes today's tape precisely. In late 2018 that combination preceded a roughly 20% S&P decline from the September peak to Christmas Eve. His conclusion is not to sell everything but to trim winners and raise cash. The wildcard he flags is Fed leadership: Chair Kevin Warsh is viewed as less committed to the inflation fight than Powell was in 2018.

### [Short rates could top 7%](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-16/ai-investment-interest-rates-ark-cathie-wood-michael-every/107156362) — Cathie Wood, ARK Invest, ABC News (2026-09-16)
Wood argues AI is inherently deflationary through productivity gains, and that this produces "good deflation" alongside an economic boom rather than the demand-collapse kind. The counterintuitive corollary: she expects short rates to approximate nominal GDP growth, as they did during the industrial revolution, which could push them toward 7%. ARK's framework has nominal GDP running 6–8% on 5–7% productivity growth, 1% labor force growth, and inflation between -2% and +1%. It is the most aggressive case on this list for why high rates and rising equities can coexist.

### [The Buffett indicator is at a record](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/buffett-indicator-236-percent-record-high) — Berkshire Hathaway valuation signal (2026-09)
The US-stocks-to-GDP ratio has reached record territory, though published readings disagree meaningfully — sources this month cite figures between 232% and 244%. Buffett's own long-stated benchmark is that approaching 200% means investors are "playing with fire." Berkshire closed Q1 2026 with a record $397.4 billion in cash and short-term Treasuries, up from $373 billion at the end of 2025, and remained a net seller of equities under Greg Abel. Note this is the signal talking, not fresh commentary from Buffett himself.

## Market Outlook

The indices finished mixed and nearly unchanged — NASDAQ +0.39%, S&P 500 +0.17%, Dow -0.18% — but that calm surface concealed one of the sharpest rotations of the month, with the 10-year yield climbing 5bp to 5.00% as traders raised the odds of another hike in October. Beneath the flat tape, Thursday's speculative leaders handed back nearly everything: NuScale fell 8.5% after rising 8.9%, Nano Nuclear fell 7.3% after rising 8.5%, Oklo gave back 4.2% of an 11.3% gain, and Tempus AI dropped 3.1% after a 14.9% surge, while Bitcoin's 6.0% move to $81,010 pulled Strategy up 16.4% and Circle up 7.9%. Risk appetite did not leave the market today, it relocated — which is a more fragile condition than either broad buying or broad selling, and it is exactly what a market does when it cannot decide whether 3.75%–4.00% is a ceiling or a waypoint. The tension worth watching is that a 5.00% 10-year and a 14.81 VIX cannot both be right about the same Fed, and with Nike closing at a new 52-week low and crude easing on unwinding supply fears, the disinflation-versus-tightening argument is now being settled one sector at a time. Next week brings no CPI or PCE print, so the October 27–28 meeting will be priced mostly off jobless claims, flash PMIs, and durable goods — thin evidence for a decision this consequential.
