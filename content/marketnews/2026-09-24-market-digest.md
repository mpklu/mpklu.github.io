A flat tape that hides a lot of moving parts. The 10-year yield pushed to another post-2007 high and front-month Brent tacked on another 4%, yet the S&P and Nasdaq finished unchanged — a trade truce extension and a well-behaved jobless claims print absorbed the shock. Under the surface it was violently two-sided: Meta closed at a 52-week high, Tempus AI added 7%, and UPS lost nearly 4%.

## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [U.S.-China trade truce extended for two months as Xi begins state visit](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/24/us-china-trade-truce-bessent-trump-xi.html) — CNBC
President Trump welcomed Xi Jinping to the White House for the first Chinese state visit to Washington in more than a decade, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confirmed the two sides extended their tariff truce — previously set to lapse in November — through **January 10**. The arrangement keeps tariff rates capped and rare-earth flows open while Beijing works through remaining deliverables. This is the single best explanation for why equities shrugged off a 5.16% 10-year yield today: the tail risk investors had penciled in for November just got pushed into next year.

### [WTI breaks $100 and the rally has legs](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/Oil-Breaks-100and-This-Rally-Has-Legs.html) — OilPrice.com
Front-month November Brent settled **+4.31% at $107.52** and November WTI **+3.35% at $95.25**, with WTI trading as high as $106.73 intraday before fading. Crude flows through the Strait of Hormuz have collapsed to under **2 million bpd** from roughly 8–9 million before fighting resumed, and alternative export routes are drawing attacks. Two offsets kept the move from running further: Saudi Arabia is preparing to restart its East-West pipeline to bypass Hormuz entirely, and Trump described US–Iran envoy meetings as "very productive." Note the curve — November Brent expires September 30 and is now **$6.52 above December** ($101.00, +2.94%), so a meaningful slice of that 4.3% is an expiry squeeze rather than a clean read on physical supply.

### [Jobless claims fall to 197,000 as labor market stays resilient](https://www.dol.gov/ui/data.pdf) — U.S. Department of Labor
Initial claims for the week ending September 19 came in at **197,000**, below the 201,000 consensus and down from a revised 198,000. The four-week average fell to 202,250. New home sales separately jumped **6.4% to 684,000** annualized against a 626,000 forecast. Coming one day after the flash composite PMI hit 58.4, this is a third consecutive datapoint arguing the economy is accelerating — which is precisely why the long end keeps selling off.

### [Treasury yields extend post-2007 highs](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/US10Y) — CNBC
The 10-year finished at **5.162%**, up 4.8 basis points on the day and up from 4.96% as recently as Monday. The move is not a Fed-repricing story in isolation — it is growth plus energy-driven inflation expectations arriving at the same time, with 10-year breakevens ticking up to 2.35%. The Fed hiked to 3.75%–4.00% on September 16, its first increase since 2023, and the data since has done nothing to argue against a follow-up.

## Market Outlook

Today was a stress test the market passed on a technicality: the 10-year printed 5.16%, front-month Brent added 4.3%, and the S&P still finished dead flat because the US–China truce extension to January 10 removed a November cliff investors had already priced. The tell is underneath the index level — Meta closing at a 52-week high and Intel up 3.9% on AI-capex news while UPS fell 3.9% and pre-revenue nuclear names bled for a second session. That is not a market pricing recession; it is a market rotating hard from anything rate-sensitive or fuel-exposed into the handful of names with visible AI-driven cash flows, and it leaves the index looking far calmer than the dispersion underneath warrants. With claims at 197K, new home sales beating by 58K, and yesterday's composite PMI at 58.4, the data keeps building the case the October 27 FOMC has room to move again. Watch the **September 30 PCE print** and **October 2 payrolls** — if inflation firms alongside this growth, the long end has further to run, and the flat-index calm on display today will get harder to sustain.
