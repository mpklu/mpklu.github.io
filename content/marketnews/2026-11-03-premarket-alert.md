## Overnight Developments

### [U.S. strikes Iran's Qeshm Island as Vance hopes for 'negotiated settlement'](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/03/iran-us-vance-trump-strikes.html) — CNBC
The U.S. struck Iran's Qeshm Island on Monday, the largest island in the Persian Gulf and close to the Strait of Hormuz. A U.S. official called it "a defensive measure" after Iran fired surface-to-air missiles at U.S. assets. The WSJ, which first reported the strikes, says Washington has been running "limited strikes" for weeks and keeping them quiet on purpose. This came hours after Vice President Vance said he and Trump "feel really good" about a negotiated settlement. A senior Iranian official told Reuters that Tehran would respond "forcefully" and that the two sides remain far from a deal. [Oil reversed part of Monday's 7% selloff](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/03/oil-prices-climb-after-us-reportedly-strikes-irans-qeshm-island.html): Brent rose 1.75% to $106.60 and WTI rose 1.7% to $98.10.
Affected sectors: Energy, Airlines/Transports, Rates/inflation-sensitive

### [Apple shares slide after weak margin outlook; Palantir slumps 8% after earnings](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/03/apple-palantir-stock-earnings.html) — CNBC
Apple's fiscal Q4 revenue beat estimates. But iPad sales missed, and its margin forecast disappointed, with rising memory costs and the cost of rolling out Siri AI as the main pressures. This was John Ternus's first report as CEO. Apple is up 25% this year, which, as one analyst put it, leaves "little room for disappointment." Palantir beat Q3 estimates and topped $2 billion in revenue for the first time. Its stock is still down about 8% pre-market because growth in U.S. commercial bookings slowed.
Affected sectors: Mega-cap Tech, Software

### [Australia hikes rates to 18-year high to fight inflation](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/03/australia-hikes-rates-18-year-high-finflation.html) — CNBC
The RBA raised its cash rate 25 bp to 4.85%, the highest since November 2008 and its fifth hike this year. Economists expected the move, but the vote was split 6–3. The RBA now sees inflation staying above its target until late 2027, three quarters later than it previously forecast, and blames fuel prices tied to the Iran war. That adds to the global pressure for higher rates, after two Fed presidents said last week that U.S. rates will need to go up.
Affected sectors: Rates, Financials
