## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Anthropic closes first day at $97.17 per share in landmark moment for AI](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/anthropic-ipo-ant-live-updates.html) — CNBC
**ANT** opened at **$111.50**, up 34% from its $83 IPO price, after raising **$81.3 billion** in the largest IPO on record. It hit $115.10 and then sold off as low as $92.72 before closing at **$97.17**, up 17%. More than 846 million shares traded. That is well above the 522 million that changed hands on SpaceX's first day. The fully diluted market cap at the close was **$1.75 trillion**. Per the prospectus, Amazon owns 14.7% of Anthropic and Google 13.7%, and Nvidia bought about $10 billion of stock in a private placement after the offering. Interactive Brokers' Steve Sosnick [said the pre-market rally had faded before the open](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) and that "the dam seemed to break once the big event was behind us." The proxy trade reversed hard. Alphabet opened up 4.1% and closed down 2.9%. Amazon went from +1.8% at the open to -3.9% at the close.

### [AMD dives on short-seller report of alleged China exposure](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/amd-falls-on-short-seller-report-of-alleged-china-exposure.html) — CNBC
AMD closed down **7.72%** at $489.19 after Hunterbrook disclosed a short position. Hunterbrook alleges that a blacklisted Chinese server maker, IEIT Systems, kept receiving newer AMD firmware. AMD said the claims "misrepresent AMD's business practices and relationships." Separately, the House Foreign Affairs Committee released documents showing that Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security had [approved sales of more than 879,000 AI chips](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/nvidia-amd-china-chip-sales-congress.html) to Chinese companies as of May. Some of those buyers are designated "Chinese Military Companies." Rep. Mast called two firms on Nvidia's license applications "fake companies." Jensen Huang is due for a closed-door interview with three House panels on **Dec. 1**, and Nvidia's general counsel is scheduled for Dec. 2.

### [Trump, Xi, Putin said they plan to boost oil production, revamp UN after trilateral talks](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/trump-xi-putin-trilateral-meeting-china.html) — CNBC
The three leaders' identical statements from Shenzhen pledged to "increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products" and reaffirmed that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought." The statements gave few details. They did not mention Ukraine or the U.S. war with Iran. Crude rose anyway: January WTI gained about 1%.

### [Gold's bad year is getting worse](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/gold-prices-fall-fed-rates-dollar.html) — CNBC
Gold peaked at $5,589.38 on Jan. 28. It broke below $3,800 on Wednesday for the first time since September 2025. "Gold can behave as a commodity or a currency," said Standard Chartered's Suki Cooper. Right now the currency side is driving it, which leaves gold exposed to rising yields and a strong dollar. December gold fell another 2.4% today to **$3,653.30**, a fresh 52-week low. The dollar index closed at a 52-week high.

### [Walmart hikes outlook](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/walmart-wmt-q3-2026-earnings.html) — CNBC
Walmart narrowly beat estimates. It raised full-year sales growth guidance to **4.9%–5.2%** and adjusted EPS guidance to $2.84–$2.89. Global e-commerce grew 17%, Walmart U.S. comps ex-fuel rose 2.9%, and Sam's Club U.S. e-commerce was up 30%. CEO John Furner said diesel costs were higher than expected but were offset by tariff refunds. That is a useful read-across for Costco ahead of its Dec. 10 report.

## Market Outlook

The S&P 500 fell 2% for a sixth straight loss, and the Dow lost 1,101 points to close below 50,000 for the first time since June. The trigger was the year's marquee AI event. Indexes rolled over the moment Anthropic opened at $111.50, and the stock gave back 13% from there to close at $97.17. A record IPO that still closed 17% up was not enough to lift the broader market. Alphabet's 6.8% open-to-close reversal and new 52-week lows in Nvidia, Alphabet and SoFi suggest investors are using AI strength to sell rather than to buy. The rate backdrop also offers no cushion: the 10-year is above 5%, the dollar is at a 52-week high, and gold is at a 52-week low. The VIX at 28 shows rising fear but not capitulation. Next Wednesday's GDP and October PCE report is the last major data before the Dec. 8–9 FOMC, and Huang's Dec. 1 congressional interview warrants attention as the next test for AI sentiment.
