*After the worst week for the Nasdaq since June, the AI trade is catching a bid before the bell — and it is the chip names leading it back up. Europe is going the other way.*

---

## Overnight Developments

### [Investors buy the dip in the AI trade](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/18/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) — CNBC

Last week was ugly: the S&P 500 lost nearly 2%, its biggest weekly decline since late June, and the Nasdaq tumbled 4.5%. Friday alone took the Nasdaq Composite down **2.15%** while the Dow gave up just 0.34% — a selloff aimed squarely at AI infrastructure, not at the market as a whole. The proximate worry was financing cost, not demand: CoreWeave ended the week **25.21% lower** on debt concerns, and the chip complex had been red for eight straight sessions.

This morning that trade is being bought back. **Intel is up more than 4%** after JPMorgan upgraded it to neutral from underweight, CoreWeave is up 3%, and AMD and Micron are each up more than 2%.

Affected sectors: Tech, Semiconductors, AI Infrastructure

### [French unrest compounds debt crisis fears](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/19/france-protests-bond-yields-markets.html) — CNBC

Weekend protests turned violent — roughly 550,000 marchers, nearly 2,400 arrests, close to 1,000 police injured. The CAC 40 is off about 1.3%, the French 10-year yield is up more than 10bp, and the OAT–Bund spread has widened to its worst since the early-2010s debt crisis. The National Assembly votes Tuesday on the Lecornu government's €54 billion budget savings plan. This is the clearest explanation for why Europe is red while US futures are green.

Affected sectors: European Banks, Luxury, Rates, FX

### [China Q3 growth beats](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/19/chinas-economic-growth-quickens-to-4point6percent-as-exports-surge-stimulus-rolls-out.html) — CNBC

Third-quarter GDP came in at **4.5%**, ahead of the 4.4% consensus and up from Q2's three-year low of 4.3%, as exports surged to a record monthly trade surplus of **$133.5 billion**. Enough to steady Asia, not enough to lift Europe.

Affected sectors: Industrials, Materials, EM

### International Markets

Europe is clearly weak: **DAX -1.08%**, **FTSE 100 -0.92%**, with France leading the decline. Asia was mixed — **Nikkei -0.62%** as PM Takaichi pushed back on criticism of the weak yen, **Shanghai +0.39%** on the GDP beat, Kospi +1.76% and ASX +0.44%. **Hong Kong was closed** for the day following the Chung Yeung Festival.

Crude eased but by less than the screens suggest: WTI is near **$97.97** and Brent near **$108.22**. Front-month WTI appears to have dropped 3.4% overnight, but that is the December contract replacing November — on a like-for-like basis the move is closer to **-1.1%**. Brent's -1.5% is clean.

## What to Watch at Open

The tape is asking one question: is this a real bottom in the AI trade or an oversold bounce? Watch whether Intel's upgrade-driven pop broadens into the rest of the chip complex or fades by mid-morning — and watch the 10-year, because this selloff was about financing costs, and stabilizing yields are what the rebound is leaning on. BTIG is already telling clients to sell into any semiconductor strength.
