## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Fed hikes 25bp to 3.75%–4.00%, its first increase since 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/fed-rate-decision-september-2026.html) — CNBC

The FOMC met September 15–16 and voted 12–0 to raise the target range a quarter point to 3.75%–4.00%, with the new range taking effect today. The statement framed the move as supporting "a timelier return" to the 2% goal while describing economic activity as expanding at a solid pace with resilient domestic spending and robust capital investment. The accompanying [statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm) marks the first hike of a cycle that markets had spent most of the year assuming was over.

### [Warsh: inflation is still too high](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/fed-meeting-today-live-updates.html) — CNBC

The updated Summary of Economic Projections carried a hawkish tilt: 16 of 18 participants see another increase later this year, and the median year-end 2026 federal funds rate of 4.1% implies one more 25bp move. Equities sold off into Wednesday's close on that dot plot — the S&P fell to 7,551.81 and the VIX rose to 17.71. Today reversed all of it and more.

### Labor and manufacturing data undercut the hawkish read

This morning's releases did the work. Initial jobless claims came in at 196K against a 208K consensus, continuing claims at 1.73M versus 1.78M expected, and the Philadelphia Fed manufacturing index printed 37.8 against a 30.5 forecast. The combination — a labor market that isn't cracking and factory activity accelerating — let the market re-read the hike as a strong-economy adjustment rather than the opening move of a grinding tightening cycle. The 10-year yield fell about 6bp to 4.95%, snapping a multi-day climb.

## Market Outlook

Markets spent Wednesday afternoon selling a hawkish dot plot and Thursday morning deciding they'd overreacted: the S&P gapped up 1.1% at the open on soft jobless claims and a strong Philadelphia Fed print, then traded flat for the entire session while the VIX collapsed 12.8% to 15.44. The composition matters more than the magnitude — the NASDAQ nearly tripled the Dow's gain, and the leadership was in the highest-beta corners of the watchlist, with nuclear developers, AI server hardware, and a turnaround semiconductor all posting high-single to double-digit gains. That is a market treating 3.75%–4.00% as close to the ceiling rather than a waypoint, an interpretation the dot plot explicitly does not endorse. The tension worth watching is that breakeven inflation eased to 2.33% and crude has fallen three sessions running even as the Fed says inflation is too high — if that disinflationary evidence holds, the implied October hike gets harder to justify; if it doesn't, today's multiple expansion was borrowed against a pause that never arrives. The October 27–28 meeting is 40 days out, which leaves a full CPI and PCE cycle to settle the argument.
