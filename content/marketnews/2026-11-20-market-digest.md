## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Nvidia stock plunges 14%, wraps up longest weekly losing streak since 2008 financial crisis](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/nvidias-7-week-losing-streak-is-longest-since-2008-financial-crisis.html) — CNBC
Nvidia fell **14%** to $125.53, a new 52-week low, for its seventh straight daily loss and seventh straight losing week. Selling picked up after [Reuters reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/stocks-moving-the-most-today-live-updates.html) that the SEC is looking into possible accounting issues at the company. The stock is down 46% over the seven weeks, about $2.6 trillion in market value. At roughly **8.4 times forward earnings**, it is now valued below AMD at 29 times. That happened even though Tuesday's report beat estimates and raised fiscal 2028 guidance. Interim CEO Colette Kress projected 75% revenue growth next fiscal year. Separately, Senate Banking Chair Tim Scott's office said former CEO Jensen Huang will [testify publicly](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/jensen-huang-nvidia-senate-testimony.html); no date is set yet. That comes on top of Huang's closed-door House interview on **Dec. 1**.

### [Anthropic falls 8.5% in first full day of trading as tech stocks get shellacked](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/anthropic-stock-ai-amd-nvidia.html) — CNBC
**ANT** closed at **$88.91**, down 8.5%, and gave back most of Thursday's 17% debut gain. At the low it hit $83.05, just above the $83 IPO price. AMD also fell more than 13%, and Micron dropped almost 14%. The Anthropic IPO and Nvidia's earnings were both expected to revive the AI trade, and neither did. Treasury Partners' Richard Saperstein [called it](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) "an AI repricing, reflecting an environment of slowing data center buildouts, reduced funding sources for AI-related projects and a slower trajectory of technology-related earnings growth." Defensive stocks were the bid: Walmart, Costco, J&J and P&G all rose, and General Mills was up 5% midday. Midday, someone [paid $93 million for SMH puts](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/a-massive-bearish-trade-just-happened-in-the-chip-stocks.html) (20,000 contracts, $380 strike, expiring September 2027), the day's single largest options trade.

### [Trump vows to stop U.S. companies from building data centers abroad, threatens 'tremendous tax'](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/trump-data-centers-ai-companies-tax.html) — CNBC
At a White House press conference, Trump said U.S. companies would be barred from building data centers in other countries, or would face a "tremendous tax" if they did. He acknowledged that "I haven't even said that to my people." Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta and Oracle all already run overseas data centers. There was no policy text, so treat this as headline risk for now. At the same press conference, Trump said he had discussed Huang with Xi and would "love to see Jensen be given a second chance." Treasury Secretary Bessent had [told CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/treasury-secretary-scott-bessent.html) earlier that Huang did not come up. Bessent also said the H200 export threshold for China "continues as planned."

### [Fed's Cook warns against falling 'behind the curve'; consumer inflation expectations drop](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) — CNBC
Governor Lisa Cook said "waiting longer to implement rate increases risks getting behind the curve." She was one of two FOMC members who dissented against the October pause. Markets fully price a quarter-point **hike at the Dec. 8–9 meeting**, and CME FedWatch shows only a small chance of a half-point move, according to [CNBC's Jeff Cox](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/warsh-fed-forward-guidance-rate-hikes.html). Governor Waller has floated a half-point hike. On the data side, the final November University of Michigan sentiment index was **51.5**, in line with consensus. One-year inflation expectations fell a full point to **3.7%**, the lowest since March. Treasuries rallied on the risk-off move: the 10-year yield fell 14 bp to 4.91%.

### [Bitcoin falls below $60,000 for the first time since July](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) — CNBC
Bitcoin fell more than 4% with the broader market and is headed for a retest of its July low of $57,737. Ether dropped 6% to about $1,651, its lowest since July 2. Crypto stocks [fell with it](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/stocks-moving-the-most-today-live-updates.html): on the watchlist, Strategy lost 9.7% and Circle 8.4%. Gold did not act as a haven. December gold fell **4.5%** to about $3,520, extending Wednesday's break below $3,800.

## Market Outlook

The S&P 500 lost 3.7% and the Nasdaq 5.3% in a seventh straight decline. That capped the worst week for stocks since April 2025: the S&P fell 8.6%, the Nasdaq erased its 2026 gains, and the VIX closed at 35.89 after touching 40. The selling has moved from a valuation trim to a crisis of confidence in the AI buildout. Nvidia fell 14% on a reported SEC accounting probe even though it now trades near 8 times forward earnings. Anthropic closed just $6 above its IPO price on its second day. Seven watchlist names set new 52-week lows, while staples and UnitedHealth caught bids. The bond market is no longer adding to the pressure, with the 10-year down 14 bp to 4.91% and one-year inflation expectations at their lowest since March. But a Fed that is fully priced to hike on Dec. 9 is not riding to the rescue. BTIG notes that durable bottoms have usually needed more breadth damage and an Apple drawdown that hasn't happened yet. Next Wednesday's GDP and October PCE, in a holiday-thinned week, are the main tests of whether rate-hike fears can ease enough to steady equities.
