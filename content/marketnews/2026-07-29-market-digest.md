## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Fed Holds at 3.50%–3.75% With Three Hawkish Dissents](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/fed-rate-decision-july-2026.html) — CNBC
The FOMC concluded its July 28–29 meeting by holding the target range steady on a 9–3 vote, with the Cleveland, Minneapolis, and Dallas presidents dissenting in favor of a hike. Chair Kevin Warsh paired the hold with a sharply higher inflation projection, lifting the PCE forecast to 3.6% from 2.7%. The combination — no move, but a materially worse inflation outlook and visible internal pressure to tighten — was read as the Fed acknowledging it may be behind the curve. Equities sold off through the afternoon and the 10-year drifted higher to 4.62%.

### [Oil Rallies on Renewed US–Iran Tensions](https://www.chemanalyst.com/NewsAndDeals/NewsDetails/oil-prices-edge-up-as-supply-surge-offsets-geopolitical-risks-43188) — ChemAnalyst
WTI gained 2.5% to $84.76 and Brent 3.3% to $90.73 as US–Iran military tensions reignited and Strait of Hormuz shipping risk returned to the tape. Roughly 20% of seaborne oil transits the Strait, so even headline risk carries an outsized inflation premium — an unhelpful development on the same day the Fed raised its own inflation forecast.

### [Japan Earthquake Halts Chip Equipment Production](https://www.tradingkey.com/news/market-movers/262061775-market-movers-tsm-20260729) — TradingKey
A 7.1-magnitude quake in Japan on July 28 forced the evacuation of TSMC's Kyushu facility, and Tokyo Electron halted plant operations on July 29 for safety inspections. Renesas also suspended its Kumamoto operations. With Tokyo Electron a critical node in the global lithography and etch supply chain, the outage compounded an already-weak semiconductor tape. TSM fell 5.1%.

### [Trump Announces Tariffs on 60+ Trading Partners](https://dailycaller.com/2026/07/24/trump-relaunches-global-trade-war-new-tariffs-forced-labor-china-eu/) — Daily Caller
Temporary 10% duties were replaced with 10–12.5% rates covering more than 60 countries including China, Canada, Mexico, the EU, India, and the UK, effective late July. Estimated household cost is roughly $900 annually. The timing layers a fresh goods-price impulse onto the Fed's revised inflation path.

## Market Outlook

The Fed held at 3.50%–3.75% but paired the pause with a PCE projection revised up to 3.6% and three dissents pushing for a hike, and the market read that combination as an admission of being behind the curve — the Dow fell 2.19%, the VIX jumped 13.5% to 20.66, and the speculative complex was liquidated wholesale, with SMCI -10%, SOFI -9%, RKLB -8%, SMR -8%, and OKLO -7.8%. What makes this more than a rate tantrum is the corroborating price action elsewhere: gold +2.1%, Brent +3.3% on Strait of Hormuz risk, and a weaker dollar alongside slightly higher yields, which is a stagflationary signature rather than a growth scare, and it arrives just as a fresh 10–12.5% tariff schedule hits 60+ trading partners. Notably, the selling ignored fundamentals entirely — Oklo cleared a DOE safety milestone, Nano Nuclear won an AFWERX contract, and Rocket Lab booked its largest-ever $266M launch award, and all three fell 7–8% anyway, which is the signature of positioning unwinding rather than reassessment. The one clean divergence worth watching is that market breakevens sit at 2.20% while the Fed now projects 3.6% PCE; that gap cannot persist, and whichever side gives way sets the direction for every long-duration asset on this list. Tomorrow brings Apple, Amazon, MicroStrategy, and Tempus reports into a tape with no risk appetite left, and how a strong print gets received will say more about this market than the print itself.
