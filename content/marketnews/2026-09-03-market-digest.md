## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [NVIDIA to acquire Hugging Face for $12.9 billion](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-to-acquire-hugging-face/) — NVIDIA / TechCrunch

NVIDIA confirmed in a Thursday SEC filing that it will buy the open-source AI platform Hugging Face for $12,930,300,000, its second-largest acquisition on record after the $20 billion Groq asset purchase in December. Hugging Face hosts roughly three million models, one million applications and half a million datasets, serving over 18 million developers. Jensen Huang committed to keeping the platform open and framework-neutral. The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027 — a bid to own the developer layer of AI, not just the silicon underneath it.

### [Yen jumps to one-month high as intervention watch intensifies](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/03/yen-japan-intervention-boj.html) — CNBC

The yen strengthened roughly 2% Thursday, touching 156.15 per dollar at one point and reaching its strongest level since Aug. 3, reversing a month of gradual decline. Two forces drove it: rising bets on Bank of Japan rate hikes — BOJ board member Hajime Takata left the door open to an outsized move and back-to-back hikes — and continued vigilance over official intervention. Japan spent a record ¥15.4 trillion ($98 billion) supporting the currency between July 30 and Aug. 26. A firmer yen and softer dollar were part of what let US equities rally.

### [Iran launches retaliatory strikes on Jordan, Kuwait and Bahrain](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/1/us-military-says-launching-new-attacks-on-iran) — Al Jazeera

Following US strikes on southern Iran that killed 11, Iran fired roughly 25 ballistic missiles at Jordan and Kuwait plus two dozen drones at Bahrain, Kuwait and Erbil on Sept. 1–2 — the heaviest US–Iran exchange in over a month, amid an intensifying standoff over the Strait of Hormuz. Jordanian air defenses engaged 13 missiles and intercepted 10; there were no American casualties. Note the market's verdict: WTI rose only 0.76% and the VIX *fell* nearly 6%. Investors are treating this as a contained risk premium rather than a supply event — a positioning that would unwind quickly if Hormuz transit were actually disrupted.

### [Canada's counter-tariffs on 700+ US products take effect Sept. 8](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/08/list-of-products-from-the-united-states-subject-to-counter-tariffs-effective-september-8-2026.html) — Government of Canada

Canada will impose tariffs of 15%, 25% and 50% on more than 700 US-origin products next Tuesday, covering C$27.6 billion (~$19.9 billion) of goods to match new US duties dollar-for-dollar. The 50% band hits steel, aluminum, furniture and clothing; 25% covers cheese, appliances and some seafood; 15% covers electronics and tools. This is a dated, scheduled catalyst five sessions out — one of the few known trade-policy events on the near calendar.

## Market Outlook

Equities rallied broadly Thursday — the S&P 500's best day since Aug. 4 — but the driver was in the bond and currency markets, not the earnings tape: the 10-year eased to 4.76%, the dollar fell 0.43%, the yen jumped nearly 2%, and that combination relieved fears of a Federal Reserve *hike* at the Sept. 15–16 meeting. That inversion of the usual setup is the key thing to understand about this market: with fed funds at 3.63%, CPI still grinding higher, and a Fed chair focused on stubborn inflation, weak labor data has become bullish for stocks rather than bearish, which is why the VIX could fall 6% on a day featuring Iranian missile strikes on three US partners and Canadian counter-tariffs eight days out. Underneath the index gains, dispersion remains extreme — bitcoin proxies MSTR (+17.5%) and CRCL (+16.2%) surged while the nuclear complex sits 79–83% below its highs and TSM went nowhere on an AI-led day — which suggests the rally is narrower than the 1%+ headline implies and warrants attention rather than extrapolation. Tomorrow's August employment report at 8:30am ET is the immediate test, with consensus at just +53,000 payrolls after July's -23,000 and 103,000 in downward revisions; a number strong enough to revive hike expectations would undercut precisely the mechanism that powered Thursday's advance. Watch whether the jobs print and the Sept. 11 CPI release let the Fed hold on Sept. 16 — that sequence, more than any single earnings result, will set the tone into quarter-end.
