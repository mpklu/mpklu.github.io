## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Oil prices climb as doubts grow that the Strait of Hormuz will open soon](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/World-News/2026/08/10/Hormuz-Strait-fears-push-oil-price-higher/5881786361765/) — UPI

The day's dominant macro story. Talks to reopen the Strait of Hormuz stalled
after Iran hardened its terms — Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran is
not in direct talks with Washington and reiterated demands that the US lift its
naval blockade of Iranian ports and compensate for war damages. WTI settled
about 5% higher at $82.16 and Brent at $87.70. With a chokepoint premium now
rebuilt into crude, energy costs move back to the center of the inflation
conversation two days before CPI.

### [Senate passes bill granting broad tariff authority](https://fortune.com/2026/08/08/trump-tariffs-100-percent-russia-sanctions-bill-congress-import-taxes/) — Fortune (2026-08-08)

The Senate approved the Sanctioning Russia Act of 2026 in an 86–11 vote, with
House approval expected. The bill grants discretion to impose tariffs of up to
100% on trading partners, working around court rulings that blocked earlier
executive orders. A durable legislative basis for tariff policy is a different
risk profile than executive action that markets had learned to discount.

## Market Outlook

A 5% surge in crude on stalled Strait of Hormuz talks knocked the major indices
off Friday's record close, though the damage was superficial — the S&P 500 gave
up just 0.06% and sits 0.5% from its high. What matters more than the magnitude
is the composition: gold up 2.45%, the 10-year at 4.70%, the dollar firmer and
VIX up 3.76% is a geopolitical risk-premium trade, not a growth scare, and it
arrives 48 hours before a July CPI print that now has a fresh energy input to
contend with. Beneath the surface the market is drawing sharp lines — TSMC posted
record July revenue up 44.7% on AI demand and still closed lower, Intel fell 4%
for raising equity into strength, and the speculative nuclear complex was
repriced hard, with Oklo down 8.1% and NuScale down 6.5% as analysts cut targets
on widening losses and rising cash burn. That combination suggests investors are
no longer paying for AI adjacency alone; they are asking which balance sheets
actually convert the theme into cash. Wednesday's CPI and NVIDIA's August 26
print are the two catalysts that will decide whether this consolidation resolves
the way Tom Lee expects or validates Dimon's warning about leverage, and near-term
the Hormuz headline flow warrants closer attention than anything on the earnings
calendar.
