## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Divided Fed holds rates steady as three presidents dissent for a hike](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/fed-rate-decision-july-2026.html) — CNBC
The FOMC left the target range at **3.50%–3.75%** on July 29 in a **9–3 vote**, with Beth Hammack, Neel Kashkari and Lorie Logan all dissenting in favor of a *quarter-point increase* — the first time since September 2016 that three policymakers dissented in the same direction. Chair Kevin Warsh told reporters he "asked for a good family fight and I got one," pledging action if inflation demands it. The long end reacted immediately: the 30-year yield jumped 12bp to **5.21%**, its highest in 19 years. Markets that entered 2026 pricing cuts are now pricing the opposite tail. ([U.S. News](https://www.usnews.com/news/national-news/articles/2026-07-29/fed-holds-rates-steady-but-3-members-favored-a-rate-hike), [CNBC analysis](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/kevin-warsh-fed-treasury-yields-inflation-credibility-interest-rates.html))

### [Treasury yields keep climbing as Fed officials float hikes](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/31/treasury-yields-inflation-interest-rates-federal-reserve.html) — CNBC
The 10-year finished Friday at **4.745%, up 8.2bp**, extending the post-FOMC backup. Rising crude on Middle East supply risk is adding to the inflation-risk premium embedded in the long end. Equities rallying into higher long-end yields is an uncomfortable combination that usually resolves one way or the other.

### [Yen surges on suspected Japanese intervention; BOJ holds at 1%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/31/yen-weakens-after-intervention-led-surge-ahead-of-boj-policy-decision.html) — CNBC
USD/JPY collapsed from **163.30 to roughly 158.0**, a ~3.3% two-session yen surge, after what market sources describe as yen-buying intervention during New York hours Thursday — Japan's first such move in three months. The MOF has neither confirmed nor denied it. The yen had touched **163.99 a week earlier, its weakest since November 1986**. The BOJ then left policy at **1%** on Friday, the highest since September 1995, after June's 25bp hike. ([Reuters instant view](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/currencies/articles/instant-view-yen-jumps-against-142545189.html))

### [Oil whipsaws as Iran threatens response to fresh U.S. strikes](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/30/oil-prices-us-iran-war.html) — CNBC
The U.S. launched a heavy wave of strikes on Iran late Wednesday in retaliation for attempted attacks on U.S. forces; the IRGC has threatened escalation. Crude sold off Thursday on a Saudi proposal for a naval coalition to protect Strait of Hormuz shipping, then recovered Friday with **WTI +1.08% to $84.49** and **Brent +1.22% to $90.12**. This conflict has whipsawed energy markets since February.

### [Hyperscaler capex guidance reframes the AI trade](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-july-31-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-081227738.html) — Yahoo Finance / Reuters
Microsoft posted its largest single-day gain on record (**+15.5% Thursday**, to $451.10) on its quarterly print, while Apple fell 7.4% Friday. Across Amazon, Microsoft, Meta and Alphabet, 2026 capex guidance now sums to roughly **$720–745 billion**. The market is paying up for cloud revenue acceleration and, so far, tolerating the spend that produces it.

### [100% pharma tariff takes effect; Mexico increase delayed 90 days](https://www.wiley.law/trump-administration-tariff-tracker) — Wiley Tariff Tracker
A **100% Section 232 tariff on patented pharmaceutical products and APIs** took effect July 31 for listed companies, with a reduced 15% rate for the EU, Japan, Korea, Switzerland, Liechtenstein and the UK. Separately, the planned August 1 increase of Mexico's reciprocal tariff to 30% was delayed 90 days. Relevant to PFE ahead of Tuesday's report.

## Market Outlook

Friday was a mega-cap earnings tape and very little else: Amazon's 15.3% surge on 37% AWS growth and Alphabet's 6.9% gain carried the S&P 500 and NASDAQ higher while the Russell 2000 closed down 0.5% and Apple shed 7.4% on soft September-quarter guidance. That divergence is the story — the market is paying a premium for demonstrated AI revenue acceleration and punishing anyone who guides below it, with roughly $720–745B of combined 2026 hyperscaler capex now the price of admission. Underneath the rally, the macro backdrop turned less friendly: the 10-year rose 8.2bp to 4.745% and the 30-year hit a 19-year high of 5.21% after three Fed presidents dissented in favor of a *hike*, and the yen's ~3.3% intervention-driven surge is a reminder that currency stress can force positions to unwind faster than fundamentals change. Equities rallying into a rising long end warrants attention, because one of those two signals is usually wrong. The August 7 payrolls report is the next catalyst that can settle it — a hot print against a committee already carrying three hawkish dissents would make the September 15–16 FOMC considerably more interesting than the market is currently pricing.
