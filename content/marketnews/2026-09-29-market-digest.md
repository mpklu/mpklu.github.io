## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Consumer Confidence Falls to a 12-Year Low](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/29/consumer-optimism-slides-sharply-as-fears-escalate-over-rising-prices-and-jobs.html) — CNBC / The Conference Board

The Conference Board's Consumer Confidence Index dropped **6.7 points to 81.9**, its lowest reading since **2014**. Economists polled by Reuters had looked for **89.2**. The Present Situation Index fell 7.9 points to **109.3**; the Expectations Index fell 5.9 points to **63.6**, a third consecutive monthly decline.

Two details stand out. For the first time since the question was introduced four years ago, more respondents described their family's finances as "bad" than "good," and net views of current business conditions turned negative for the first time since September 2024. The Conference Board noted that write-in references to prices — **oil and gas in particular** — hit new highs.

[JOLTS job openings](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-tuesday-september-29-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-080526442.html) reinforced it: **7.097 million** in August against expectations of **7.228 million**.

### Weak Data, Higher Yields

Here is the day's real puzzle. Demand data came in soft, and the long end sold off anyway. The **30-year Treasury yield** closed at **5.59%**, up 3bp, after touching **5.62%** intraday — its highest since **2002**. The 10-year added 2bp to **5.26%**.

Soft consumer data would normally pull yields down. It didn't. And the consumer's stated grievance — fuel costs — was actively resolving today, with crude down almost 4%. When the long end rises through weak demand data *and* falling energy prices, the pressure is coming from term premium and supply, not from the growth outlook. That is a harder problem for equities than a simple inflation scare.

### [Crude Gives Back the Hormuz Premium](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/29/oil-prices-today-brent-wti-hormuz.html) — CNBC

November WTI fell **-3.83% to $89.05** as Middle East supply came back online. Flows through Hormuz reached a seven-day average of **13.2 million bpd** — about **77%** of the 17 million bpd that transited before the US–Iran war, per Kpler — and Saudi Arabia resumed East-West pipeline exports.

This completes the round trip flagged yesterday: Monday's 4% geopolitical spike faded intraday, and today the premium came out entirely.

### [Anthropic's IPO Prospectus Leaks](https://fortune.com/2026/09/29/anthropic-leaked-ipo-prospectus-losses-growth-ai-end-humanity/) — Fortune / Reuters

A leaked prospectus obtained by Reuters put 2025 revenue at **$4.59 billion**, up roughly **1,088%**, against a GAAP net loss of **$41.97 billion** (from $8.31 billion in 2024) and an operating loss near **$8.06 billion**. The filing discloses about **$518 billion** in future cloud and compute commitments, and notes that nearly **25% of revenue** came from two customers. Expected valuation tops **$2 trillion**.

The prospectus also warns that the technology could pose a ["catastrophic or existential risk to humanity"](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/29/tech/anthropic-ipo-details-leak) — unusual language for a risk-factors section.

It landed alongside news that [OpenAI shelved GPT-6.1 Astra](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/29/openai-scraps-release-of-latest-ai-model-over-safety-concerns), a model slated for an October debut, after it fell short in internal alignment testing. Safety chief Saachi Jain confirmed the decision to the *Wall Street Journal*.

Two frontier labs signaling caution on the same day is a genuine sentiment event. Notably, the AI hardware complex barely flinched — NVIDIA **-0.72%**, TSMC **+0.90%**. The jitters stayed in the narrative and out of the tape.

### [Tech bull Dan Ives flags 'shocker risk' in Anthropic's IPO leak](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/tech-bull-dan-ives-flags-shocker-risk-in-anthropics-ipo-leak-172922273.html) — Dan Ives, Yorkville Ives on Yahoo Finance (Sept 29)

Ives called the existential-risk disclosures "a shocker risk relative to what I believe," while treating them as somewhat alarmist and staying firmly bullish on the sector. His framing of the losses and commitments: "Anthropic is the heart and lungs of the AI revolution — and that goes for OpenAI as well," with the spending representing the cost of "building out a new economy over the next 10 years." Worth noting what that argument concedes. A Vegas Strip analogy is a land-grab thesis, not a cash-flow thesis — it defends $518 billion of commitments on the grounds that the position is worth more than the price, which is exactly the claim the tape is now testing.

## Market Outlook

The indices did almost nothing — S&P 500 **-0.17%**, NASDAQ **-0.09%** — while consumer confidence printed **81.9**, a 12-year low and a **7.3-point** miss against consensus, job openings came in at **7.097 million**, and the 30-year yield still climbed to **5.59%**, its highest since 2002. That combination is the day's signal: the long end rose *through* weak demand data and a near-4% decline in crude, which points at term premium and supply rather than at growth or energy inflation. Beneath the flat surface the rotation was sharp and specific — Meta **+3.24%** and Apple **-2.66%** on opposite sides of the same analyst note about Meta's Muse agent, Rocket Lab **-3.45%** with a soft aerospace tape, and Tempus **-2.94%** despite a fresh FDA clearance, the familiar pattern of long-duration equity failing to get paid for good news. The AI narrative absorbed two real shocks — Anthropic's leaked prospectus showing a **$41.97 billion** loss against **$4.59 billion** of revenue, and OpenAI shelving GPT-6.1 Astra on safety grounds — yet NVIDIA fell only **0.72%** and TSMC rose **0.90%**, suggesting the hardware complex is still trading on order books rather than on sentiment. With **Core PCE** tomorrow and **September payrolls** Friday against a **90K** consensus, the question that warrants the most attention is whether a genuinely weakening consumer can pull the long end down — because today it conspicuously failed to.
