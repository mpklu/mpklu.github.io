## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [US adds 162,000 jobs in August, blowing past the 53,000 consensus](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/04/jobs-report-august-2026.html) — CNBC / BLS

Nonfarm payrolls rose **162,000** in August against a consensus of roughly **53,000** — the strongest monthly gain since March — while the unemployment rate held at **4.1%**. June and July were revised up by a combined **55,000**. The gain was concentrated rather than broad: food service added 59,000 (against a 12,000 monthly average over the prior year) and local-government education added 42,000. Markets moved toward pricing a September *hike*, with reported odds around 60%, an unusual place for this cycle to be. The [BLS-confirmed figure is already in FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PAYEMS): total payrolls rose from 158,913k in July to 159,075k in August.

### [Chip tariff phase two: build in America or pay](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/326474/20260903/chip-tariff-phase-two-confirmed-build-america-pay-lutnick-announces.htm) — TechTimes

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick confirmed on Sept. 3 that chipmakers building fabrication capacity in the US will receive duty-free access, while those that do not face steep import duties. Lutnick specifically cited TSMC's $265 billion Arizona commitment as the policy working as designed. TSM rose 3.00% on the read-through. Note the asymmetry this creates — it is a subsidy to incumbents with US capacity and a tax on everyone else, and it lands with the September FOMC and CPI both inside the next seven sessions.

### [FTC and 22 states sue Amazon over a "secret ad surcharge scheme"](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/08/ftc-states-sue-amazon-over-secret-ad-surcharge-scheme) — Federal Trade Commission

The FTC and 22 state attorneys general filed suit in Seattle alleging Amazon secretly inflated prices in its search-advertising auctions, [extracting more than $20 billion from about 1.2 million advertisers since 2019](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/31/amazon-ftc-lawsuit-advertisers.html) — nearly half of them small and medium businesses. Amazon denies wrongdoing and says its auctions lower costs. AMZN closed down just 0.29%, which tells you the market is treating this as a multi-year penalty risk rather than a threat to the advertising business itself.

## Market Outlook

August payrolls came in at 162,000 against a 53,000 consensus, and equities responded with a shrug — the S&P 500 slipped 0.38% while small caps rose and semiconductors led, hardly the profile of a market bracing for tighter policy. The interesting tell is in rates rather than stocks: the 10-year moved two basis points and breakevens rose one, which suggests the repricing is confined to the near-term policy path rather than reflecting any change in the long-run inflation or growth view. Meanwhile the day's biggest single-name moves — Intel up 4.72%, Super Micro up 4.62% — both traced back to one Dell earnings report rather than anything macro, a reminder that AI infrastructure demand is still setting its own agenda independent of the rate debate. With reported hike odds near 60% into a Sept. 15–16 FOMC, the gap between what futures imply and what the long end is willing to confirm warrants attention. Friday's August CPI is now the entire ballgame — it is the last major input before the Fed decides, and it arrives after a holiday-shortened week that leaves little room to reposition.
