## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Oil slides as US–Iran talks point toward a Hormuz reopening](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/oil-prices-investors-hormuz) — Transport Topics

Crude snapped a two-day rally on reports that Washington and Tehran are weighing a phased deal that would reopen the Strait of Hormuz and lift the US blockade on Iranian ports. Front-month Brent fell 1.9% and WTI 2.0%, unwinding part of this week's risk premium. Renewed Houthi strikes on Saudi targets, including Yanbu and Taif, keep the downside capped — this is a de-escalation trade, not a resolution.

### [Consumer sentiment stays near record lows even as durable goods hold up](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/calendar) — Trading Economics

The final September University of Michigan sentiment reading came in at 48.1, up from the 47.6 preliminary but far below August's 51.7. Year-ahead inflation expectations jumped to 4.6% from 4.0%. Durable goods orders were flat at 0.0% versus a -0.4% consensus, and core capital goods orders (non-defense ex-air) rose 1.6% against a 0.5% forecast — business capex is holding while household mood deteriorates.

### [Fed's first hike since 2023 still setting the tone](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/fed-rate-decision-september-2026.html) — CNBC

Nine days on, the September 16 decision remains the dominant macro variable. The FOMC raised the target range 25bp to **3.75%–4.00%** in a unanimous vote under new Chair Kevin Warsh, and the dot plot showed 16 of 18 participants expecting at least one more increase this year. With 10-year yields at 5.18% and breakevens easing to 2.33%, the market is accepting the hiking path without repricing inflation expectations higher.

## Market Outlook

Equities closed a constructive week with the Dow up 0.93% and the VIX back to 14.87, as falling crude and signs of a phased US–Iran arrangement drained some of the geopolitical premium that has defined September. The internals were less uniform than the headline suggests: Goldman's warning that hyperscalers need roughly $1 trillion a year to earn a real return on AI capex knocked Meta down 3.3% a day after the same spending was celebrated, while NVIDIA and TSMC held firm — the market is starting to separate the companies selling AI infrastructure from those buying it. That divergence sits uneasily alongside a consumer reading of 48.1 with 4.6% year-ahead inflation expectations, a combination that gives the Warsh Fed little room to soften its stance before October 27. Next week's Core PCE on September 30 and the September payrolls print on October 2 will do more to set the tone than anything in this week's tape, and a Chicago PMI consensus of 46.9 against a 51.3 prior warrants attention as an early crack in the activity data.
