## Overnight Developments

### [Nasdaq futures rebound after the worst rout in 19 months](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) — CNBC
Thursday was the **worst session for all three major averages since April 2025**. The Nasdaq Composite fell **5.5%** and is now in correction (more than 10% below its high). The S&P 500 lost **4.4%** and the Dow fell more than 1,800 points (**3.4%**). All 11 S&P sectors closed lower, but AI build-out names led the decline: [Nvidia fell about 9% and Micron about 17%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/ai-data-center-midterms-selloff.html). The trigger is fear that the Democratic sweep of Congress and key governorships will slow data-center construction. Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa said Thursday evening that "we need to stop what we are doing with data centers." This morning dip buyers are back in chips: Nasdaq-100 futures are up about **1.7%**, while Dow futures are still down about **1%**.
Affected sectors: Semiconductors, AI infrastructure, Megacap tech

### [The selloff swept Asia: Nikkei and Taiex enter bear markets](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/tech-stocks-sk-hynix-samsung-softbank.html) — CNBC
Asia took Thursday's Wall Street rout on the chin. The **Nikkei 225 fell 5.96%** and the **Taiex fell 4.51%**, putting both in bear-market territory. The **Kospi dropped more than 6%** and is down **11.7% this week**. Individual chip names fell harder: SK Hynix -10.4%, SoftBank Group -10.8%, Kioxia -8.7%, Samsung -6.4% and TSMC -4.7% in Taipei. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index had dropped **12.2%** on Thursday. Goldman's Timothy Moe blamed position unwinding and stop-loss selling: "The market's tendency is always to shoot first and ask questions later."
Affected sectors: Semiconductors, Memory, AI hardware supply chain

### [October jobs report at 8:30 a.m. ET](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/october-jobs-report-preview.html) — CNBC
The next catalyst arrives before the open. The consensus is **+59,000 payrolls** with **unemployment steady at 4.2%**. A weak print would feed growth worries on top of the AI selloff. A hot one would revive rate-hike fears, and those fears were part of what was already pressuring the AI trade.
Affected sectors: Rates, Broad market

### [Iran risk keeps Brent near $100](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/us-iran-war-trump-hormuz-oil-midterms.html) — CNBC
Rapidan Energy's Bob McNally puts the odds of a resumption of US-Iran fighting at **80%** now that the midterms are over, and expects both crude benchmarks above $100. On the other side, Reuters reported Friday that Iran sent the US a nuclear proposal through Qatar. It is conditioned on "seven demands," including an end to the naval blockade, in exchange for free passage through Hormuz. Brent was last near **$98.65** (+0.4%) after touching almost $100 overnight, with WTI near **$91**.
Affected sectors: Energy, Airlines, Defense
