## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Google Strikes ~$4.3B Nuclear Deal with Constellation, Igniting the Reactor Complex](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/06/nuclear-stocks-rally-as-googles-reactor-deal-lifts-the-sector-nano-nuclear-energy-jumps-8-oklo-climbs-8-nuscale-power-gains-7/) — 24/7 Wall St. / Bloomberg

The day's cleanest trade. Google signed a **20-year power purchase agreement with Constellation Energy** to bring roughly **890 megawatts** of nuclear capacity online, with a reported value near **$4.3 billion**, paired with a separate 15-year supply arrangement for another **2.7 gigawatts**. Critically, the new capacity comes from **efficiency upgrades at six existing reactors** in Illinois, New Jersey and Pennsylvania — modernized turbines, steam generators and digital controls — not new construction. Getting more output from units already licensed and running is cheaper and dramatically faster than building, and data centers need the electrons now.

The read-through lifted the entire complex on sentiment alone: **OKLO +7.17%**, **NNE +6.32%**, **SMR +4.43%** on our watchlist, with Constellation itself up double digits and the Global X Uranium ETF up around 5%.

Worth being precise about what this does *not* change. Google contracted with an operator of existing reactors, not with any small-modular-reactor developer. As 24/7 Wall St. put it, the agreement "leaves their licensing and construction schedules where they were." OKLO, NuScale and NANO Nuclear are still pre-revenue, still years from delivering power, and still down sharply from their highs — **OKLO remains about 80% below its 52-week high of $193.84**. This was a repricing of the *demand* thesis, not of any individual company's timeline.

### [US Trade Deficit Widens to $105.6 Billion, Largest Since March 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/06/trade-deficit-hits-105point6-billion-widest-since-just-before-trump-tariffs-enacted-last-year.html) — CNBC / BEA

August's goods-and-services deficit jumped **$12.7 billion to $105.6 billion**, well past the **$102.0 billion** consensus and above July's revised **$92.8 billion**. The driver was entirely the import side: imports surged **$17.2 billion to an all-time high of $420.8 billion**, against exports of $315.2 billion. Industrial supplies rose $9.1 billion on crude oil and nonmonetary gold, and capital goods added $6.2 billion on semiconductors and industrial machinery.

The composition matters more than the headline. Record imports of semiconductors and capital equipment are what an AI-infrastructure build-out looks like in the trade data — this is demand pulling goods in, not weakness. Markets treated it that way and looked through the miss entirely.

## Market Outlook

US equities closed at record highs on the S&P 500 and NASDAQ, but the gains were narrow — the Russell 2000 fell 0.59% and sits 8% below its own peak, meaning a handful of mega-caps carried the tape while the median stock went nowhere. The clearest signal of the day was Google's ~$4.3 billion, 890-megawatt nuclear agreement with Constellation, which validated the hyperscaler power-demand thesis and lifted every reactor name on the board; the caution is that the deal went to uprating *existing* plants, so the pre-revenue SMR developers that rallied 4–7% gained sentiment without gaining a single day on their licensing timelines. Meanwhile a record $420.8 billion import month pushed the trade deficit to $105.6 billion — a figure driven by semiconductors and capital goods, which looks more like AI capex pulling goods inward than like underlying weakness. With gold near $4,190, Brent at $100 and the 10-year at 5.27%, the inflation side of the ledger still warrants caution despite a VIX at 15. Wednesday's FOMC minutes are the only macro event with the power to move the rate debate before the October 27–28 meeting, and TSMC's October 15 report — nine days out, with the stock 1% from its 52-week high — is the next real test of whether the AI trade can keep validating these multiples.
