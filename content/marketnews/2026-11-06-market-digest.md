Thursday was the worst day for all three major averages since April 2025. The Nasdaq fell **5.5%** into correction territory, the S&P 500 lost 4.4% and the Dow dropped more than 1,800 points, as investors priced in a Democratic Congress and statehouses hostile to new data centers. Friday brought a partial rebound led by chips: the Nasdaq rose 1.09% and the Dow slipped 0.46%, per CNBC's [live market blog](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/stock-market-today-live-updates.html). The week still ended red, with the Nasdaq **-3.4%**, the S&P 500 -2.7% and the Dow -0.9%. XLK was down 8% on the week, its fifth straight weekly loss, and energy was the worst S&P sector at nearly -12% as crude collapsed. The VIX came off 9% but still closed at 25.64, versus 16.61 on Wednesday.

## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [Nasdaq rebounds 1% following worst session in 18 months](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) — CNBC
Chipmakers clawed back part of Thursday's rout. AMD rose **9%** after a 17.3% drop, Intel added almost 7% after losing 10.3%, and Nvidia and Marvell gained more than 4% after falling 9.3% and 16%. Bank of America's Wamsi Mohan argued the damage is to multiples, not earnings: "The recent sell-off in AI-exposed IT hardware has come from lower valuation multiples, not lower earnings estimates." Thursday's carnage had erased **$1 trillion** of Magnificent Seven market value, including $440 billion from Nvidia alone, per CNBC's [week-ahead preview](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/stock-market-next-week-outlook-for-nov-9-13-2026.html). Vanda Research called it the biggest day of retail selling since the pandemic. Freedom Capital's Jay Woods expects the selling "to continue over the next few days, maybe another week, before we hit a bottom."

### [Microsoft plans to use fewer neoclouds as AI buildout shows signs of strain](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/microsoft-moves-to-consolidate-its-relationships-with-neoclouds-in-ai.html) — CNBC
Microsoft is trimming its roster of neocloud partners, which includes CoreWeave, Nebius, IREN, Lambda and Nscale, to favor its own data centers, according to people familiar with the matter. Two sources said it also opted not to renew a deal with Oracle. The news capped Friday's rally. Credit was already flashing red: CoreWeave's 2032 bond, which carries a 9.625% coupon, now yields **above 16%**, and its five-year CDS spiked above 1,289 bp on Thursday, per [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/coreweave-bond-yield-tops-16percent-as-ai-credit-stress-rises.html). Jefferies' Christopher Wood: "The most plausible way the AI capex arms race will end is that the credit card will be taken away." On Thursday alone, Nvidia lost 9.3%, Micron 17.5% and Broadcom 10.4%, and Capital.com's Kyle Rodda said Mag 7 results were already "flashing warning signs that the pace of AI spending is slowing down," per [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/ai-data-center-midterms-selloff.html).

### [U.S. economy added 165,000 jobs in October, way more than expected](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/jobs-report-october-2026.html) — CNBC
Payrolls rose **165,000** against a Dow Jones estimate of 59,000, and unemployment slipped to **4.1%** from 4.2%. August and September were revised up by a combined 84,000. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% on the month and 3.1% on the year, both above forecast. Gains were broad: professional and business services added 70,000, healthcare 42,000, transportation 25,000 and manufacturing 19,000. The U-6 underemployment rate rose 0.3 point to 7.9% as the labor force grew by 421,000. The preliminary November University of Michigan sentiment index also beat, at **51.0** against a 47.5 consensus, and one-year inflation expectations eased to 4.3%, per the [live blog](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/stock-market-today-live-updates.html). Survey director Joanne Hsu noted "growing anxiety over a possible AI bubble."

### [Fed's Daly says more rate hikes are likely](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/fed-daly-interest-rates-inflation.html) — CNBC
San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly read the jobs beat as a reason to keep tightening. "My expectation is we're going to have to tighten policy further. Exactly when and how much is still to be decided," she told CNBC. Real wages are falling: pay is up 3.1% a year, against September CPI inflation of 3.7%. Daly said households are "on a treadmill of high inflation." The FOMC hiked to **3.75%–4%** in September and held last week, saying it wanted "the accumulation of more information." The 10-year yield ended up about 2 bp at 4.86%, and the dollar index gained 0.7%.

### [Oil posts steep weekly loss as investors weigh Iran risk after midterms](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/oil-price-today-brent-wti.html) — CNBC
WTI settled up 31 cents at **$90.83** and Brent added 3 cents to $98.32. The week was a rout, with WTI down about 12% and Brent about 16%, per the [live blog](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/stock-market-today-live-updates.html). The trade rests on a hope that a Democratic Congress, and Vance blaming the GOP's losses on the Iran war, push Trump toward a deal that restores Gulf supply. CNBC's [Jenni Reid](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/oil-prices-interest-rates-midterms.html) notes the stakes for rates: the Fed, BoE, ECB and BoJ have all hiked in recent months, partly on war-driven energy costs. A sustained oil retreat is the clearest path to the Fed stopping.

## Market Outlook

Friday's 1.09% Nasdaq bounce recovered only a fraction of Thursday's 5.5% plunge, and the week closed with the Nasdaq down 3.4% and the VIX still above 25. Two forces hit the AI trade at once: political risk from a Congress and statehouses hostile to data centers, and a rising cost of capital. CoreWeave's bonds yield above 16%, Meta is paying 7.5% on 30-year paper and Microsoft is pruning its neocloud partners. A 165,000 jobs beat that would normally be welcome instead strengthened the case for another Fed hike, so the market can't count on lower rates to cushion the de-rating. Tuesday's October CPI is the swing factor: a hot print alongside Daly's hawkishness would put a December hike firmly in play. After that, Nvidia's Nov. 17 report is the first real test of Ives' view that this is a capex timing problem rather than the end of the buildout.
