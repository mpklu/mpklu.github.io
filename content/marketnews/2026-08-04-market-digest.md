## Breaking News & Macro Events

### [S&P 500 back at a record high, Dow tops 54,000](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/04/investing/us-stock-market) — CNN Business
A broad rally lifted every major index, with the S&P 500 surpassing its early-June closing peak. Two forces converged: a strong earnings tape and traders pricing in progress toward reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Breadth was healthy — the Russell 2000's +1.85% kept pace with large caps, suggesting this was more than a mega-cap melt-up.

### [Palantir posts "otherworldly" quarter, stock surges ~29%](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/08/03/palantir-pltr-earnings-q2-2026.html) — CNBC
Palantir shares jumped 29.5% on the session after results CEO Alex Karp characterized as "otherworldly." The move was the single largest contributor to sentiment across the AI complex and helped drag adjacent infrastructure names sharply higher. Caterpillar — an industrial beneficiary of AI datacenter buildout — also rallied after topping $20 billion in quarterly revenue for the first time.

### [Crude unwinds war premium on Hormuz optimism](https://fxdailyreport.com/wti-crude-oil-price-analysis-for-august-4-2026/) — FX Daily Report
WTI fell 6.2% and Brent 5.8% as the market unwound the risk premium built up during the latest round of Middle East escalation. Worth noting the fragility here: Iran has publicly **denied** the "Hormuz deal" referenced by President Trump, so the move rests on a negotiation that is not finalized. Supply-side factors — expectations of rising output and moderating inventory draws — reinforced the decline.

## Market Outlook

The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,736.52 and the Dow cleared 54,000 for the first time, as a Palantir-led earnings surge collided with a 6.2% collapse in WTI crude on hopes of reopening the Strait of Hormuz. The combination is what makes today notable: cheaper energy, a six-basis-point drop in the 10-year to 4.63%, and small caps keeping pace with mega caps suggest the market repriced the inflation path and geopolitical risk simultaneously, rather than simply extending a narrow AI trade. That said, the AI complex still did the heavy lifting — INTC +10.8%, SMCI +10.7%, TSM +2.7% — and the VIX rising to 16.50 *alongside* record highs indicates investors are paying up for hedges rather than chasing with abandon. The soft spot in the setup is that Iran has denied the Hormuz deal underpinning the oil move, which suggests caution about treating today's energy repricing as durable. With seven watchlist names reporting inside the next eight sessions and July CPI landing August 12, the next two weeks will test whether this rally rests on earnings substance or on a geopolitical headline that has not yet been confirmed.
