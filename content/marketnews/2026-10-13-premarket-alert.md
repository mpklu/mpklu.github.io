## Overnight Developments

### [Micron, SoftBank and ASML slide as Wall Street chip rout spreads](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/13/sk-hynix-softbank-samsung-electronics-shares-tech-stocks-fall.html) — CNBC

A semiconductor sell-off that began on Wall Street Monday widened into Asia and Europe overnight. The trigger: China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) announced progress over the weekend on a new memory-chip architecture that packs more data into less space. CXMT is China's leading rival to Micron, SK Hynix and Samsung, and while analysts broadly still see it trailing the US and Korean leaders, the news landed on a market already nervous about AI financing and stretched valuations.

Per CNBC, SK Hynix plunged more than 10% and Samsung Electronics shed 7.4% — both to their lowest levels since August. SoftBank Group sank 8.5% and Tokyo Electron dropped 7.2%. In Europe, ASML fell 3.3% in early trade, with STMicroelectronics and Infineon both down about 5%. UBP's Vey-Sern Ling framed it as positioning rather than fundamentals: "Investors are jittery and looking for excuses to take profit on AI-related stocks that have outperformed this year, especially memory stocks."

Affected sectors: Semiconductors, AI infrastructure, Technology

### [UnitedHealth tops estimates and raises full-year outlook](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/13/unitedhealth-group-unh-earnings-q3-2026.html) — CNBC

UnitedHealth reported Q3 2026 revenue of **$112.7 billion**, adjusted earnings of **$4.69 per share** ($4.56 GAAP), and raised its full-year adjusted outlook to **at least $20.50 per share**. The medical cost ratio came in at **88.4%** against roughly 89.9% expected — the number that has punished insurers for two years. CFO Wayne DeVeydt cautioned that medical costs "continue to be elevated versus historical norms" and flagged the No Surprises Act dispute process as adding at least 100 basis points to total medical costs.

Affected sectors: Managed Care, Healthcare

### International Markets

Asia split sharply along sector lines. The **Nikkei fell 1.82%** to 67,777 in its first session back after Monday's Health-Sports Day holiday, dragged down by SoftBank and the chip equipment names. But **Hang Seng rose 0.30%** and **Shanghai gained 0.10%** — China's market took the CXMT news as good news for China. Europe shrugged it off at the index level: **FTSE 100 +0.62%**, **DAX +0.35%**, even as their chip constituents fell hard.
